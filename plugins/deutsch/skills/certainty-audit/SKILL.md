# The Certainty Audit

Derived from *The Fabric of Reality* by David Deutsch (Chapter 10 (The Nature of Mathematics)).

## Core Principle

Deutsch's argument in the chapter on mathematics is that a mathematical proof is a physical process, symbols being manipulated according to rules, whether on paper, in a computer, or in a brain, and its reliability depends entirely on unprovable assumptions about whether that physical process actually tracks the abstract truth it claims to represent. Euclidean geometry was treated as certain for two thousand years before physics showed that real space is not Euclidean. So mathematical intuition is a species of physical intuition, not a separate, higher category of truth, and the same downgrade applies to any claim that borrows the authority of formal rigor, a spreadsheet, a model, a proof, to end an argument rather than to advance it.

## Framework

### Step 1: Ask What the Proof Is Made Of

Identify the physical or symbolic process behind the claim, a spreadsheet, a formal derivation, a chain of citations, rather than treating it as a direct window onto truth.

### Step 2: Find the Assumption That Is Not Being Checked

Ask what has to be true about the world, stable inputs, correct data, a matching model, for this process to actually correspond to reality.

### Step 3: Test That Assumption Like Any Other Explanation

Ask whether the assumption is hard to vary and has survived real criticism, or whether it is simply being assumed because the layer built on top of it looks rigorous.

### Step 4: Downgrade Proven to Best Available

Treat the conclusion with the same tentative status as any other explanation, no matter how formal or numerical its derivation looked.

## Anti-patterns

- Letting formal rigor, a model, a spreadsheet, a proof, substitute for checking the assumption it depends on.
- Assuming a domain is exempt from ordinary fallibility because it uses numbers or formal logic.
- Treating your own confidence in a calculation as evidence that the calculation is correct.

## Output shape

The hidden assumption behind the proof named in one sentence, plus a verdict on whether that assumption has actually been tested or only inherited.
