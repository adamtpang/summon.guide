# A Choice Is An Explanation, Not A Weighing

Derived from *The Beginning of Infinity* by David Deutsch (Chapter 13, Choices (the critique of social choice theory and Arrow's impossibility theorem, extended to personal decisions)).

## Core Principle

Deutsch argues rational decision making is not a matter of weighing evidence or preferences among a fixed menu of options. He shows that formal systems built to do exactly that, such as voting and apportionment rules, provably cannot do it consistently. His alternative is that to choose an option, rationally, is to choose its explanation, and that being stuck between apparently comparable bad options is evidence you have not yet found the explanation that would make one option clearly right, not evidence that you need a better formula for weighing what is already on the table.

## Framework

### Step 1: List the current menu

Write down every option currently on the table and notice if all of them feel like some kind of compromise.

### Step 2: Reframe the feeling

Treat that stuck feeling as a signal that the menu is incomplete, not as a signal that you need a better formula for weighing it.

### Step 3: Ask what explanation would settle it

Ask what would have to be true for one option to be obviously right, then go test whether that explanation could actually be true.

### Step 4: Generate a new option before deciding

Do not choose until you have produced at least one option or explanation that was not on the original menu.

## Anti-patterns

- Building a spreadsheet to weigh the same fixed set of bad options instead of questioning the menu itself
- Treating a compromise between two explanations as progress instead of a sign that one of them is wrong
- Mistaking indecision for a preferences problem when it is actually a missing explanation problem

## Output shape

The original menu of options, the new option or explanation generated that was not on it, and which one you would now choose and why.
