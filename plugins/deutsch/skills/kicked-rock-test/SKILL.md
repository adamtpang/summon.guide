# The Kicked Rock Test

Derived from *The Fabric of Reality* by David Deutsch (Chapter 4 (Criteria for Reality)).

## Core Principle

Deutsch generalizes Dr Johnson's retort to Bishop Berkeley, kicking a rock to refute the claim that matter is not real, into a working test for what to treat as real. If the simplest explanation of something requires it to be complex and autonomous, capable of kicking back, it is real, no matter how abstract or unseen it is. When that test alone leaves you unsure, he adds a second move, taking the doubted claim fully seriously and following its own logic to the end, because a belief that cannot survive being taken seriously on its own terms, or that secretly has to smuggle in the very thing it denies, was never a live rival explanation in the first place.

## Framework

### Step 1: Write the Explanation Assuming It Is Real

State plainly how you would explain the pattern, motive, or claim if you treated it as a genuine, independent thing.

### Step 2: Write the Explanation Assuming It Is Fake

State what you would have to add, in improvised patches, to explain away the same evidence if you denied it.

### Step 3: Compare Complexity, Not Confidence

Whichever explanation needs fewer ad hoc patches to fit the facts is the one to provisionally believe, regardless of how sure either side sounds.

### Step 4: If Still in Doubt, Take It All the Way Seriously

Follow the doubted claim to its logical conclusion as if it were true, and check whether it collapses into contradiction or has to quietly borrow the thing it denies.

## Anti-patterns

- Dismissing a claim as unreal just because it cannot be directly touched or seen.
- Accepting a claim as real just because it feels emotionally compelling or comes from someone confident.
- Rejecting a belief from the outside without ever following its own logic to see where it actually breaks.
- Confusing I cannot prove it with the simpler explanation is that it is fake.

## Output shape

Two short explanations side by side, one assuming the claim is real and one assuming it is fake, a verdict on which is simpler, and if needed a short walk through of the doubted claim followed to its logical end.
