# The Problem-Solving Cycle

Derived from *The Fabric of Reality* by David Deutsch (Chapter 3 (Problem-solving)).

## Core Principle

Deutsch replaces the folk picture of knowledge growing by piling up observations with a five stage cycle that starts from a problem, not from data. A problem is simply a sign that your current explanation or plan is inadequate somewhere. From there you conjecture rival explanations, criticize them against what the problem itself demands they account for, tentatively adopt whichever survives best, and then treat that solution as the starting point of the next problem. Nothing in this cycle ever finishes. A good solution is not a resting place, it is where the next problem comes from.

## Framework

### Step 1: Name the Problem Precisely

State exactly where your current explanation or plan fails, what it does not account for, rather than a vague feeling that something is wrong.

### Step 2: Conjecture Rival Explanations

Generate at least two candidate theories or plans that could resolve the problem, including a modified version of the one you already have.

### Step 3: Criticize Each Candidate Against the Problem's Own Terms

Test every candidate against what the problem specifically requires it to explain, not against how confident it sounds or who proposed it.

### Step 4: Adopt the Survivor, Tentatively

Proceed with whichever candidate best survives criticism, and hold it as the best available for now, not as a final answer.

### Step 5: Let the Solution Generate the Next Problem

Treat the point where you stop as the starting point of a new cycle. Name what the new solution will probably fail to account for next.

## Anti-patterns

- Waiting to collect more data before acting, as though problems get solved by accumulating observations rather than by conjecture and criticism.
- Treating a plan that survived one round of criticism as proven rather than merely the best available for now.
- Scrapping an entire plan wholesale instead of locating the specific part that failed to survive criticism.
- Criticizing candidate explanations by vague gut feeling instead of by what the problem itself requires them to account for.

## Output shape

A one line problem statement, two or more rival conjectures, the specific criticism that eliminates each loser, and the surviving plan stated as tentative along with its likely next problem.
