# The Seminar Room Rule

Derived from *The Fabric of Reality* by David Deutsch (Chapter 13 (The Four Strands), the critique of Kuhn's paradigms).

## Core Principle

Against Thomas Kuhn's claim that scientific communities are held together by unexamined sociological conformity, Deutsch points to what actually happens in a research seminar. The most junior person present can challenge the most senior person's equation, and any reply that leans on authority instead of argument causes visible embarrassment in the room. The moment the group leaves for dinner, ordinary hierarchy reasserts itself completely. What makes real progress possible is not that scientists are unusually free of ego, it is that they have built a deliberately bounded zone where rank is not an allowed move, and everyone knows exactly when that zone opens and closes.

## Framework

### Step 1: Declare the Zone

Say explicitly when a discussion has entered criticize the idea mode, separate from normal work and social hierarchy.

### Step 2: Ban Rank as a Rebuttal

Inside that zone, experience, seniority, or title is not an acceptable answer to a specific objection. Only a better explanation is.

### Step 3: Credit the Objection by Its Content

Treat a correct criticism the same whether it came from the newest hire or the most senior person, and say so visibly.

### Step 4: Close the Zone Explicitly

End the discussion and let ordinary hierarchy resume, so no one is left unsure which mode is currently in effect.

## Anti-patterns

- Letting seniority substitute for a rebuttal when someone raises a specific, checkable objection.
- Punishing a junior person, even subtly, for a correct criticism of a senior person's idea.
- Running open criticism as a permanent, unbounded state so no one can tell when it is safe to speak up or when normal rank is back in force.

## Output shape

A named moment where the criticism zone opened and closed, and the specific objection that was allowed to stand regardless of who raised it.
