# Rational Versus Anti-Rational Memes

Derived from *The Beginning of Infinity* by David Deutsch (Chapter 15, The Evolution of Culture (rational memes, anti-rational memes, static society thinking)).

## Core Principle

Deutsch draws a precise line between two kinds of ideas that spread between minds. A rational meme relies on the holder's critical faculties to get itself replicated, so it tends to improve under scrutiny. An anti-rational meme relies on disabling the holder's critical faculties, through fear, taboo, deference, or punishment of dissent, to get itself replicated, so scrutiny is the thing it defends against rather than benefits from. His own markers for spotting the second kind are a complex behavior repeated without deviation, persistence after its stated justification has disappeared, and catching yourself explaining your own behavior with a bad explanation.

## Framework

### Step 1: Name the belief and its justification

Write down the belief or rule and the reason you were given for it.

### Step 2: Scrutinize it and watch what happens

Ask a real question about the belief and notice whether the justification gets better, or whether the pressure shifts to making you stop asking.

### Step 3: Check how it is enforced

Notice whether the rule is defended with a reason, or with an appeal to authority, fear, or tradition alone, such as because I said so.

### Step 4: Check whether it survives losing its reason

Ask whether the rule would still be enforced even if its stated justification turned out to be false. If it would, that persistence is the tell.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating fear, guilt, or social pressure not to ask as a sign the belief is important rather than a sign it cannot survive scrutiny
- Accepting because I said so or that is just tradition as a stopping point
- Assuming a belief must be rational just because you have held it a long time or it feels core to your identity

## Output shape

A short verdict on the belief under review: rational meme, naming what scrutiny would improve about it, or anti-rational meme, naming the mechanism disabling scrutiny, plus the one question you will now ask its defenders.
