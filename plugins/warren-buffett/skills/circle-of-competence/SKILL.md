# Circle of Competence

Derived from *Berkshire Hathaway Shareholder Letters, 1977-2024* by Warren E. Buffett (1996 letter, common stock investments section).

## Core Principle

The size of the circle matters less than knowing its boundary. A clean pass is a successful decision when the important economics cannot be evaluated reliably.

## Framework

### Step 1: Name the engine

Explain in plain language how value is created, captured, and converted into cash.

### Step 2: List decisive variables

Identify the few factors that determine the result and mark which ones can be estimated from evidence.

### Step 3: Separate knowledge from borrowed confidence

Label direct knowledge, verified facts, assumptions, and claims that depend on an expert or promoter.

### Step 4: Test predictability

Ask whether the relevant economics can be estimated across a useful time horizon without heroic assumptions.

### Step 5: Choose learn, partner, or pass

Define the cheapest evidence that could expand the circle, the trustworthy expert needed, or the reason to decline.

## Anti-patterns

- Mistaking familiarity with a product for understanding its economics.
- Expanding the circle because others appear confident or prices are moving.
- Treating uncertainty as a weakness that must be concealed.
- Giving personalized investment instructions when the proper result is an evidence plan or pass.

## Output shape

Return: Inside the circle; Outside the circle; Decisive unknowns; Cheapest learning tests; Learn, partner, or pass; Revisit condition.

*"The size of that circle is not very important; knowing its boundaries, however, is vital."* Warren E. Buffett.
