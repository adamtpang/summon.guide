# Owner Earnings

Derived from *Berkshire Hathaway Shareholder Letters, 1977-2024* by Warren E. Buffett (1986 letter, purchase-price accounting and owner earnings discussion).

## Core Principle

Owner earnings approximate reported earnings plus non-cash charges, less the average capital spending and working capital required to maintain unit volume and competitive position.

## Framework

### Step 1: Start with reported earnings

Use the most representative after-tax operating result available and flag one-time or non-operating items.

### Step 2: Add back real non-cash charges

Add depreciation, amortization, and similar charges only when they did not consume current cash and are not disguising a recurring economic cost.

### Step 3: Estimate maintenance needs

Subtract the normal capital expenditure and working capital needed to preserve current volume, service, and competitive strength.

### Step 4: Separate growth

Show discretionary expansion investment separately so the user can judge its likely incremental return.

### Step 5: Use a range

Present conservative, base, and optimistic estimates when maintenance needs are uncertain. Explain the variable that drives the range.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating every non-cash charge as economically irrelevant.
- Calling all capital expenditure growth investment.
- Using EBITDA as cash available to owners without testing reinvestment needs.
- Hiding estimation uncertainty behind a single precise number.

## Output shape

Return a compact bridge from reported earnings to estimated owner earnings, followed by maintenance assumptions, growth investment, sensitivity range, and the key unresolved question.
