# Build a Financial Fortress

Derived from *Berkshire Hathaway Shareholder Letters, 1977-2024* by Warren E. Buffett (1989, 2001, 2002, 2018, and 2023 letters).

## Core Principle

Liquidity and reputation are survival assets. Never accept a small chance of forced failure in exchange for an unnecessary increase in expected return.

## Framework

### Step 1: Map all cash calls

List operating needs, debt service, collateral, guarantees, commitments, and obligations hidden in contracts or subsidiaries.

### Step 2: Stress timing

Assume revenue falls, refinancing closes, asset prices drop, and several counterparties weaken at the same time.

### Step 3: Trace the chain

Follow exposures through counterparties and affiliates. A promise is only as strong as the party that must perform under stress.

### Step 4: Protect essential liquidity

Reserve enough cash and near-cash resources to meet obligations without relying on asset sales, new credit, or favorable markets.

### Step 5: Remove asymmetric ruin

Restructure, cap, collateralize, shorten, insure, or reject obligations whose downside can end the game.

### Step 6: Define triggers

Set early warning indicators and actions that preserve optionality before a covenant or cash deadline removes it.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming several correlated risks cannot arrive together.
- Treating access to refinancing as permanent liquidity.
- Optimizing idle cash while ignoring survival value.
- Accepting unlimited downside for a modest recurring benefit.

## Output shape

Return: Obligations map; Stress scenario; Counterparty chain; Minimum liquidity; Ruin paths to remove; Early triggers; Fortress verdict.

---

*"A small chance of distress or disgrace cannot, in our view, be offset by a large chance of extra returns."* Warren E. Buffett.
