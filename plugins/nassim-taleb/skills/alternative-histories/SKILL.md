# Judge the Decision, Not the Outcome

Derived from *Fooled by Randomness* by Nassim Nicholas Taleb (Chapter Two, A Bizarre Accounting Method (Russian Roulette, Alternative History)).

## Core Principle

A decision should be judged by the full probability distribution of outcomes it could have produced, not by the single realized one. Two people can earn the exact same amount of money while taking wildly different amounts of risk, and the accounting looks identical once the money is in the bank. Taleb's example is the tycoon who offers ten million dollars to play Russian roulette: five of six histories end in riches, one ends in an obituary, and the winner looks no different on paper from a dentist who earned the same sum slowly and safely. The skill is to price the unseen histories, not just the one you happen to be living in.

## Framework

### Step 1: Name the generator

Identify the actual mechanism that produced the outcome (a repeatable edge, a one time bet, a coin flip dressed up as a strategy) so you can ask what else that mechanism could plausibly have produced.

### Step 2: Count the unseen histories

Estimate how many other outcomes were realistically possible from the same decision, and how bad the worst of them would have been, not just how good the best one turned out to be.

### Step 3: Price the near misses

Ask what fraction of the alternative histories end in serious loss or ruin. If any meaningful fraction does, discount the win regardless of its size, because you were exposed to that fraction whether or not it happened to land.

### Step 4: Re-rate the process, not the receipt

Assign your judgment to the quality of the decision process that produced the bet, independent of whether this particular draw came up favorable. A good process with a bad outcome is still a good process; a reckless process with a good outcome is still reckless.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a single lucky outcome as proof of skill
- Judging a decision only by its dollar result
- Ignoring how many other, worse outcomes were possible for the same bet
- Letting the visible winner set the standard while everyone who took the same risk and lost disappears from view

## Output shape

A short written re-rating of the decision: the realized outcome, the plausible alternative range, and a revised confidence score for whether the process, not the result, was sound.
