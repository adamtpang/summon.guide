# The Green Lumber Test for Hiring and Trusting Experts

Derived from *Skin in the Game* by Nassim Nicholas Taleb (Chapter 9, Surgeons Should Not Look Like Surgeons (Looking the Part, The Green Lumber Fallacy, A BS Detection Heuristic)).

## Core Principle

The green lumber fallacy is named for a trader who made a fortune trading green lumber without knowing it meant freshly cut wood, not wood painted green, while the person who knew every detail went bankrupt. What it takes to succeed at a real, consequential activity is often not what sounds impressive when explained. Presentation skill, credential prestige, and the ability to give a clear, convincing account of what one does are a different skill from actually doing it well under real stakes, and the two are weakly correlated at best. The fix is to weight demonstrated track record under real, own money or own consequence conditions over polish, and to be suspicious of your own attraction to the candidate who explains things most smoothly.

## Framework

### Step 1: Ask for the track record before the pitch

Before listening to how a candidate explains their approach, get the actual, checkable record: results over time, under real conditions, with real consequences to them if they failed. Evaluate that first.

### Step 2: Notice when you are impressed by fluency, not results

If you catch yourself thinking someone sounds like they know what they are doing, stop and ask what independent evidence you have beyond how they sound. Clear, confident explanation is a sales skill, not proof of competence.

### Step 3: Invert the credential bias

Conditional on an equally strong track record, give real weight to the candidate who succeeded despite less prestigious credentials or a rougher presentation; they had to clear a higher bar of actual results to get taken seriously at all.

### Step 4: Distinguish decorative expertise from load bearing expertise

Some domains are decorative by design (ceremony, branding, credentialed ritual), and some are load bearing, meaning the thing actually has to work or someone gets hurt or loses money. Match your scrutiny to which kind of domain you are hiring into.

### Step 5: Ask what happens to them if they get it wrong

As a final filter, check whether the candidate's compensation or reputation is actually tied to outcomes, or whether they are paid regardless of results, by the hour or by the appearance of sophistication. Prefer the ones whose pay tracked their actual record.

## Anti-patterns

- Choosing the more articulate, better credentialed, or better dressed candidate without checking a real, consequential track record.
- Treating fluency in a field's jargon as evidence of competence in that field.
- Applying decorative domain scrutiny, does it look sophisticated, to a load bearing domain, does it actually work.
- Hiring someone whose pay or reputation does not depend on the actual outcomes they produce.

## Output shape

A short comparison of the candidates or experts in question: their actual track record under real stakes, how much of the impression they made comes from polish versus results, and a recommendation weighted toward demonstrated performance over presentation.
