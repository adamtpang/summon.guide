# The Turkey Problem

Derived from *The Black Swan* by Nassim Nicholas Taleb (Chapter 4: One Thousand and One Days, or How Not to Be a Sucker).

## Core Principle

A creature fed every day for a thousand days rationally concludes, from pure observation, that humans exist to feed it, and its confidence in that belief is highest on the afternoon before Thanksgiving, exactly when the risk is highest. Past data, however long the run, cannot reveal a structural break that has not happened yet, so a long safe track record is not evidence of safety, it is evidence that the fatal event has not yet occurred inside the observation window. The correct response to a long good streak in a domain that can produce extreme outcomes is more suspicion, not more confidence.

## Framework

### Step 1: Name whose hand is feeding you

Identify who or what controls the good outcomes you have been observing (a client, a market regime, a regulator, a boss). Ask directly whether that same party or mechanism could, without warning, become the one that wrings your neck.

### Step 2: Check whether the track record and the risk share a source

If years of profit have come from a single kind of bet (one client, one strategy, one country, one asset class), the long track record is not independent evidence of safety, it is one long unbroken exposure to the very same risk.

### Step 3: Ask what a first-time event would look like

Explicitly imagine the one thousand and first day: the event with no antecedent in your data. If you cannot describe even a rough shape for it, that is a sign you have been extrapolating the past instead of bounding the future.

### Step 4: Notice when comfort is rising with risk

Watch for the specific pattern where confidence, bonuses, or reputation for being conservative are increasing at the same time that unrecognized exposure is quietly building. That combination is the turkey's exact position, not a coincidence to be dismissed.

### Step 5: Refuse to be reassured by more of the same evidence

When someone offers a longer version of the same track record as reassurance (twelve risk managers instead of two, another decade of the same data), recognize that more of the same kind of evidence does not address a break that has never yet occurred, and respond by reducing exposure instead of accepting the reassurance.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a long unbroken streak as proof of a sound method rather than as an unbounded exposure that has not yet been tested.
- Accepting reassurance in the form of more data from the same source, instead of asking what kind of event the data could never have shown you.
- Letting rising confidence and rising unrecognized risk build together without noticing they are the same phenomenon.
- Assuming that because you cannot describe the next break precisely, it is safe to assume none is coming.

## Output shape

A short brief naming the feeding hand, whether the track record and the risk share a single source, a rough sketch of what a first-time break would look like, and one concrete change to reduce exposure regardless of how good the streak has been.
