# Distrust the Track Record That Fits the Current Regime Too Well

Derived from *Fooled by Randomness* by Nassim Nicholas Taleb (Chapter Five, Survival of the Least Fit, A Review of Market Fools of Randomness Constants).

## Core Principle

At any single point in time, the person or strategy dominating a field is disproportionately likely to be whoever happens to fit the current cycle's specific conditions, not whoever has the most durable edge. The longer that fit goes untested by a regime change, the more exposed it is when the cycle turns, and the operator often has no idea the exposure exists. The book's own list of failure traits gives a concrete checklist for spotting this before the regime turns: overestimating the accuracy of one's own beliefs, refusing to revise a losing position, having no predetermined exit plan, and reflexive denial when challenged.

## Framework

### Step 1: Ask what regime produced this winner

Identify the specific conditions, a rally, a rate environment, a narrow style, that the current top performer's trait happens to match.

### Step 2: Run the checklist

Check the streak holder's behavior against the named failure traits: overestimating the accuracy of their own beliefs, marrying a position instead of revising it, having no predetermined exit plan, and reflexive denial when challenged.

### Step 3: Ask what breaks it

Name the specific regime change that would end the streak, and estimate how exposed the operator actually is to it.

### Step 4: Separate imitation from adaptation

If you copy the strategy, copy the risk controls that would survive a regime change, not just the trait that happens to be winning right now.

## Anti-patterns

- Copying whoever is currently winning without asking what regime produced the win
- Treating a run of good years as proof against the possibility of a single bad one
- Ignoring a streak holder who has no predetermined plan for what happens if the trade goes wrong
- Mistaking confidence and swagger for evidence of skill

## Output shape

A short regime audit: what conditions produced the current winner, which of the named failure traits are present, and what specific event would end the streak.
