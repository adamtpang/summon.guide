# The Silent Evidence Audit

Derived from *The Black Swan* by Nassim Nicholas Taleb (Chapter 8: Giacomo Casanova's Unfailing Luck: The Problem of Silent Evidence).

## Core Principle

History and every visible success record are written exclusively by the survivors, so any trait that a group of winners shares is worthless as an explanation unless you also check whether the losers, the ones who left no trace, shared the exact same trait. Diagoras was shown paintings of worshippers who prayed and survived a shipwreck, and asked where the paintings were of those who prayed and drowned. The bias hides best exactly where it matters most, because the more lethal or more selective a process is, the more thoroughly it erases the evidence of its own randomness.

## Framework

### Step 1: Name the process that produced your visible sample

Identify the specific filter that determined who or what you are looking at (survived to write a memoir, got published, kept their job, is still in business). Every trait you are about to admire was filtered through that process, not just displayed by the winner.

### Step 2: Sketch the invisible cemetery

Estimate, even roughly, how many people or attempts started down the same path and did not make it into your sample. If the visible winners are a tiny fraction of the starters, treat the shared traits among winners as close to meaningless until proven otherwise.

### Step 3: Check if the losers shared the same trait

Ask specifically whether the failed attempts also had the trait being credited for the success (courage, a certain habit, a certain style). If the graveyard is full of people with the identical trait, the trait did not cause the outcome, luck plus survival selection did.

### Step 4: Run the computational cemetery test

For a claim based on a track record (an investor who beat the market for years, a method that never fails), mentally simulate a large population of purely random actors doing the same thing and check whether some of them would produce an equally impressive streak by chance alone. If yes, the observed streak is not proof of skill.

### Step 5: Weight severity by how well the failures were hidden

Treat any domain where failure is especially costly, embarrassing, or fatal (financial blowups, medical outcomes, extreme risk taking) with extra suspicion, because those are exactly the domains where the failures are least likely to have left a visible record.

## Anti-patterns

- Extracting lessons from a set of winners without checking whether the losers shared the identical traits.
- Treating a track record as proof of skill without asking how many random actors doing the same thing would have produced an equally good streak by chance.
- Assuming a strategy is safe because the visible practitioners of it are all still around, when the ones who blew up left no memoir.
- Giving the most weight to the domains where failure is most severe, which are precisely the domains where the failures are hardest to see.

## Output shape

A short note naming the filter that produced the visible sample, a rough estimate of the hidden cemetery size, and a verdict on whether the celebrated trait actually distinguishes winners from losers or simply describes everyone who tried.
