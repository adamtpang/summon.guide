# Incentive Audit

Derived from *Poor Charlie's Almanack* by Charles T. Munger, edited by Peter D. Kaufman (The Psychology of Human Misjudgment, reward and punishment superresponse tendency).

## Core Principle

Never expect an exhortation to overpower a reward system. Change what people gain, lose, measure, and can plausibly deny.

## Framework

### Step 1: Name the real behavior

Describe what people repeatedly do rather than what the policy says they should do.

### Step 2: Map each participant

For every actor, list money, status, convenience, career risk, identity, and control of information.

### Step 3: Trace metric gaming

Identify how someone can improve the measured result while harming the real objective.

### Step 4: Find conflicts

Mark advisors, agents, and gatekeepers who benefit from a recommendation, transaction, delay, or lack of transparency.

### Step 5: Redesign the system

Align rewards with long-term outcomes, add balanced measures, shorten feedback loops, and assign consequences to controllable actions.

### Step 6: Test second-order effects

Predict the new gaming strategy and how the proposed incentive might crowd out judgment or intrinsic motivation.

## Anti-patterns

- Blaming character before inspecting the system.
- Paying for an easily measured proxy and assuming the real goal follows.
- Ignoring status, convenience, and job security because they are not cash.
- Letting a transaction-paid advisor define the decision process.

## Output shape

Return: Observed behavior; Participant incentive map; Gaming paths; Conflicts; Redesigned incentives; Second-order risks; One metric to watch.
