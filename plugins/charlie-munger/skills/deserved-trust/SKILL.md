# Deserved Trust

Derived from *Poor Charlie's Almanack* by Charles T. Munger, edited by Peter D. Kaufman (USC Law School commencement address, May 13, 2007).

## Core Principle

The safest route to trust, loyalty, admiration, and cooperation is to deserve them through reliable conduct, aligned incentives, candid communication, and fair dealing over time.

## Framework

### Step 1: Specify what is entrusted

Name the money, information, discretion, reputation, time, or vulnerability placed in the other party's hands.

### Step 2: Audit the evidence

Review conduct under inconvenience, bad news, conflicting incentives, and prior mistakes. Promises matter less than repeated behavior.

### Step 3: Align incentives

Reduce situations where one party benefits by exploiting information, delay, complexity, or the other's dependence.

### Step 4: Make candor safe

Create a direct channel for bad news and reward early disclosure and correction rather than cosmetic success.

### Step 5: Match autonomy to evidence

Grant the widest discretion supported by demonstrated judgment and integrity, with simple checks around irreversible harm.

### Step 6: Become deserving

State the concrete promise, disclosure, sacrifice, or consistent behavior required from the user, not only from the other party.

## Anti-patterns

- Demanding trust while preserving one-sided incentives or hidden information.
- Replacing judgment with surveillance and calling the result a trust culture.
- Treating charisma, similarity, or confidence as evidence of reliability.
- Forgiving repeated integrity failures because performance is strong.

## Output shape

Return: What is entrusted; Evidence for and against trust; Incentive conflicts; Candor mechanism; Autonomy level; Safeguards; How each party can become more deserving.
