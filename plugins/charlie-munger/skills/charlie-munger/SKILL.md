# The Munger Rationality Checklist

Derived from *Poor Charlie's Almanack* by Charles T. Munger, edited by Peter D. Kaufman (Elementary Worldly Wisdom, Psychology of Human Misjudgment, USC 2007 address, and Vice Chairman's Thoughts).

## Core Principle

Reliable judgment comes from avoiding ruin and obvious folly, then viewing the remaining choice through incentives, psychology, opportunity cost, and a latticework of models from multiple disciplines.

## Framework

### Step 1: Invert

List the outcomes that would make the situation a disaster, then identify the behaviors and conditions that reliably produce them.

### Step 2: Remove ruin

Eliminate paths that can permanently destroy capital, health, trust, reputation, or future options.

### Step 3: Follow incentives

Map what every participant gains from action, delay, measurement, and advice. Expect behavior to follow the reward system.

### Step 4: Run the psychology checklist

Test commitment, social proof, authority, envy, reciprocation, deprival, contrast, availability, and combinations of biases.

### Step 5: Use a latticework

Reframe the problem through economics, probability, engineering, biology, and history. Seek models that disagree.

### Step 6: Compare opportunity costs

Judge each option against the best alternative, including waiting. Give the scarce resource to the strongest use.

### Step 7: State the least stupid action

Recommend the action that survives the checks, along with the conditions that would reverse it.

## Anti-patterns

- Using one favorite model for every problem.
- Accepting a person's explanation without inspecting the incentive.
- Seeking clever upside before removing paths to ruin.
- Recommending named securities or personalized allocations instead of decision principles.

## Output shape

Return: Inversion; Ruin paths; Incentive map; Psychology check; Mental-model views; Opportunity cost; Least stupid action; Reversal conditions.
