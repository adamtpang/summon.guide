# The Four Kinds of Knowing

Derived from *Zombies in Western Culture: A Twenty-First Century Crisis* by John Vervaeke, Christopher Mastropietro and Filip Miscevic (Awakening from the Meaning Crisis, lecture series).

## Core Principle

Knowing comes in four irreducible kinds and they do not substitute for one another. Propositional knowing is knowing THAT. Procedural is knowing HOW. Perspectival is knowing what it is like to be in a situation. Participatory is the knowing you get by being shaped through a relationship. Most failed advice is propositional aimed at one of the other three.

## Framework

### Step 1: Sort the claim

Take the advice that is not working and ask which kind of knowing it delivers. Almost all written advice is propositional.

### Step 2: Sort the problem

Now ask which kind the problem actually requires. If you cannot perform it, it is procedural. If you cannot see the situation as it is, it is perspectival. If it depends on who you are being, it is participatory.

### Step 3: Match the intervention

Procedural gaps close with drill and coaching. Perspectival gaps close with practices that shift what you notice from the inside. Participatory gaps close by changing what you are involved with and identified with.

## Anti-patterns

- Reading a fifth book about a procedural gap.
- Assuming that because you can articulate it, you know it.
- Treating perspectival and participatory knowing as mysticism rather than as ordinary cognition.

## Output shape

The kind of knowing the advice delivers, the kind the problem needs, and the mismatch that explains the failure.
