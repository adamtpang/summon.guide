# Greatness Is Deviance

Derived from Visakan Veerasamy, essay *Greatness is Deviance* (visakanv.com/blog/greatness-is-deviance).

## Core Principle

Veerasamy argues that greatness, by definition, requires behaving differently than most people, and that difference will look and feel like insanity before it looks like anything else. Nobody achieves a result nobody else can yet see without first passing through the crucible of cringe, caring about something before the people around them agree it is worth caring about. If you want to do something great, deviating from the norm is not a risk of the path, it is the path.

## Framework

### Step 1: Name what you are actually holding back

Identify the specific ambition, project, or piece of work you are not pursuing or not sharing because of how it would look.

### Step 2: Locate the cringe precisely

Ask exactly what feels embarrassing about it: caring too visibly, being premature, being wrong in public, or being unlike the people around you. Naming it precisely is more useful than a vague sense of discomfort.

### Step 3: Check whether the cringe is evidence against the idea or evidence of its direction

Ask whether the discomfort is telling you the idea is bad, or telling you it deviates from what is currently considered normal. These are different signals and get treated very differently.

### Step 4: Do the deviant version anyway, in public, in small steps

Share or attempt the version that feels premature or strange, in a small enough step that it is survivable, rather than waiting for a moment when it will feel safe.

### Step 5: Expect the discomfort to precede, not follow, the result

Do not wait for the cringe to disappear before acting. The cringe is the leading edge of doing something before consensus has caught up to it, not a sign to stop.

## Anti-patterns

- Waiting for an idea to stop feeling embarrassing before pursuing it, when the embarrassment is often the signal it deviates from the norm rather than proof it is wrong.
- Treating social discomfort as automatic evidence that an idea is bad.
- Sharing only the safe, already validated version of your work instead of the deviant one.
- Needing consensus approval before starting, when consensus by definition arrives after deviance, not before it.

## Output shape

A short plan for the thing you are holding back: what specifically it is, what exactly feels cringe about it, whether that discomfort is really evidence against the idea or just evidence it is early, and one small, survivable, public step you can take on it this week.
