# Write for the Outliers, Not Most People

Derived from Visakan Veerasamy, essay *Most People* (visakanv.com/blog/most-people).

## Core Principle

Veerasamy is explicit that he does not write for the average reader. He writes for the outliers, the 0.1 percent in optimism, thoughtfulness, creativity, kindness, competence, ambition, drive, and curiosity, on the reasoning that aiming at the median produces work that reaches everyone a little and moves no one much, while work aimed precisely at the outliers actually changes something for the people it is genuinely for.

## Framework

### Step 1: Notice when you are aiming at "most people"

Catch the moments you are softening a piece of work, a request, or a personal standard specifically so it will not alienate an imagined average person.

### Step 2: Name who the work is actually for

Instead of imagining a generic audience, name the specific kind of person, the outlier in some dimension, this is genuinely meant to reach.

### Step 3: Raise the standard back up

Remove the softening you added for the average reader and restate the work at the level that would actually land for the outlier audience you named.

### Step 4: Accept that most people will not respond

Expect that aiming precisely at outliers means most people will pass over the work without much reaction. That is the cost of aiming correctly, not a sign the aim was wrong.

### Step 5: Trust the smaller, stronger reaction over the larger, weaker one

If the outlier audience responds strongly, treat that as success, even if the total reach is smaller than the diluted version would have gotten.

## Anti-patterns

- Softening a piece of work specifically to avoid alienating an imagined average person, at the cost of impact on the people it is actually for.
- Measuring success only by total reach instead of by depth of reaction from the people the work was actually aimed at.
- Being discouraged by a lukewarm response from people the work was never meant for in the first place.
- Lowering a personal standard to impress the average of your friends instead of the people whose standards you actually respect.

## Output shape

A short revision plan for the piece of work or standard in question: where it has been softened for an average reader, who the actual intended outlier audience is, the raised version restated for that audience, and an acknowledgment that reach may shrink while impact should grow.
