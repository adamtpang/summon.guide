# Word Magic

Derived from Visakan Veerasamy, essay *Word Magic* (visakanv.com/blog/word-magic), and the 1000wordvomits project begun in 2012.

## Core Principle

Veerasamy writes to figure himself out, not to report a conclusion he has already reached elsewhere. He calls this word magic: experimenting with many different phrasings of a half formed thought until a handful of them, what he calls words of power, actually carry the real weight of what he means. His 1000wordvomits project, a commitment to a thousand unedited essays of at least a thousand words, treats this as a deliberate long term practice, not an occasional exercise.

## Framework

### Step 1: Start before you understand it

Begin writing about the confusing or emotionally loaded thing before you feel ready, rather than waiting for clarity that writing itself is supposed to produce.

### Step 2: Write it unedited, in full

Do not stop to polish or reorganize while writing the first pass. Let the associative, messy version come out completely before judging any of it.

### Step 3: Try the same thought several different ways

Rephrase the central point multiple times in different words. Do not settle for the first phrasing; the version that finally feels exactly right is often the fourth or fifth attempt, not the first.

### Step 4: Notice which phrase actually lands

Read back through what you wrote and notice which specific sentence or phrase carries more weight than the others, the one that feels true in a way the rest does not quite reach. That is the word of power for this particular thought.

### Step 5: Let the writing change your understanding, not just record it

Treat the act of writing as capable of changing what you actually think, not merely describing a fixed prior opinion. If your view shifts partway through, follow the shift rather than forcing the ending to agree with the beginning.

## Anti-patterns

- Waiting to write about something until you already understand it, which guarantees you never start on the hardest things.
- Editing while drafting, which cuts off the associative process before it finds the real phrasing.
- Settling for the first phrasing of an idea instead of trying it several different ways.
- Treating writing as only a record of a finished thought instead of a tool that can genuinely change your mind mid-sentence.

## Output shape

A short piece of unedited writing on the confusing thing itself: written in full before editing, tried in at least two different phrasings, with the sentence that actually carries the most weight identified afterward as the word of power for this particular thought.
