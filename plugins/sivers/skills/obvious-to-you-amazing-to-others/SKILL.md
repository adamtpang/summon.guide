# Obvious to You, Amazing to Others

Derived from Derek Sivers, essay *Obvious to You. Amazing to Others.* (sive.rs/obvious).

## Core Principle

Everybody's ideas seem obvious to them. That is exactly why a person is the wrong judge of their own creations: the very knowledge that makes something feel too basic to mention is the same knowledge that makes it valuable to someone standing one step behind you. Sivers' fix is not to manufacture false confidence, it is to stop trusting your own flat judgment and let the work reach other people so they can decide.

## Framework

### Step 1: Notice the dismissal itself

Catch the specific moment you think "everyone already knows this" or "this is too simple to be worth sharing." That reaction is data about your own proximity to the idea, not a verdict on its value.

### Step 2: Ask who is one step behind you, not zero steps

The idea does not need to be new to experts. It needs to be new, or newly clear, to someone who has not yet had the exact realization you are dismissing as obvious.

### Step 3: Put the smallest version of it out

Do not wait to build the polished, comprehensive version. Share the plain, obvious to you version and see what actually lands, because your prediction about its value is unreliable either direction.

### Step 4: Let the response be the judge, not your prediction

Track what people actually respond to, not what you expected them to respond to. The gap between your prediction and the real response is exactly the information you cannot generate by thinking harder alone.

### Step 5: Repeat with the next thing you are calling obvious

Treat every future "this is too obvious to mention" as a flag to check, not a fact to accept. The pattern repeats throughout a working life, not just once.

## Anti-patterns

- Withholding knowledge or a skill because it feels too basic, without ever testing that judgment against a real audience.
- Waiting to share until the material feels advanced enough to be impressive to you specifically.
- Assuming your own excitement or boredom with an idea predicts how useful it will be to someone else.
- Deciding an idea has no value based only on your own reaction, with no outside check at all.

## Output shape

A short check on whatever you are calling too obvious to matter: what specifically you are dismissing, who might be exactly one step behind you on it, the smallest version of it you could put out this week, and what you will actually track to find out if your own judgment was right.
