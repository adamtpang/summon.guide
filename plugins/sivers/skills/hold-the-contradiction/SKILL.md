# Hold the Contradiction

Derived from *How to Live* by Derek Sivers, 27 chapters, each fully convinced of a different way to live.

## Core Principle

Sivers wrote How to Live as 27 short chapters, each one arguing its own way of living completely, with no hedging, often directly contradicting the chapter next to it. Independence and commitment are each argued as fully true. Mastering one thing and staying a permanent beginner are each argued as fully true. He does not think the contradiction is a flaw to be resolved. He thinks forcing every situation into one universal rule is the actual error, and that different moments in a life genuinely call for opposite approaches.

## Framework

### Step 1: Name both sides fully, without softening either

State the two approaches you are torn between as strongly as their strongest advocate would state them, not as a weak version you have already decided to reject.

### Step 2: Resist collapsing them into one rule immediately

Notice the urge to declare one side the real answer and the other a mistake. Sit with both being genuinely true before choosing.

### Step 3: Ask which side this specific situation calls for

Instead of asking which approach is correct in general, ask which one fits the actual situation you are in right now, at this stage, with these constraints.

### Step 4: Let the choice be provisional, not permanent

Choose the side that fits now, and treat it as the right call for this chapter, not a lifelong verdict. The other side may be exactly right for a different situation later.

### Step 5: Revisit when the situation changes

When circumstances shift, allow the opposite approach to become correct again without treating that as inconsistency. The contradiction is the honest map, not a bug in your thinking.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating one side of a genuine contradiction as universally correct and the other as always wrong.
- Refusing to choose at all because both sides feel true, instead of picking the one this specific moment calls for.
- Feeling like a hypocrite for acting on the opposite approach later, when the situation itself has changed.
- Flattening a real tension into a compromise that satisfies neither side, instead of fully committing to the one the moment calls for.

## Output shape

A short resolution for the specific tension you are in: both sides stated fully and fairly, which one this particular situation actually calls for and why, and an acknowledgment that the other side remains true in general and may be the right call again later.
