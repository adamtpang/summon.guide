# Earn What Can't Be Given

Derived from *How to Live* by Derek Sivers, the chapter on mastering something.

## Core Principle

Sivers argues mastery is the best possible goal precisely because it is the one thing that cannot be handed to anyone. The rich cannot buy it, the impatient cannot rush it, the privileged cannot inherit it, and nobody can steal it once it is earned. Every other common goal, wealth, status, connections, can in principle be given to you by someone else's decision. Mastery requires your own repeated hours and cannot be substituted.

## Framework

### Step 1: List what you are currently pursuing

Write down the actual goals you are spending time on right now, not the goals you would claim if asked.

### Step 2: Sort each one by whether it could be given to you

For each goal, ask honestly: could someone else simply hand me this if they wanted to. Money, connections, a title, and status generally can be given. A skill generally cannot.

### Step 3: Notice which pursuits you are outsourcing hope to

Look for goals where you are quietly hoping someone else will provide the break, the introduction, or the opportunity, rather than building the underlying ability yourself.

### Step 4: Redirect time toward what only repetition can produce

Choose the goal on your list that depends most on your own accumulated hours, and treat it as the priority, even if it is slower and less immediately visible than the goals that depend on someone else's decision.

### Step 5: Judge progress by hours invested, not by permission granted

Track whether you actually put in the work this week, rather than whether anyone noticed, approved, or opened a door. The door opening is a side effect, not the goal.

## Anti-patterns

- Spending most of your effort on goals that depend on someone else deciding to give you something.
- Treating a lucky break as proof of mastery, when the underlying skill was never actually built.
- Getting discouraged by the slowness of mastery and switching to a faster, giveable goal instead.
- Measuring progress by external recognition instead of by hours of real practice invested.

## Output shape

A short audit of your current goals: which ones could be given to you by someone else's decision, which one depends entirely on your own accumulated hours, and a concrete commitment to hours this week toward the one thing nobody can hand you.
