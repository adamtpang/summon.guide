# Most Productive Humans of All Time — the daily-method roster

A companion to [`greatest-humans.md`](greatest-humans.md), ranked on a **different axis**.
That index ranks by Pantheon HPI, which measures *fame*. This one ranks by **output plus
transferable daily method**: how much a person actually produced, and how much of *how they
did it* you can steal for your own days. Machine-readable source:
[`data/most-productive-humans.json`](../data/most-productive-humans.json). Curated 2026-07-25.

**16 humans.** Only 5 appear in the HPI fame index (Edison, Churchill, Bach, Euler,
Picasso). Of the other 11, one (Franklin) you already have as a guide, and the remaining
10 (Trollope, Asimov, Drucker, Newport, Holiday, Ferriss, Clear, King, Erdos, Roosevelt)
are invisible to a fame ranking yet are exactly who you learn daily execution from. That
gap is the whole point of this list.

Legend: **PI** = Productivity Index (see scoring) · ✅ = already a summon.guide guide ·
📇 = already in the HPI fame index · ⚠️ = severe landmine, gate onboarding · Track = how
you get their corpus (historical = their books; modern = youchop video + books).

## How the Productivity Index is scored

Not a computed metric like HPI. An editorial score, but a transparent one. Each person gets:

- **Output (0 to 40)** — sheer documented volume of finished work.
- **Method (0 to 40)** — how concrete and transferable the daily technique is. Trollope's
  word-count clock maxes this. "Be a genius" scores near zero.
- **Fit (0 to 20)** — relevance to a knowledge worker trying to be productive in their days.

`PI = Output + Method + Fit`. The tension is deliberate: Picasso and Euler have the maximum
Output score and still land at the bottom, because raw volume is not a method you can borrow.
The people at the top are not the most prolific. They are the most *learnable*.

## Tier 1 — Steal the method (build these first)

These solve the stated problem directly: being productive in your days. Each leaves a
concrete, transferable daily technique, not just an inspiring life.

- **Isaac Asimov** — PI 95. Wrote or edited ~500 books across science fiction, science,
  history, and more. His secret was structural: he ran several books at once, so when one
  stalled he rotated to another and never sat blocked. "I write for the same reason I
  breathe." Source: *It's Been a Good Life* and *I. Asimov: A Memoir*.
  Skill idea: `/asimov:no-writers-block`.
- **Anthony Trollope** — PI 92. Produced 47 novels while holding a demanding full-time job
  at the Post Office, by writing 250 words every 15 minutes with a watch on the desk, three
  hours each morning before work. If he finished a novel mid-session he started the next one
  on the same page. The most concrete daily-output system ever recorded. Source: *An
  Autobiography* (public domain). Skill idea: `/trollope:the-clock-method`.
- **Stephen King** — PI 88. 2,000 words a day, every day, holidays and birthdays included.
  A fixed quota removes the daily negotiation with yourself. Source: *On Writing*. Modern,
  so also youchop-able. Skill idea: `/stephen-king:the-daily-quota`.
- **Benjamin Franklin** — PI 85. Already a guide, so a productivity skill costs zero corpus
  work. His daily schedule ("What good shall I do this day?" at 5am, a nightly reckoning)
  and 13-virtue rotation are the original self-management system. Source: *Autobiography*
  (already grounded in the repo). Skill idea: `/franklin:the-daily-schedule`.
- **Peter Drucker** — PI 84. Invented the term "knowledge worker" and wrote the manual for
  managing one: yourself. *The Effective Executive* argues you manage time before tasks,
  organize around contribution not activity, and do first things first, one thing at a time.
  Skill idea: `/drucker:effective-executive`.

## Tier 2 — Output monsters (proof a system compounds)

Staggering raw volume. The method is less portable, but the lives prove what a daily engine
produces over decades.

- **Thomas Edison** — 1,093 patents via a systematized lab. Already in the fame index. ⚠️
  business ruthlessness (the Tesla feud, publicity stunts) gates a clean onboarding.
- **Winston Churchill** — ~43 books and a Nobel in Literature *alongside* running a war,
  on a rigid daily rhythm. Already in the fame index. ⚠️ colonial record (Bengal famine).
- **J.S. Bach** — ~1,100 works on a weekly delivery cadence. Already in the fame index.
- **Leonhard Euler** — ~886 works, productive through total blindness. Already in the fame
  index. The lesson is equanimity, which is real but hard to package as a daily how-to.
- **Paul Erdos** — ~1,500 papers, more than any mathematician, through monomania and radical
  collaboration. NOT in the fame index. ⚠️ his amphetamine use must not be valorized; the
  transferable lesson is the focus and the collaboration, not the speed.
- **Pablo Picasso** — ~50,000 works, the all-time record. Already in the fame index. ⚠️
  personal conduct. Proves the thesis perfectly: maximum output, minimal borrowable method.

## Tier 3 — Modern teachers (best youchop targets)

Living, with a large body of talks and podcasts. Their corpus comes from video via
youchop.app, plus their books. These are the ones your stated pipeline was built for.

- **Cal Newport** — *Deep Work*, *Slow Productivity*. Aimed exactly at the modern knowledge
  worker. Hundreds of hours of talks.
- **Ryan Holiday** — a book a year on a documented notecard research system. Prolific *and*
  methodical, a rare combination. Very youchop-able.
- **Tim Ferriss** — the effectiveness-over-efficiency canon, plus a podcast that is itself a
  corpus of how hundreds of top performers structure their days.
- **James Clear** — *Atomic Habits*. Habits are the atomic unit of daily productivity, which
  makes him the most on-the-nose entry for the stated problem.

## Getting the corpus: two tracks

Your stated pipeline was "find their books, use youchop.app/extract, create skills." One
correction that reroutes half the list: **youchop extracts YouTube transcripts, so it only
works on people with video.** The dead giants have none. For them the book *is* the corpus.

| Track | Who | Source | Tool | Availability |
|-------|-----|--------|------|--------------|
| **Historical** | Trollope, Asimov, Drucker, Edison, Euler, Bach, Erdos, TR, Picasso, Churchill | Their books, letters, papers | `pdf-to-markdown`, Gutenberg, archive.org | Trollope, TR, Edison public domain; others purchasable |
| **Already grounded** | Franklin | Autobiography, already in the repo | none needed | in repo |
| **Modern** | Newport, King, Holiday, Ferriss, Clear | YouTube talks + podcasts, plus books | youchop.app + `channel-corpus` skill | video free, books purchasable |

## Recommended build order

1. **Trollope, Asimov, Drucker** first. Purest daily methods, historical track, mostly
   public domain or cheap, no video dependency. Three runnable skills fast, filling a real
   hole: the current roster is thin on pure execution discipline.
2. **Franklin productivity skill** next. Zero corpus cost, he is already grounded, and the
   daily schedule is directly on-problem.
3. **Cal Newport + James Clear** to prove the modern youchop track end to end.
4. Everyone else as the roster deepens.

Onboarding each figure follows the existing guide-onboarding checklist and the
[`PROMPT_founders-corpus-upgrade.md`](../PROMPT_founders-corpus-upgrade.md) grounding pattern:
import corpus, map figure to sources, ground the chat, then generate one playbook skill per
figure from the real source. No figure ships citing a source that is not in the corpus.
