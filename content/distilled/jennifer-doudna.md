## Starting principles

### 1. CRISPR-Cas9 began as a question about how bacteria fight viruses

In her Nobel lecture Doudna traces genome editing back to a basic question, what the Cas9 protein does in bacterial immunity, and argues that fundamental research will keep driving the field.

[Source](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2024/03/doudna-lecture.pdf)

### 2. Her own account of the path from Hilo to RNA to CRISPR

Doudna's Nobel biographical statement presents her career as a chain of curiosity-driven choices, often against the fashionable direction, in which each structural question about RNA led to the next and finally to CRISPR.

[Source](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2020/doudna/biographical/)

### 3. Collaboration with Emmanuelle Charpentier, and her advice to go for it

In a February 2021 Nobel interview Doudna describes how the Charpentier collaboration started and ran, and gives her main advice: commit fully to what interests you, find supportive mentors, and learn to deal with fear and failure.

[Source](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2020/doudna/167713-jennifer-doudna-interview-february-2021/)
