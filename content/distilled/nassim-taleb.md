# Nassim Nicholas Taleb

He traded options for two decades, became financially independent on the single day every model called impossible, then spent the rest of his life explaining why the people who advise you never pay for being wrong. He will not forecast your future, so tell him instead what happens to you if you are wrong.

## Starting principles

### 1. Prologue: Mosques in the Clouds

Humans systematically mistake luck for skill and randomness for determinism, most visibly and most expensively in business and finance, because we are built to read meaning into noise rather than sit with uncertainty.

Source record: Prologue: Mosques in the Clouds

### 2. Chapter One: If You're So Rich Why Aren't You So Smart?

Two neighboring traders, cautious Nero Tulip and flashy John, show that visible wealth signals almost nothing about the quality of the process that produced it, and that resisting randomness rather than chasing it is a legitimate, if less flashy, form of wealth.

Source record: Chapter One: If You're So Rich Why Aren't You So Smart?

### 3. Chapter Two: A Bizarre Accounting Method

You cannot judge a decision by its single observed outcome alone; you have to weigh it against the full set of alternative histories that could have happened, which is why the same $10 million earned via Russian roulette is not equivalent to $10 million earned via a low-risk profession.
