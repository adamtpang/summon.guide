# Reason Is Fun: Selected Essays

This governs what you do when part of you will not go along with the plan. Tanett works in the Popper and Deutsch tradition and applies it inward: your motivations are competing ideas that can be in genuine rational conflict, not one unified will that needs more resolve. Her central claim is that coercion, including the kind you aim at yourself, can block a thought but can never produce one, so overriding your own resistance cannot be the mechanism by which anything actually changes.

## Do

- **Treat needing to force yourself as a diagnosis rather than a virtue.** Effortful self-coercion is evidence that part of you disagrees with the plan and that the disagreement has not been worked out. Find what the resisting part is objecting to. Overriding a live disagreement does not end it, it suppresses it until it comes back as procrastination, burnout, or resentment. [Source: "Discipline Is Fighting Yourself"]

- **Run the decision through the fun test before committing to it.** Tanett identifies one mechanism underneath Deutsch's fun criterion, Marie Kondo's spark joy, and Derek Sivers' hell yeah or no: all three test whether your own tacit ideas are aligned or quietly objecting. Because the three come from unrelated fields, epistemology, tidying, and lifestyle advice, she treats the convergence as evidence the underlying pattern is real rather than a coincidence of vocabulary. [Source: "The Fun Criterion"]

- **Find the actual contradiction before proposing a fix.** In her model a real problem is an internal contradiction inside knowledge you already hold, not a task assigned from outside, which is why locating the real problem is itself the difficult and valuable step rather than a formality before the work starts. [Source: "How Knowledge Works"]

- **Supply something you genuinely want to move toward.** Her key asymmetry: coercive patterns work by repulsion, closing off avenues of thought, while only creativity can generate a new idea. Removing a block therefore leaves nothing in its place. She draws the parallel to scientific method: a theory is not abandoned because it was criticized, it is abandoned once a better one is available. [Source: "Coercion Can't Make You Think Particular Things"]

- **Ask what would be awesome instead of what would be acceptable.** Her list of creativity boosters is concrete: orient toward what is enjoyable, navigate toward a wanted outcome rather than only away from a feared one, and treat ideas as experiments rather than commitments. [Source: "Coercion Can't Make You Think Particular Things"]

- **Notice the script when fear, hurt or anger shows up, and notice it without a verdict.** These are the three states she names as triggers for coercive scripts, which then redirect your creativity into defending the problem rather than solving it. One effect of a script running is losing track of feeling bad at all, which is part of what keeps it invisible from inside. Her remedies are compassion, attention on what you actually want, and repeated nonjudgmental noticing. [Source: "Why People Get Stuck: Coercive Scripts"]

- **Put a pause between the stimulus and the habitual reaction.** This is what she calls inhibition, and she is careful to distinguish it from social suppression: the pause exists to make room to choose with your full mind, not to stop yourself acting. Her sequence is set an intention, expand awareness, notice the urge to over-effort, decline that urge, and let the action follow. [Source: "The Alexander Technique Is Not About Posture"]

- **Keep a written list of the questions you have not answered.** Following Popper's advice to organize inquiry around problems rather than subjects, she maintains a live page of open problems grouped by theme, covering memetics, what blocks progress, goals and coercion, epistemology and aesthetics, and offers it publicly for other people to attack. [Source: "Open Problems"]

- **Reach for persuasion before force, including with yourself.** Drawing on Popper and William Godwin, she holds the same preference across parenting, disagreement about law, and politics, and treats the parliamentary tradition as a model for settling conflict by argument rather than by violence. The internal case is the same case. [Source: "Answers: A Worldview in Q&A"]

## Don't

- **Don't identify yourself with your current opinions and self-talk.** She separates the self, where awareness and creativity originate, from what the self has produced: the running monologue, the current beliefs, the habits. Criticizing the content is not an attack on the person, and collapsing that distinction is what makes ordinary feedback feel like an assault. [Source: "Self-Improvement Is Self-Aggression"]

- **Don't start a change from the premise that something is wrong with you.** That framing borrows the structure of coercive schooling: a predetermined correct answer imposed from outside, or here by one part of your mind onto another, instead of a real problem being found and solved on its own terms. Her replacement word for the whole activity is self-discovery. [Source: "Self-Improvement Is Self-Aggression"]

- **Don't go at the visible symptom directly.** Better posture happens as a byproduct of expanded awareness. Chasing posture itself produces exactly the effortful straining the technique was designed to dissolve, which is the general shape of the mistake: attacking the output instead of the thing generating it. [Source: "The Alexander Technique Is Not About Posture"]

- **Don't treat the pattern recurring as evidence about your character.** Recurrence is what the pattern does. Reading each return as a personal failure is itself part of the mechanism keeping it running, which is why she specifies noticing it nonjudgmentally and repeatedly rather than resolving to never do it again. [Source: "Why People Get Stuck: Coercive Scripts"]

- **Don't let "it is just how I am wired" close the question.** People routinely override hunger, pain and sexual desire through reasoning: dieting or deliberately overeating against hunger signals, choosing painful exercise, and, citing Godwin, the way distressing information can instantly end arousal. If reason overrides the strongest drives evolution installed, weaker social and relational tendencies have far less claim to being fixed. [Source: "Genes Don't Control People"]

- **Don't dismiss an emotion as noise for reason to override.** She declines the idea that reason should simply overrule feeling, treating emotions instead as legitimate critical input into your thinking, and treating the reflex to override them as its own kind of dogmatism rather than a rational stance. [Source: "Answers: A Worldview in Q&A"]

- **Don't expect a solved problem to be the end of it.** In her cycle, a conjecture that survives criticism generates the next problem rather than terminating the process. Treating a fix as final is what makes the next problem arrive feeling like proof the fix did not work. [Source: "How Knowledge Works"]

- **Don't accept the options you were handed.** Answering a long quiz, she repeatedly pushes back on its multiple-choice framing, arguing the offered options miss the more precise position she actually holds, and applies the same scrutiny to the format as to the questions inside it. [Source: "Answers: A Worldview in Q&A"]

## The one line

If you have to force yourself, the problem is not your willpower, it is a disagreement inside you that has not been heard out yet, and force is the one tool that cannot resolve it.
