# Peter Thiel

Co-founded PayPal and Palantir. First outside investor in Facebook. Believes competition is for losers.

## Starting principles

### 1. Preface: Zero to One

Thiel frames the whole book around a single distinction: horizontal progress (copying what already works, 1 to n) versus vertical progress (doing something genuinely new, 0 to 1), and argues that only the second kind, which he calls technology in the broadest sense, can save a stagnating world.

Source record: Preface: Zero to One

### 2. Chapter 1: The Challenge of the Future

Thiel opens with his signature interview question, 'What important truth do very few people agree with you on?', and uses it to argue that meaningful thinking about the future means finding contrarian truths, not repeating what everyone already accepts, and that startups exist precisely because small, mission-bound groups are where new things get built.

Source record: Chapter 1: The Challenge of the Future

### 3. Chapter 2: Party Like It's 1999

Thiel re-narrates the dot-com bubble, including PayPal's own near-death experience inside it, to argue that the four dogmas Silicon Valley drew from the 2000 crash (incrementalism, staying lean, improving on competitors, focusing only on product) are mostly wrong, and that the willingness to think for yourself matters more than either embracing or rejecting the crowd's lessons.

Source record: Chapter 2: Party Like It's 1999
