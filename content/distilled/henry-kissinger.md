## Starting principles

### 1. The secret trip to Beijing: how Kissinger opened the first talk with Zhou Enlai

In his first meeting with Zhou Enlai on July 9, 1971, Kissinger opened with a prepared statement of purpose, an agenda, and assurances, then separated what could be settled soon from what needed time, and said plainly what he could not deliver.

[Source](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v17/d139)

### 2. The statesman's problem: acting in a fog of incomplete knowledge

In his October 1973 Pacem in Terris address, Kissinger argues that the policymaker must choose before the facts are in, balance what is desirable against what is possible, and accept partial steps, while a purely pragmatic policy without moral purpose is empty.

[Source](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v38p1/d19)

### 3. Detente: reconciling competition with the need to coexist

Testifying to the Senate Foreign Relations Committee in September 1974, Kissinger describes detente as a continuing process of resisting pressure and rewarding restraint between two rivals who cannot impose their will on each other without intolerable risk.

[Source](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v38p1/d45)
