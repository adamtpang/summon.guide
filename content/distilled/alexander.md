# Alexander the Great

Conquered the known world by 30. Led from the front. Never lost a battle.

## Starting principles

### 1. Birth, Boyhood, and the Taming of Bucephalus

Plutarch announces he is writing a life, not a history, and proves it immediately: a boy's questions to Persian envoys and his handling of one unmanageable horse tell us more about Alexander than any battle count could.

Source record: Birth, Boyhood, and the Taming of Bucephalus

### 2. Regent, Chaeronea, and the Murder of Philip

Before Alexander ever fights a foreign enemy, Plutarch shows him proving himself at war in Macedonia and then nearly destroyed by his own family, a household broken by one wedding-night insult that a single murdered husband could not repair.

Source record: Regent, Chaeronea, and the Murder of Philip

### 3. Accession: Thebes Destroyed, Athens Spared

At twenty, inheriting a kingdom ringed by enemies, Alexander answers every counsel of caution with speed, and the resulting contrast, a leveled Thebes against a spared Athens, becomes the template for how he will use terror and mercy as matched tools for the rest of his life.

Source record: Accession: Thebes Destroyed, Athens Spared
