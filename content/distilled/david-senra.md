# David Senra

Senra's method is not celebrity worship. It is repeated close reading across hundreds of founder biographies, autobiographies, letters, interviews, and company histories. The unit of value is a pattern that survives contact with many different lives. The aim is to borrow judgment without borrowing mythology.

## Do

- **Go to the founder's own words first.** Read letters, memoirs, interviews, and internal documents before accepting a biographer's interpretation. A secondary account can supply context, but it should not replace the subject's own reasoning. [Source: "My Conversation With Patrick O'Shaughnessy, Founder of Colossus & Positive Sum"]

- **Reread the highest-signal source.** The first pass tells you what happened. Repetition reveals what the founder considered important enough to say again and what you failed to notice because you lacked the experience to recognize it. [Source: "My Conversation With Patrick O'Shaughnessy, Founder of Colossus & Positive Sum"]

- **Look for belief before ability.** Many great builders committed before their present competence justified the commitment. The useful question is not whether they started qualified, but what feedback loop let them become qualified faster than the problem grew. [Source: "The Stubborn Genius of James Dyson"]

- **Name the one obsession.** Rockefeller watched costs, Graves protected one product, Dyson stayed with one engineering problem, and Jobs guarded the product experience. The industries differ. The concentration does not. [Source: "How Todd Graves Built Raising Cane's"] [Source: "The Stubborn Genius of James Dyson"]

- **Track what they refused to sell.** Control, attention, reputation, and time often mattered more than the largest available check. Refusal exposes the founder's real objective function more clearly than a mission statement. [Source: "How Todd Graves Built Raising Cane's"] [Source: "Evan Spiegel, Snapchat: Building a Multi-Billion Dollar Company"]

- **Translate admiration into a behavior this week.** A founder story that does not change a calendar, product decision, reading habit, hiring bar, or cost structure is entertainment.

## Don't

- **Don't confuse intensity with correctness.** Obsession compounds good judgment and bad judgment alike. Keep the correction mechanism alive.

- **Don't copy the surface.** The clothing, routine, vocabulary, and industry are usually incidental. Copy the decision rule that survived across contexts.

- **Don't collect biographies as status objects.** Finish fewer, mark them up, revisit them, and make the lesson pay rent in a current decision.

## Hold

- A founder's life is a case study, not a commandment.
- Money is usually the receipt for service, ownership, focus, or leverage, not the thing the best builders optimized directly.
- The same strength that creates the company can damage the person. Study both sides of the trait.

## The one line

Read the source twice, find the repeated behavior beneath the story, and make it change one decision before the day ends.
