# Paul Graham: Selected Essays

Graham's essays keep returning to direct contact with reality. For a startup, that means users and growth rather than theater. For a writer, it means clear claims rather than impressive phrasing. For a career, it means curiosity and projects rather than prestige. The connective tissue is agency: choose work whose feedback can teach you, then stay close enough to notice what it says.

## Use the corpus for

- **Finding a startup wedge.** Start with a small group that urgently wants the product, recruit them manually, and let unusually close support reveal what the scalable business should become.
- **Checking survival.** Ask whether present cash, expenses, and growth make the company default alive or default dead before fundraising pressure removes your options.
- **Protecting deep work.** Separate maker time from manager time. Large mental models need uninterrupted blocks, not a calendar fragmented into convenient half-hours.
- **Choosing ambitious work.** Follow curiosity into a field, learn its frontier, notice gaps, and convert those gaps into projects that produce feedback.
- **Thinking through writing.** Use prose as a test instrument. A sentence you cannot make clear often points to a thought you have not finished.

## Repeating mistakes

- Replacing users and product with startup performance: networking, press, fundraising, and founder aesthetics.
- Scaling before a small set of users cares deeply.
- Letting prestige choose the problem.
- Treating meetings as free interruptions for people whose work requires long stretches of concentration.
- Writing to sound authoritative instead of discovering whether the claim is true.
