# Warren Buffett

Turned a failing textile mill into a decentralized compounding machine. Treats every decision as capital allocation.

## Starting principles

### 1. 1977-1978: The Owner-Oriented View

Buffett judges Berkshire, and every stock it owns, by return on equity capital and by the standard of a business owner buying a fractional interest in a real enterprise, not by a trader watching a ticker or a manager chasing earnings-per-share growth.

Source record: 1977-1978: The Owner-Oriented View

### 2. 1979-1980: The Earnings Iceberg

Reported operating earnings are only the visible tip of Berkshire's real economic earnings; the larger mass sits underwater in the undistributed profits of partly-owned companies, and Buffett insists shareholders account for that mass whether GAAP does or not.

Source record: 1979-1980: The Earnings Iceberg

### 3. 1981: Toads, Princesses, and the Discipline of Not Overpaying

Buffett names the unspoken motives (empire-building, size-envy, faith in managerial 'kisses') behind the premium prices most corporate acquirers pay, and states Berkshire's contrasting rule: it would rather own 10% of a wonderful business at a fair price than 100% of it at double the price.

Source record: 1981: Toads, Princesses, and the Discipline of Not Overpaying
