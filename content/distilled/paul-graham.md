# Paul Graham

Graham's essays connect startups, writing, taste, ambition, and independent thought through one recurring test: stop performing the role and work directly on the thing. Startups discover demand by making something a small number of users urgently want. Essays discover ideas by writing toward what is true. Careers become interesting when curiosity outruns prestige.

## Do

- **Make something people want.** Treat use as the hard evidence. Early users who would be genuinely upset if the product disappeared tell you more than praise, forecasts, or a polished launch. Source: [How to Start a Startup](https://www.paulgraham.com/start.html).

- **Do the unscalable work while learning.** Recruit users manually, watch them use the product, solve their edge cases, and let that contact reshape what you build. The manual phase is not beneath the company. It is how the company learns what can later scale. Source: [Do Things that Don't Scale](https://www.paulgraham.com/ds.html).

- **Ask whether you are default alive.** Using current cash, expenses, and growth, determine whether the company reaches profitability before the bank account reaches zero. Run the arithmetic before fundraising becomes an emergency. Source: [Default Alive or Default Dead?](https://www.paulgraham.com/aord.html).

- **Protect maker time.** Work that requires holding a large mental model cannot be chopped into convenient meeting-sized pieces. Put meetings together and preserve long, uninterrupted blocks for making. Source: [Maker's Schedule, Manager's Schedule](https://www.paulgraham.com/makersschedule.html).

- **Follow curiosity far enough to produce work.** Curiosity chooses the direction, but projects create the feedback. The path to great work is to choose a field, learn its frontier, notice gaps, and become willing to look strange while pursuing one. Source: [How to Do Great Work](https://www.paulgraham.com/greatwork.html).

- **Write to discover what you think.** Good writing is not decoration applied after the idea. The pressure to state a claim clearly exposes missing thought and produces new thought. Source: [Putting Ideas into Words](https://www.paulgraham.com/words.html).

## Don't

- **Don't mistake startup theater for a startup.** Networking, fundraising, press, and founder aesthetics can consume the time that should have gone into users and product.

- **Don't choose by prestige.** Prestige is delayed social approval for work other people once found inspiring. It can pull an ambitious person away from the live edge of their own curiosity. Source: [How to Do What You Love](https://www.paulgraham.com/love.html).

- **Don't scale a weak signal.** Growth amplifies the thing already present. If a small group does not care deeply, a bigger launch usually produces a bigger version of indifference.

## Hold

- Work on what seems important, even when its importance is not legible yet.
- Earn independence by reducing the number of people whose permission your work needs.
- A strong idea often begins as something the right small group wants and the broader world overlooks.

## The one line

Work directly on the real problem, stay close to the people who feel it, and let clear writing expose where your thinking is still fake.
