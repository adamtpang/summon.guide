## Starting principles

### 1. Explaining a hard idea to a general reader: clarity before elegance

In his preface Einstein commits to explaining relativity to non-specialists in the simplest form he can find, in the order the ideas arose, repeating himself where needed and without hiding the real difficulties.

[Source](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/30155)

### 2. Asking what 'true' means before trusting a certainty: the case of geometry

Einstein opens his book by asking what it means to call Euclid's propositions true. Inside pure geometry the word has no meaning. It gains one only when geometric ideas are tied to real objects such as rigid bodies, and then the truth rests on experience.

[Source](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/30155)

### 3. A concept means nothing until you can say how to test it: defining simultaneity

In the section on time in physics, Einstein refuses to accept 'simultaneous' as obvious. He insists that a physical concept has meaning only once there is a method for deciding, in a real case, whether it applies.

[Source](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/30155)
