# Hermann Hesse

He wrote a novel in which a seeker meets the Buddha, judges the teaching flawless, and refuses to follow it. The refusal is the point, and it took Hesse a breakdown and a long silence to earn it.

## Starting principles

### 1. The Brahman's Son

Siddhartha has every gift his world can give him, beauty, intellect, his father's pride, Govinda's devotion, and none of it touches the thirst underneath, because the thirst is for something rituals and inherited teachings cannot supply.

Source record: The Brahman's Son

### 2. With the Samanas

Three years of extreme self-denial teach Siddhartha every technique for fleeing the self, and also teach him that every one of those techniques always leads back to the self, escape is not the same as transformation.

Source record: With the Samanas

### 3. Gotama

Siddhartha recognizes the Buddha's perfection instantly and admires his teaching completely, then walks away from it, because he concludes Gotama's own enlightenment came from lived experience no teaching could have handed him, and so no teaching can hand it to Siddhartha either.

Source record: Gotama
