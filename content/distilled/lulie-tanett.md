# Lulie Tanett

Self-educated Oxford writer working in the Popper/Deutsch tradition. Argues that discipline is usually just internal conflict, and coercion (including on yourself) can't create a new thought.

## Starting principles

### 1. Discipline Is Fighting Yourself

What people call 'discipline' is usually internal conflict, forcing yourself to do something part of you doesn't actually want, and the fix is not more willpower but resolving the underlying disagreement about what you actually want.

Source record: Discipline Is Fighting Yourself

### 2. Self-Improvement Is Self-Aggression

Treating yourself as a broken object to be fixed through 'self-improvement' misidentifies the self with the content of your thoughts, and imports the same coercive, predetermined-answer logic as traditional schooling onto your own mind.

Source record: Self-Improvement Is Self-Aggression

### 3. The Alexander Technique Is Not About Posture

The Alexander Technique is a way of thinking and approaching action, expanding awareness, pausing before reacting, and allowing effortless action rather than straining for it, and better posture is a side effect of that, not the goal.

Source record: The Alexander Technique Is Not About Posture
