## Key lessons

- Situation: Fanny McCullough, a young woman, has lost her father, an Army officer, and her grief is worse than common. Lincoln, ten months after burying his own son Willie, writes to her from the White House.
- He is direct: sorrow comes to all, and to the young it comes with the bitterest agony because it takes them unaware. He offers no cure, saying "Perfect relief is not possible, except with time."
- He names the trap of grief: she cannot now imagine feeling better, yet he tells her that is a mistake, and that knowing it will make her a little less miserable now.
- He predicts the memory of her father will turn from an agony into "a sad sweet feeling", and sends regards to her mother. Lesson: honor the pain, promise nothing false about speed, and offer time and memory as the healing.
