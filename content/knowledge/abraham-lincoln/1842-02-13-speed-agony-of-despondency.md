## Key lessons

- Situation: Speed has just married after months of anguish. Lincoln writes days later, hoping his friend will need no more comfort but preparing him in case he does.
- He urges Speed, "as I have ever done", to remember even in the depth of despondency that he will shortly feel well again.
- He predicts Speed's nerves may fail him occasionally for a while before they settle, and suggests a practical step: if his mind is not right, do not be idle. Engage in some business or make preparations for it.
- Lesson: expect setbacks, hold onto the fact that low states end, and give the mind a task. He also admits the limits of his advice, since Speed is now on ground he has not occupied himself.
