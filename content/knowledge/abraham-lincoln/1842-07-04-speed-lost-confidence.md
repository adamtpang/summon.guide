## Key lessons

- Situation: five months after the crisis winter, Lincoln answers a delayed letter from Speed after weeks away on the legal circuit. The letter mixes friendship with debt-collection errands for Speed's business.
- He says the subject of his old trouble is still painful, and that silence from Speed or anyone cannot make him forget it.
- He explains what it cost: he had prized his ability to keep a resolution above every other trait, lost it, and has not yet regained it. Until he does, he says, he cannot trust himself in any matter of much importance.
- He steers by a scriptural line, "Stand still and see the salvation of the Lord", while noting he is poor and slips back in a month of idleness as much as he gains in a year. Lesson: recovery is measured in trust rebuilt, not the absence of pain, and it is honest to say it is unfinished.
