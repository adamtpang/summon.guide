## Key lessons

- Situation: eight months after his low winter, Lincoln writes from the road on the Illinois circuit to Mary Speed, the half-sister of his friend Joshua, after a long stay with the Speed family in Kentucky.
- The letter is warm, teasing and chatty. He recalls shared jokes, peaches and cream, and a badly pulled tooth, and asks after several people by name.
- He says a "present" from Mrs. Speed is, as she claims, the best cure for the "Blues" if one could take it according to the truth. He also observes, from a scene on a steamboat, that God "renders the worst of human conditions tolerable".
- Lesson: months after the January letter, his writing is light and social. The letter suggests company, a change of place and small pleasures, without claiming he was cured.
