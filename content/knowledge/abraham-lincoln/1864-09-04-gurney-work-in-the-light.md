## Key lessons

- Situation: after three years of war, Lincoln answers a Quaker minister who had visited him two years earlier and written to steady his faith.
- He thanks her and the many Christians whose prayers and consolations have carried him, saying it has been her purpose to strengthen his reliance on God.
- He admits the war has lasted far longer than hoped, and says God knows best and has ruled otherwise. The passage reads "The purposes of the Almighty are perfect", though we may not see them in advance.
- Lesson: he keeps faith without claiming to understand the suffering, accepts help from those who pray for him, and turns the uncertainty into earnest work in whatever light is available.
