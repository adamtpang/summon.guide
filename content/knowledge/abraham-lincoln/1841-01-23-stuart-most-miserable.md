## Key lessons

- Situation: Lincoln, a 31-year-old Illinois legislator, answers his friend and former law partner Stuart about a congressional election and apologizes that the "deplorable state of my mind" will make the reply thin.
- He does the practical political report anyway, then tells Stuart plainly that he cannot give the usual news. He writes that he is "the most miserable man living" and that he cannot say whether he will ever be better.
- He says he fears he will be unable to attend to business and suggests a change of scene might help, then stops: he can write no more. He asks Stuart to adjust plans to his condition.
- Lesson: he told a friend the truth about his state, said what he could not do, and asked for a practical adjustment instead of pretending. This note describes the letter and does not diagnose him.
