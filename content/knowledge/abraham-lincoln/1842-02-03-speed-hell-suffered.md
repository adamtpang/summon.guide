## Key lessons

- Situation: Speed, tormented by doubts and fear for his fiancee's health, has written of his "excessively bad feeling". Lincoln replies from Springfield.
- Lincoln says he feels Speed's sorrows as keenly as his own, but reads the distress as evidence of Speed's love rather than its absence: a man who did not care would be calmly resigned.
- He acknowledges the cost to himself: "You know the Hell I have suffered on that point." He adds that he has been "quite clear of hypo" since Speed left, even better than the previous autumn, and he closes with village news and a request for a fast reply.
- Lesson: he speaks from his own scar, offers a reframe of the pain rather than a lecture, and reports his own improvement without claiming it is permanent.
