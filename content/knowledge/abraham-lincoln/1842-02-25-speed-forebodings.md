## Key lessons

- Situation: Lincoln has waited anxiously for two letters from Speed and opens the second with "intense anxiety and trepidation", though it turns out better than he feared.
- He tells Speed their forebodings, "for which you and I are rather peculiar", are nonsense, and points to the evidence: Speed's second letter is plainly calmer than the first, at the very time Lincoln feared he would be worse.
- He suggests both men dream of an ideal far beyond anything earthly, so real life will fall short of the dream without being a failure. He passes on his father's saying about hugging a bad bargain tighter.
- Lesson: check dark predictions against the actual record, expect steadiness to come slowly, and do not measure a real life against an imagined perfect one.
