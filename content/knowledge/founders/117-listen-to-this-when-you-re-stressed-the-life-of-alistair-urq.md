# Listen to this when you're stressed: The Life of Alistair Urquhart

> **Founder principle (Alistair Urquhart):** Do the unglamorous groundwork long before any crisis has a name, and trust what people visibly do over what they officially say. Ration an unbearable ordeal down to one survivable day at a time rather than carrying its full weight at once. Surviving the event is only half the job; what you build with your life afterward, deliberately and on purpose, is the other half. His own adopted motto was simple: "there is no such word as can't."

*David Senra on Alistair Urquhart, reading from The Forgotten Highlander: An Incredible World War II Story of Survival in the Pacific. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1UqPlf2x9ZQ) · 50 min · published 2024-04-05.*

## Key lessons

- **Build physical and mental readiness years before you know why you will need it.** As a teenager in Scotland with no money for extras, he filled his idle hours with football, rugby, cricket, and cold water swimming every Sunday morning purely to stay busy; that unplanned fitness later helped him endure years of forced labor, and his boy scout swimming training was the specific skill that let him swim clear of a sinking, torpedoed ship instead of being pulled under with it.
- **Weigh what people are doing against what they are officially saying, and believe the doing.** While Singapore's newspapers ran headlines calling the garrison impregnable, he watched his own officers issue antique rifles, enforce thick wool uniforms and a mandatory afternoon siesta in tropical heat, and quietly speed up the evacuation of civilian families; the growing gap between the official confidence and the visible behavior around him was the real signal, and it proved right within weeks.
- **Judge a captor's or counterparty's promises only by their sustained past behavior, never by what they say is coming.** Fellow prisoners repeatedly believed guards' promises of an easier camp ahead; Urquhart consistently refused to share that optimism because nothing in the captors' actual conduct over the preceding months supported it, and each time he turned out to be right.
- **Position yourself where you can see trouble early, not where it feels safest.** On a multi-day forced march where stragglers were left behind to die, he deliberately stayed near the front of the column, reasoning that from the back you watch other men quit and fall, and that watching it erodes your own will to continue.
- **Ration your attention to the smallest survivable unit of time.** His daily mantra through 750 days of slave labor was simply "survive this day," repeated every morning; he did not try to carry the full weight of a years-long, open-ended ordeal at once, only the next 24 hours of it.
- **Having someone beyond yourself to live for is a measurable survival advantage.** He noticed that older married men in their thirties and forties, who talked constantly about getting home to wives and children, survived the starvation and forced labor of the railway at meaningfully higher rates than fitter, younger men in their late teens who had only themselves to live for.
- **Use whatever knowledge exists, however crude, when no real resource is available.** With no medicine and his legs rotting from tropical ulcers, the camp doctor packed the open wounds with maggots gathered from the latrines to eat away the dead flesh, and it worked; the same doctor's later warning to reintroduce food slowly to a starvation-shrunk stomach proved just as life-saving.
- **Surviving the event and surviving it well afterward are two different jobs.** A close friend from the same camps was never able to process the experience and drank himself to death within ten years of coming home; Urquhart instead deliberately returned to a prewar passion, ballroom dancing, as active rehabilitation, and credited rebuilding a full life around marriage, children, and grandchildren with finishing what surviving alone had not.
- **A long silence does not have to be the final word.** He said nothing publicly about any of this for over 60 years, then broke that silence deliberately in his 90s specifically so the account could become a tool other people under real strain could use against their own adversity, which is the same reason this episode exists.
