# The Biography of Bill Gates

> **Founder principle (Bill Gates):** This early Gates, the one built by Hard Drive rather than his own later memoir, is defined by total obsession and a refusal to leave money or ownership on the table. He rewrote contract language himself to own outright what he could have merely licensed, insisted on royalties instead of a flat fee on the IBM deal that later made him a billionaire, and treated every competitor as someone to be actively put out of business. He also knew exactly where his own judgment stopped, handing brand and naming decisions to people who understood them better than he did.

*David Senra on Bill Gates, reading from Hard Drive by James Wallace and Jim Erickson. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5kP8HqwM-7E) · 53 min · published 2024-02-16.*

## Key lessons

- **Channel an obsessive interest completely, as early as possible.** Gates discovered computers at 13 at Lakeside School, then hacked the school's PDP-10 accounting system with friends to give themselves more free computer time; when they got caught, the ordeal led to a paid gig hunting software bugs at night in exchange for unlimited machine access. He described the shift in himself plainly: that was when he became "hardcore."
- **Never accept a fixed price on something with unlimited upside.** IBM proposed paying Microsoft a flat fee for unlimited copies of the operating system software it needed; Gates rejected it and insisted on a royalty per copy instead, a decision the book treats as one of the most consequential of his career, and it is the arrangement that later made him a billionaire.
- **Insist on owning what you build instead of merely licensing it.** On the same IBM deal, Microsoft kept ownership of the software it developed rather than handing it over outright, partly because IBM was then under decades of antitrust scrutiny and did not want to own more. Gates personally rewrote the contract language on the Seattle Computer Products purchase so Microsoft bought the DOS rights outright for $50,000, a deal later called "the bargain of the century."
- **Treat competitors as targets to be eliminated, not coexisted with.** Gates vowed to put rivals out of business, counted a lost $50,000 contract as effectively a $100,000 swing since the money went to a competitor instead, and tracked rival companies' revenue and leadership in detail; he was once found alone at an industry event simply staring at a photograph of a rival founder.
- **When you find something you are not good at, hand it to someone who is.** Gates originally wanted to call Microsoft's new word processor Multi-Tool Word, a continuation of an existing product line; a colleague convinced him the brand itself, not the product name, had to be the hero so customers would associate quality directly with Microsoft, and Gates adopted the renamed strategy immediately.
- **Move faster than slow incumbents, and their best people will come to you.** Microsoft and Apple both recruited engineers who had grown disillusioned at Xerox PARC, where good ideas rarely reached the market because the culture ran everything by committee. Microsoft's speed of decision-making, with Gates personally involved in every decision from top to bottom, was what those engineers said drew them away.
- **Stay lean and skeptical of outside capital even while the company is printing money.** Four years after founding, Microsoft still had only 11 employees; when it later took roughly a million dollars from the venture firm Technology Venture Investors, Gates said the company mainly wanted the investor's advice rather than the cash, and the money simply went into the bank alongside the rest of Microsoft's reserves.
- **Do not celebrate a milestone as though the game is over.** On the day Microsoft went public, Gates was on a chartered sailing trip off Australia reading books rather than at the office; back at headquarters, Steve Ballmer just taped the newspaper clipping to the front door, smiles and handshakes but no party, because a new version of the operating system was already in development.
