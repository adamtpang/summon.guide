# Tortured Into Greatness: The Life of Andre Agassi

> **Founder principle (Andre Agassi):** When you are performing a mission someone else chose for you, survive it by building your own internal control system: repeated self-talk, honest diagnosis after every loss, and a small circle of people willing to tell you hard truths instead of flattering you. Achievement built on a goal that was never truly yours will feel empty until you find a purpose bigger than winning itself.

*David Senra on Andre Agassi, reading from Open. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d_Bv59Rxo6w) · 61 min · published 2026-02-04.*

## Key lessons

- **Talk to yourself out loud as an active performance tool.** Agassi describes repeating phrases to himself in the shower "until I believe them," and during matches saying his mantra "control what you can control" aloud because hearing the words made him feel braver than simply thinking them.
- **Diagnose losses honestly instead of reaching for excuses.** Rather than blaming bad calls or an off day, Agassi trained himself to analyze exactly which weaknesses cost him a match, a habit that let him identify real gaps to close instead of protecting his ego with explanations.
- **Chase consistency over perfection.** His coach Brad Gilbert's core diagnosis was that perfectionism, trying to hit a winner on every ball, was actively losing him matches he could win by simply staying steady and attacking an opponent's specific weaknesses instead of chasing his own flawless shot.
- **A full collapse sometimes requires an outside voice forcing a full rebuild.** After falling to the 141st ranking while using drugs and skipping training, it took Gilbert bluntly refusing to let him coast on former talent, insisting on a complete overhaul from the body up, before Agassi committed to climbing back from the bottom of the sport.
- **An achievement built on someone else's goal will not satisfy you once you reach it.** Reaching the number one ranking left him feeling nothing, because he realized he had never actually wanted the ranking himself, it was a goal his father had set for him before he was born.
- **Real motivation often comes from something bigger than your own scoreboard.** He describes his deepest satisfaction coming not from titles but from quietly setting aside stock for a restaurant manager's children's education, and from playing for Team USA at the Olympics, where the swelling emotion he felt had nothing to do with himself.
- **Keep a small circle who will tell you the truth, not the crowd who only reacts to your image.** His trainer Gil, his friend and pastor JP, and his coach Brad Gilbert each played a distinct role as honest counsel, in contrast to sportswriters and strangers whose praise or criticism he could not stop reading and letting distort how he felt about himself.
- **Fear that never fully goes away can still be managed rather than eliminated.** When Agassi asked whether the fear before a match ever disappears, JP told him fear was his fire and that he would not want to see it go out completely, reframing an unwanted feeling as fuel instead of a problem to solve.
