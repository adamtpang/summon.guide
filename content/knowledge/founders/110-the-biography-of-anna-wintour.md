# The Biography of Anna Wintour

> **Founder principle (Anna Wintour):** Cap every review inside a fixed, short time window so people are forced to arrive with the answer, not a discussion. Demand to see the physical work before it runs, and kill anything short of excellent without explanation, so the standard is never in doubt. Treat every party, every dollar of mentorship, and every relationship as an investment in influence rather than a social obligation, and use that influence to build assets, a fund, a gala, a network of loyal people, beyond the one title you were actually hired for.

*David Senra on Anna Wintour, reading from Anna: The Biography by Amy Odell, and a Financial Times interview with Wintour. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=npZmuHLtpd4) · 66 min · published 2024-04-13.*

## Key lessons

- **Cap every review inside a fixed, short time window so people arrive with an answer, not a discussion.** Wintour's meetings run about two minutes, the first sixty seconds guaranteed and the second a courtesy; her mentor Alexander Liberman similarly kept meetings under five minutes with a desk buzzer to end them, in sharp contrast to her predecessor's eight-to-ten-hour clothing selection meetings, which ended in vague, repeated requests for more without ever defining what more meant.
- **Require the physical work in front of you before you approve anything, and be willing to kill it after the money is spent.** Wintour had racks of clothing wheeled into her office before any shoot, reviewed every photo from every shoot herself, and killed entire six-figure stories without explanation when the result fell short, which taught every editor and photographer that only excellent work would run.
- **Replace open-ended feedback with a single, unambiguous mark of approval.** Wintour's final sign-off on a story became office shorthand staff checked for by name, the same discipline she applied to marking up copy with single-word responses instead of long notes, so no one had to guess whether something had cleared.
- **Build the direct relationship with whoever actually owns the decision, regardless of the org chart.** Wintour cultivated a close relationship with Conde Nast owner Si Newhouse while her predecessor avoided him; when the company needed one trusted voice on Vogue's direction, she was already it, and her predecessor learned she had been fired from a television rumor instead of a conversation.
- **Treat your social calendar as a work function, not a break from it.** Wintour rarely drank and left events early, but attended constantly, using parties and dinners as reconnaissance on the industry; she later formalized that instinct into the Met Gala, an invite-only event where she personally approves every guest, every seating placement, and even who is allowed to bring guests, turning a party into a controlled business gathering.
- **Fund and mentor the next generation before you need anything from them, then collect the relationship later.** Through the CFDA/Vogue Fashion Fund, Wintour gives emerging designers cash and specific tactical advice, once telling a hot young designer to sell out of an unfinished space rather than spend eight months building a proper store; the designer who ignored that advice named the delay as one of the reasons his brand later failed.
- **When you take over a demoralized or complacent team, replace people fast and make the change visible immediately.** Sent to run British Vogue in 1985, and again promoted to oversee every Conde Nast title in 2020, Wintour fired much of the existing staff right away, reasoning that everyone needed to feel "it was a different day."
- **State what you want out loud, even to the person currently holding the job.** Early in her career, when the sitting editor-in-chief of Vogue asked what position Wintour actually wanted, she answered "yours" and ended the meeting there; she treated every job she held as a platform toward the next one rather than a place to settle.
- **Set a personal operating rhythm strict enough that it becomes the standard everyone around you is measured against.** Up at 5am and exercising by 5:30, arriving to issue rapid instructions with no greeting or small talk, Wintour ran a culture where complaint was pointless and self-sufficiency was assumed, and where the two traits she rewarded above all else were speed and genuine passion for the work.
