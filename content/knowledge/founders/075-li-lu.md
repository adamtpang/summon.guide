# Li Lu

> **Founder principle (Li Lu):** Treat accurate, complete information as a survival skill worth building from childhood, not a professional technique picked up later. Test claims yourself instead of accepting what the group repeats, ask relentless follow-up questions when you lack direct access to something, and read everything within reach as if your future depends on it. For Li Lu, formed under extreme deprivation and instability in Cultural Revolution China, that habit carried directly into how he researches companies today.

*David Senra on Li Lu, reading from Moving the Mountain: My Life in China from the Cultural Revolution to Tiananmen Square by Li Lu. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CslLl1fVK8U) · 34 min · published 2024-09-05.*

## Key lessons

- **Test a claim yourself instead of accepting what everyone else repeats.** At his preschool other children warned that the lizards swarming the grounds were poisonous, but Li Lu let them crawl over him, found nothing happened, and traced his later confidence directly to that first refusal to accept secondhand fear as fact.
- **Extract information through relentless, detailed questioning when you have no direct access.** Cut off from his family and the outside world, he interrogated other children about their weekend visits home in exhaustive detail, a habit he credits with building the skills in narration, imagination, and interviewing that he had no reason yet to think he would ever need.
- **Make reading the mechanism for escaping your circumstances.** He describes books as the way he could reach every corner of the world from inside a life with no money, no stable home, and no outside contact, and he organized a secret reading group at school specifically because questioning the official story was dangerous.
- **Design a punishing personal schedule when one exam is your only way out.** Three months before the national college entrance exam he mapped his days from 4:45 a.m. through midnight in defined study, review, and exercise blocks, and placed fifth in his school as a direct result.
- **Treat information as complete only when it actually is, not merely available.** The demand for accurate and complete information followed him into investing, where he has gone as far as attending a CEO's church or talking with their neighbors rather than settle for a surface-level read on management.
- **Build self-reliance from repeated loss instead of waiting for stability.** He was placed with at least nine different families before age nine, lost his adoptive father in a mining accident and his adoptive family in the Tangshan earthquake, and each time what carried forward was not possessions but the habits of reading, questioning, and self-sufficiency he had already built.
- **Seek out the discarded, unofficial version of the story.** Finding a hidden suitcase of his father's pre-revolution photographs, and realizing how different that history was from what he had been told, taught him early that the official account and the real one were often different things.
- **Once given real access, use it completely.** The first time he saw a real school library he called it astonishing, and his response was total, reading all day, a habit that never left him and that still shows decades later when he lectures Columbia Business School MBA students on how unprepared they are by comparison.
