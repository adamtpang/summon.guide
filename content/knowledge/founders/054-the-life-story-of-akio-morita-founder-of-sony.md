# The Life Story of Akio Morita: Founder of Sony

> **Founder principle (Akio Morita):** Engineering a new capability is only part of creating a market. Sony also had to find people who needed that capability, teach them how to use it, and make the experience worthy of a premium price. Morita joined technical ambition to direct customer contact and a willingness to fund a reputation over decades.

*David Senra on Akio Morita, reading from Made in Japan: Akio Morita and Sony. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ynlY9rIb7O8) · 69 min · published 2025-05-06.*

## Key lessons

- **Find the customer whose problem makes your invention worth buying.** Sony's early tape recorder attracted curiosity without enough purchases when demonstrated to ordinary consumers. Morita recognized that Japanese courts faced a shortage of stenographers, and a demonstration to the Supreme Court produced an immediate order for twenty machines. Define the practical task and buyer before concluding that a technically strong product has no demand.
- **Use a small contract to earn access to larger opportunities.** Sony built broadcasting equipment in premises so primitive that an American officer worried about fire. The delivered equipment impressed him and helped establish trust for further work. That relationship also exposed the founders to a tape recorder, showing how excellent execution on today's assignment can reveal tomorrow's product.
- **Remove features that obstruct the customer's main use.** Ibuka wanted music while traveling without the weight of his portable recorder or disturbing other people. Morita asked engineers to remove the recording circuit and speaker and use lightweight headphones. The Walkman followed from prioritizing listening and portability, even when colleagues considered the missing recording function a reason customers would reject it.
- **Protect the brand when a large order points in the wrong direction.** Bulova offered to buy 100,000 radios if its own name replaced Sony's. Morita refused despite the order's importance to the young company's finances because he wanted Sony's reputation to accumulate with every sale. Compare a proposed deal with the asset you intend to own in fifty years, as well as the cash it brings today.
- **Control the explanation of an unfamiliar product.** Morita believed that several layers of distributors weakened communication between Sony and the people using its inventions. He developed direct outlets and showrooms where visitors could try products, first in Tokyo's Ginza district and later on Fifth Avenue. For a product customers must experience to understand, distribution is also part of teaching its value.
- **Learn a strategically important market through daily life.** Morita moved his family to the United States because he believed Sony's future depended on understanding American customers. Living there exposed him to habits that a sequence of sales visits would miss. Match the depth of your market knowledge to the importance of the decisions you are making about that market.
- **Give useful critics a place inside the company.** Sony paid music student Norio Ohga to criticize its early recorder because his demanding standards revealed real weaknesses. Morita also introduced confidential applications for internal jobs after hearing how a poor supervisor could trap an otherwise enthusiastic employee. Seek product criticism deliberately and create ways for employees to reveal management problems without depending on the manager being criticized.
- **Fund customer education over the life of the relationship.** Morita challenged a Sony executive who resisted spending on a new product because the immediate campaign might lose money. He judged the investment partly by the customers it could develop over five or ten years. Review whether a reported profit reflects a stronger business or simply reduced investment in future demand.
- **Put scarce resources into the product and the work that makes it.** Morita objected to comfortable executive offices taking priority over factory conditions and described studying even screwdriver use and soldering temperatures. Sony also examined energy consumption in its factories, offices, and products. Make resourcefulness concrete through small process decisions, rather than treating low spending alone as proof of discipline.
