# The Making of Winston Churchill Part 1

> **Founder principle (Winston Churchill):** Decide you are destined for something great long before you have any proof of it, then deliberately seek out the danger and distinction other people avoid. Treat public failure as fuel rather than a verdict, and when you have no resources of your own, be willing to put your fate completely in the hands of people brave enough to help you.

*David Senra on Winston Churchill, reading from Hero of the Empire: The Boer War, a Daring Escape, and the Making of Winston Churchill. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QoEoY0AOFqw) · 48 min · published 2024-04-06.*

## Key lessons

- **Decide you are destined for greatness before you have any evidence for it.** By 24, without a single major achievement to his name yet, Churchill was already certain he would one day become prime minister; the conviction came first, and the actions, seeking out war after war for personal distinction, followed from it rather than the other way around.
- **Deliberately seek out personal danger as the fastest route to public distinction.** He talked his way into postings across three continents as a young man, and a bullet killed the horse standing beside him in one campaign, because he understood that obscure colonial conflicts offered an ordinary man an irresistible shortcut to glory that a quiet career did not.
- **Never wait for an invitation, and use every relationship you have to get where you want to go.** Churchill described himself as needing no prodding at all, only a chance, saying flatly "I am a prod," and he pulled every family and social connection available to secure postings that were never offered to him freely.
- **Treat public speaking as a trained skill, not a gift, and rehearse until it looks effortless.** Public speaking did not come naturally to him even as a young man, so he wrote and rewrote every line in advance and rehearsed it extensively, a habit that made even his supposedly spontaneous remarks sound fully composed.
- **Let a public failure harden your resolve instead of ending your run.** He lost his first election at 24 and was mocked by a critic as "a young man of promises" rather than accomplishments; rather than retreat, he treated the humiliation as more fuel and kept pushing toward the same goal.
- **When you have no resources of your own, the plan has to run through other people's courage.** His escape from a prisoner of war camp depended entirely on strangers: a mine manager hid him down a pitch dark mineshaft and then in a secret office space, and a shopkeeper personally rode beside him for three days in a train car, bribing armed guards at every stop at risk of his own life.
- **Commit fully to your own persona, even when it is reckless.** Before escaping, he left a taunting letter for the enemy's Secretary of State for War on his own bed so it would be found, a stunt that triggered a manhunt with thousands of wanted posters and offered no real upside beyond having the last word.
- **Read for hours every day as a substitute for the belonging you do not have.** Disliked at school and often at the bottom of his class, with parents who gave him little attention, he found his real companionship in books on history, philosophy, and economics, reading four to five hours a day, a habit that outlasted his entire childhood.
- **Once the stunt succeeds, follow the character all the way through to the end.** Still not technically safe after crossing into neutral territory, guided the whole way by his own maxim of "always more audacity," he fired his revolver into the air and shouted with joy; reaching the British consulate filthy and exhausted, he still angrily demanded to see the consul general by name rather than wait quietly to be noticed.
