# I had dinner with John Mackey: Founder of Whole Foods

> **Founder principle (John Mackey):** Build alliances with competitors before you have the capital to beat them, and grow by acquiring the network of peers you already know rather than fighting them for share for decades. Treat outside investors as temporary passengers, useful for the ride but never allowed to grab the wheel, and win the category not with the single best store but with more ambition, more frugality, and more patience than anyone else in the business.

*David Senra on John Mackey, reading from The Whole Story: Adventures in Love, Life, and Capitalism by John Mackey and drawing on two days of in-person conversation with Mackey. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SqMI-RugFk0) · 92 min · published 2024-08-16.*

## Key lessons

- **Turn competitors into allies before you have the capital to beat them outright.** With one small store against rivals who had five and better wholesale pricing, Mackey approached two owners of a rival natural food store and proposed combining their buying power into a shared distribution company; that alliance became the founding merger that created Whole Foods.
- **Size your ambition to the market's real scale, not the scale you started at.** Reading that a Los Angeles competitor was doing about $100,000 a week against his own $8,000 to $10,000, Mackey concluded that size mattered in retail and that staying a small vegetarian shop would let the moment pass, and he shifted his full attention to expansion within the same year.
- **When regulation would kill the business before it is born, do the necessary work first and seek permission after.** Facing months of required inspections his fledgling store could not survive financially, Mackey took an older entrepreneur's advice to build out the store overnight while the city inspectors were asleep, then secured the permits afterward.
- **Grow overwhelmingly through the informal network of peers you already know, and act on a rare opportunity even when you are not ready for it.** Mackey toured natural food stores across the country building relationships with owners who were technically competitors, then bought most of them one by one; when a superior regional chain became available before Whole Foods had digested its prior acquisitions, he pushed the board to buy anyway, arguing the opportunity would not come again.
- **Take outside capital only when it buys speed you cannot generate yourself, and treat it as temporary.** Mackey described his venture investors as "hitchhikers with credit cards," willing to help pay for gas as long as the car was headed where they wanted but ready to grab the wheel if the company drifted, and he pushed for an IPO specifically to end their leverage over the board.
- **When a co-CEO standoff drags on because the other person keeps offering to resign out of loyalty, accept the offer once instead of continuing to decline it.** Reading a biography of Lincoln during a prolonged conflict with his co-CEO, Mackey borrowed Lincoln's own resolution of a nearly identical standoff with a cabinet rival, accepted the resignation that had already been offered twice, and ended the impasse.
- **Move first to find your own buyer the moment an activist investor starts accumulating stock, rather than wait for a board vote you might lose.** When an activist fund disclosed an 8.8 percent stake in 2017 and a fellow investor warned that even a friendly looking board would likely still vote him out, Mackey proactively approached Amazon rather than wait to be forced into a worse outcome.
- **The category is won by discipline, not by having the best product.** Mackey's own explanation for why Whole Foods, not a higher quality regional rival, became the dominant national natural food company: greater ambition, a longer time horizon, frugal operations, and comfort with profit, not superior stores.
