# Napoleon's Maxims and Strategy

> **Founder principle (Napoleon):** Napoleon's maxims describe a single discipline applied to war that translates directly to running an organization: think exhaustively about every contingency before you commit, then commit completely and do not stop until the objective is taken. Mass every available resource behind one objective instead of spreading them thin, move faster than an opponent can react, and never divide command, because one decisive chief beats a committee every time.

*David Senra on Napoleon, reading from Roots of Strategy (the Military Maxims of Napoleon section) and Napoleon and Modern War, an annotated 1949 edition of Napoleon's own maxims. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZaPyk33pRM0) · 61 min · published 2024-04-24.*

## Key lessons

- **Think through every contingency before committing, then commit without turning back.** Napoleon's own discipline was to plan exhaustively and then hold the offensive "to the last extremity" once begun, a two step process of thorough thought followed by total commitment, not thorough thought as a substitute for commitment.
- **Rehearse your own vulnerabilities before the market finds them.** Napoleon said a commander should ask himself several times a day what he would do if the enemy appeared to his front, his right, or his left; if he could not answer immediately, his position had a real flaw that needed fixing before someone else exposed it.
- **Speed compounds; slow decisions cost you your best people and your position.** Napoleon's maxims return to rapid movement more than almost any other idea, and David ties this directly to the modern version of the same warning, that a slow pace of decision making drives talented people out of an organization.
- **Strip out anything that slows communication down.** Napoleon issued orders that were only a few sentences long on purpose, on the reasoning that long orders which take time to write, read, and understand are the "enemies of speed," and that a smaller or weaker force only survives by moving faster than a larger one.
- **Concentrate everything on one objective instead of spreading resources thin.** The maxims repeat the same instruction in different forms: collect all your strength and leave none unemployed, mass fire on a single point, and never divide your attention, because a battalion held in reserve can decide the entire outcome.
- **Never divide command.** Unity of command is the maxim Napoleon returns to most directly: one army, acting on one line, led by one chief, because divided authority produces conflicting opinions and delay, and a single decision maker moves without needing to confer.
- **Control the story told about you and your organization, because public opinion decides more than the facts do.** Napoleon treated shaping perception, both the enemy's and his own side's, as a deliberate discipline, including one documented case of praising a genuinely weak opposing general specifically to keep that general at his post.
- **Guard against the comfort that follows success.** Napoleon avoided quartering troops too long in comfortable surroundings because he believed prolonged ease eroded discipline, the same cycle David draws out in advertising, where a hungry young agency eventually grows rich, complacent, and vulnerable to whoever is hungrier next.
- **Study the people who came before you, and reread them rather than read them once.** Napoleon's own instruction was to study the campaigns of Alexander, Hannibal, Caesar, and Frederick the Great repeatedly, on the reasoning that genuinely great practitioners are extremely rare, so their accumulated judgment is worth returning to again and again.
