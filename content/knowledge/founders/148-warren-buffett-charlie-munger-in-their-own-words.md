# Warren Buffett & Charlie Munger In Their Own Words

> **Founder principle (Warren Buffett and Charlie Munger):** They run their partnership on rationality: mute the outside world, guard unstructured time to think, and invert every problem by studying what causes failure before deciding what to pursue. Both treat leverage and debt as the one mistake that can turn a lifetime of good decisions into ruin, and both deliberately choose their heroes, living or dead, then copy exactly what made those heroes work.

*David Senra on Warren Buffett and Charlie Munger, reading from All I Want to Know Is Where I'm Going to Die So I'll Never Go There: Buffett and Munger, A Study in Simplicity and Uncommon Common Sense by Peter Bevelin. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VG3MZHZazeI) · 71 min · published 2024-02-04.*

## Key lessons

- **Charlie Munger: invert the problem before you try to solve it.** His lifelong method is to study how things fail or how businesses decline first, then build toward the opposite, rather than starting only from what success is supposed to look like.
- **Warren Buffett: guard your calendar like a scarce asset.** He kept a standing letter to the managers of Berkshire's dozens of subsidiary businesses instructing them to turn down speaking requests, donation asks, and favors made in his name on his behalf, and to say no immediately rather than checking with him first.
- **Charlie Munger: name the exact ways smart people go broke.** He grouped them as liquor, ladies, and leverage, and Buffett's own explanation for why leverage is the worst of the three is that a long run of good positive results can be erased entirely by one multiplication against a loss.
- **Warren Buffett: ignore forecasters and stay fixed on business quality and price.** He described paying no attention to macroeconomic or market predictions across five decades of partnership with Munger, arguing that forecasters can entertain you but have never once made anyone money.
- **Warren Buffett: choose your heroes on purpose, then study exactly what made them work.** He kept a physical reminder of that idea on his own desk for years, and Munger's addition is that you should not limit the search to the living, since history holds models nobody alive can outbid.
- **Warren Buffett: hitch yourself to winners instead of trying to be indispensable yourself.** He compared his own approach to a bat boy who kept switching to whichever team was about to win a championship, arguing that how well you carry out a small role matters less than who you attach yourself to.
- **Charlie Munger: go where the competition is weak.** He credited much of his own success to seeking out fields with light competition rather than fighting for scraps in crowded ones, joking that his real advantage was a long supply of rivals making the same mistakes over and over.
- **Warren Buffett: earn a reputation as the buyer sellers actually want.** Berkshire's long stated goal was to be the first call for family owned businesses, and Buffett tied that reputation directly to keeping promises, avoiding loading up acquired companies with debt, and granting real autonomy to the managers who stayed on.
