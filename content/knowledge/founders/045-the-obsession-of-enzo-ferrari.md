# The Obsession of Enzo Ferrari

> **Founder principle (Enzo Ferrari):** Pick one all-consuming mission and let nothing else compete for your attention for the rest of your life. Manufacture desire through scarcity and total emotional control rather than open promotion, let winning itself do the marketing, and treat every setback as the better teacher, since a loss tells you exactly what to fix while a win leaves you unsure what actually worked.

*David Senra on Enzo Ferrari, reading from the New Yorker profile "The Terrible Joys of Enzo Ferrari," The Story of Ferrari, Enzo Ferrari by Luca Dal Monte, Enzo Ferrari by Brock Yates, and Go Like Hell. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-quUT9uGApo) · 54 min · published 2025-07-31.*

## Key lessons

- **Pick one singular, lifelong mission and let nothing else compete for it.** From the first race he watched with his father at age 10, hardly a day passed for 60 years when winning under his own name wasn't foremost in his mind, and he worked at it until his body gave out at 90.
- **Turn early rejection into permanent fuel.** Fiat turned him down as a penniless young man with no family left; he never let go of that slight, and roughly 50 years later Fiat came to him instead, buying half of the company he had built without them.
- **Manufacture scarcity on purpose.** He kept a lot of unsold Ferraris hidden from view and told a wealthy would-be buyer he'd have to wait months, insisting "a Ferrari must be desired," since visibly available inventory kills the exact desire that sells the car.
- **Let winning do the marketing instead of advertising.** He believed success on the track, not conventional promotion, was what brought wealthy customers to his door, so building fast cars and selling them were never treated as separate activities.
- **Manage proud, egocentric talent by studying men as closely as engines.** He called himself "an agitator of men" rather than a designer or inventor, and considered his real gift the ability to stir competitive energy out of highly skilled people with outsized egos.
- **Treat losing as the more useful teacher.** He noticed the workshop was calmer after a defeat than after a win, since a loss tells you precisely what needs fixing while a win leaves you unsure what actually worked.
- **Keep headquarters small and stay physically close to the work.** About 600 workers turned out roughly two cars a day by hand, and Ferrari could survey the entire operation just by walking a few yards, while refusing travel, air flights, and nearly all life outside his home region.
- **Never look backward, no matter how much you've already achieved.** His motto was "my next car is best," he disliked monuments and rejected nostalgia about his own record, and into his 80s he still spoke of the car and the victory he hadn't yet achieved.
- **Protect the mystique through total emotional control.** Dark sunglasses that hid his eyes, a face described as a mask, and a reluctance to share credit with his own winning drivers all served the same purpose: the myth of the man was itself part of what made the cars desirable.
