# Red Bull's Billionaire Maniac Founder: Dietrich Mateschitz

> **Founder principle (Dietrich Mateschitz):** Protect the brand's message with near total secrecy and let it eclipse the founder's own persona. Own the platforms you market through instead of renting space on them, and reject profit maximization as the point of the business in favor of independence, freedom, and enjoyment of the work.

*David Senra on Dietrich Mateschitz, reading from an untranslated German biography of Mateschitz and the Bloomberg Businessweek profile 'Red Bull's Billionaire Maniac'. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=798LBCW-u2M) · 68 min · published 2024-04-20.*

## Key lessons

- **Guard the brand's message with the same intensity you guard the product.** Mateschitz ran what insiders called a press prevention office, not a public relations department, whose job was to say nothing and explain why the boss was unavailable, granted interviews to only a small set of hand picked publications, and reportedly bought into an Austrian gossip magazine for the simple reason that owning it was the surest way to keep himself out of its pages.
- **Put the brand's face on everything and the founder's face on nothing.** His own stated philosophy was that Red Bull, not he, belonged on the shelf for sale, so nearly every dollar of promotion went to the brand and almost none to his own persona, even as he became one of the richest people in Austria.
- **Do not sponsor a sport, own a piece of it.** Where most companies pay for logo space, Mateschitz bought soccer clubs outright and renamed them Red Bull, and built Formula 1 teams from the ground up specifically so the company would have full operating control inside the sport rather than being just another logo pasted on someone else's car.
- **Spend on stunts that are too extreme to attend but too strange to ignore.** About half of Red Bull's marketing budget funded events like running in the Himalayas, skydiving the English Channel, or mountain biking inside a mine, few people ever saw them live, but their sheer extravagance pulled in free coverage from outlets Red Bull never had to pay.
- **Build sponsorships on personal trust, not paperwork.** Negotiations with the athletes he backed often gave way to hours spent together off the clock, including the time he handed a windsurfer he had just met the keys to his own Ferrari for a mountain drive, letting the relationship come first and the sponsorship deal follow from it.
- **Make the physical product itself part of the marketing.** He shaped the Red Bull can tall and slim specifically so it looked unlike anything else on a shelf of similar cans, and carried that same design discipline absurdly far, down to cupholders sized for that one can inside his own private aircraft.
- **Reject the standard definition of what a company is for.** He called the idea that maximizing profit is a company's primary goal simply incorrect and said money was never his main motivation, naming independence, freedom over his own time, and enjoyment of the work as the real goals, which is also the reason he gave for ruling out an IPO or a sale for the rest of his life.
- **Research a brand new category by living inside it yourself before spending a cent on marketing.** Before committing to a formula or a price, he organized what he called energy drink parties, gathering competing tonics from across Asia and having his own circle taste and compare them, so the eventual positioning came from direct experience rather than secondhand research.
- **Cut a failed line instantly and put everything behind the one thing that is working.** An early lemonade style extension called the Red Rooster flopped shortly after launch, and rather than iterate on it he abandoned every side product and concentrated all future spending on the single drink that had already proven itself, a discipline he kept for the rest of his career.
