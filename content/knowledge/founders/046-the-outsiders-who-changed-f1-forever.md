# The Outsiders Who Changed F1 Forever

> **Founder principle (F1's outsiders):** Those on the margins of an industry often come to control its center. Question every convention as negotiable, whether it's engine weight, team secrecy, or how a paddock is supposed to look, and treat the resulting disruption as the actual unfair advantage rather than a side effect of being new.

*David Senra on Colin Chapman, Bernie Ecclestone, and Dietrich Mateschitz, reading from The Formula by Joshua Robinson and Jonathan Clegg. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NQpo80z30gY) · 61 min · published 2025-07-22.*

## Key lessons

- **Subtract weight before you add power, and question every rule as negotiable.** Chapman's mantra was that adding power makes a car fast on the straights while subtracting weight makes it faster everywhere, and he thought the entire F1 rulebook should fit on the back of an envelope.
- **Turn your team's biggest cost into new revenue instead of just cutting it.** Chronically short on cash, Chapman sold the sport's first sponsored paint job, a decision that opened four decades of tobacco sponsorship pouring billions into the sport.
- **Recognize that rivals on the track are partners off it.** Ecclestone saw that F1 teams competed for only a few hours a dozen times a year but were effectively business partners the rest of the time, and organized them into a single bargaining bloc when no other owner wanted the job.
- **Take on all the risk yourself in exchange for a small guaranteed cut of everything.** Ecclestone offered to run the teams' race logistics for a fee that other owners scoffed at, then built decades of leverage and fortune from that one unglamorous role.
- **Never give away future rights for a small amount of cash today.** Ecclestone locked up broadcast rights when they were worth almost nothing, while the sport's own governing body later sold its remaining 30 percent stake in those same rights for just $9 million, a decision it would regret for decades.
- **Impose order on a chaotic market, then charge for the certainty you created.** Ecclestone required broadcasters to air every race instead of cherry-picking the popular ones and moved every Grand Prix to a fixed start time, professionalizing a fractured sport into one with a billion-plus viewers.
- **Buy into an old, established sport and refuse to act like the incumbents.** Mateschitz bought a failing F1 team for one pound sterling, handed it to a 31-year-old team principal with no racing background, and told him "we're not going to be corporate."
- **Overpay for irreplaceable talent without hesitation.** Red Bull paid designer Adrian Newey more than Mateschitz initially thought reasonable, but the deciding question was simply what a faster lap time was worth, since it's nearly impossible to truly overpay for the right person.
- **Replace closed, secretive team spaces with radical openness once you have nothing to lose by being different.** Red Bull built a glass-and-steel "energy station" in the paddock and threw its doors open to every mechanic, driver, and engineer, breaking the sport's tradition of guarded, sponsor-only enclaves.
