# Mr Beast's Leaked Memo

> **Founder principle (Jimmy Donaldson):** Run the operation as a single-goal, results-only culture: state one goal for the whole company, judge everyone strictly by outcomes rather than hours worked, and only greenlight work that nobody else could produce. Tell the truth bluntly rather than softening it to be liked, push past a single no, and report bad news before good news so problems surface while they are still cheap to fix. Pay the people who prove themselves the best in the world at their job far above market rate instead of keeping the upside for yourself.

## Key lessons

- **State one goal for the whole organization and use it as the filter for every decision.** The memo says the company's only goal is to make the best YouTube videos possible, not the best-produced, funniest, or best-looking ones, and warns that after six months in the weeds people forget this and need to be pulled back to it.
- **Judge people by the result, not the hours they logged.** The memo states plainly that the company is results-based and that the amount of hours worked is irrelevant, illustrated by one staffer solving in 30 minutes a problem a team of five had failed to solve in a week because he understood the whole business.
- **Sort people into A, B, and C players and remove the C players fast.** Jimmy writes that there is only room for A players, that B players are new hires who need training into A players, and that C players are poisonous and should be moved to a different company immediately.
- **Only build what nobody else can build.** The memo's repeated instruction is to design segments only Mr Beast can do, illustrated by paying someone $500,000 to live in a circle in a field, then craning the house in during the first thirty seconds of the video purely for the effect no other channel could match.
- **Assign the bottleneck by name and check in on it daily.** The memo describes telling a specific team member, by name, that they are the bottleneck on a 45-day production and why, then confirming a delivery date and checking in on it every single day rather than setting a reminder and walking away.
- **Report the bad news before the good news.** The memo instructs staff to say the negatives first because good news takes care of itself, and to never take a supplier's answer at face value, such as investigating why an eleventh vendor can mysteriously produce 10,000 pillows when ten others could not.
- **Use the highest form of communication the situation calls for.** The memo ranks email below text below phone below face to face, states that written communication does not constitute communication unless the recipient confirms they read it, and requires a call or in-person response once a video is a week out and in trouble.
- **Push past a single no instead of accepting it as final.** One example in the memo is calling other employees, their bosses, and even a company's social media team before accepting that filming at a location is impossible, then trying other locations rather than stopping at the first refusal.
- **Pay for proven talent instead of hoarding the company's winnings.** The memo states that Jimmy has no interest in hoarding money and pays top performers heavily once he judges them the best in the world at their job, closing with a promise of unlimited growth for anyone who makes themselves essential to the company.
