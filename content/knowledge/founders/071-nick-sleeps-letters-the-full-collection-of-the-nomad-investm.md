# Nick Sleep's Letters: The Full Collection of the Nomad Investment Partnership Letters

> **Founder principle (Nick Sleep):** A business can strengthen its position by returning efficiency gains to customers through lower prices. Nomad's letters trace how studying that mechanism changed both its investments and its own operating choices. The discipline was to investigate the underlying business, let the evidence change the portfolio, and secure the patience to remain involved through uncertainty.

*David Senra on Nick Sleep and his investment partner, reading from The Nomad Investment Partnership Letters, with supporting passages and comparisons from other sources. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y3-86dXpg4) · 51 min · published 2024-10-15.*

## Key lessons

- **Make the required time horizon part of the agreement with your backers.** Sleep repeatedly told partners that patience across the investor base was central to Nomad's approach. Calling it a partnership expressed a relationship different from trading the next market move. Explain how the strategy is expected to work before a difficult period tests everyone's commitment.
- **Identify the economic engine before extrapolating the reported results.** Nomad studied Costco's operating costs, restricted product selection, supplier terms, and handling of scale benefits. Passing savings to customers encouraged further demand and reinforced purchasing advantages. Ask which recurring behavior produces the numbers, and whether growth strengthens that behavior.
- **Protect the customer promise when breaking it would be profitable today.** A Costco buyer proposed taking extra margin on an unusually favorable jeans purchase. Jim Sinegal insisted on the usual markup because making an exception could become a habit. Translate the company's promise into a rule that still applies when customers would struggle to detect a deviation.
- **Give long-term judgments evidence without pretending they are exact forecasts.** The Bezos passages distinguish measurable short-term price effects from the harder question of what years of lower prices and convenience create. Sleep used that distinction to assess Amazon's reinvestment. State the expected mechanism, its uncertainties, and the time needed to observe it rather than presenting an immediate margin calculation as the whole answer.
- **Compare what you own with what your research says you should own.** Nomad kept a list of unusually capable, honest capital allocators and noticed that its actual portfolio did not match that desired destination. The letters show a gradual change in thinking and allocation rather than instant certainty. Review the gap between stated conviction and the resources committed to it.
- **Treat refusing expansion as a capital-allocation decision.** Sleep pointed to Stagecoach's retrenchment and National Indemnity's willingness to write less insurance when pricing was poor. Growth could destroy value if the additional business had weak economics. Before funding another venture, compare its return with improving, retaining, or shrinking the existing operation.
- **Review operating resilience separately from a falling market price.** During the financial crisis, the letters compared Amazon's customer activity and cost position with the decline in its shares and weakness elsewhere in retail. The question was whether the business mechanism remained intact. Use observable operating evidence to revisit a thesis rather than assuming a price move answers that question by itself.
- **Make doing nothing an informed choice with an opportunity cost.** Nomad compared its holdings with extensive research into alternatives and often preferred what it already owned. Sleep also used past sales of long-lived compounders to illustrate mistakes of omission. The lesson is disciplined comparison and continuing scrutiny, not a claim that every founder-led business should be held indefinitely or concentrated without limits.
