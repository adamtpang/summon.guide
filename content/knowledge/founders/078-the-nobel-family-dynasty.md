# The Nobel Family Dynasty

> **Founder principle (Ludwig Nobel):** Learn explicitly from a parent's failure and build the opposite discipline into the business you start: never depend on one customer, control every link in the chain from invention to distribution, and treat the people who work for you as an asset worth investing in. Temperament matters as much as strategy; Ludwig ran a hands on, competitive business while his brother Alfred ran a delegated monopoly, and both approaches worked because each was authentic to the man running it.

*David Senra on Ludwig Nobel and the Nobel family, reading from The Russian Rockefellers: The Saga of the Nobel Family and the Russian Oil Industry by Robert Tolf. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0eS7Z5te4EU) · 59 min · published 2024-08-19.*

## Key lessons

- **Learn explicitly from a parent's single-customer failure, and build the opposite discipline into the business you start.** Emanuel Nobel Sr was a gifted inventor whose entire arms business depended on one customer, the Tsar's war ministry; when Nicholas I died and Alexander II's ministers ignored the old contracts, decades of expansion collapsed into a second bankruptcy, and his sons Ludwig and Alfred both deliberately built businesses that never again depended on a single buyer.
- **Add a consumer product to insulate the business from any one customer, even a lucrative one.** Even while more than a third of the Russian state budget was going to the military, Ludwig invented the Nobel wheel, a carriage wheel built to survive Russia's terrible roads, won a market monopoly with it, and gave the company a revenue stream the war ministry could never take away.
- **Enter a market of second rate competitors and simply out build them on standards.** When Ludwig moved into Baku's oil fields in 1876, the roughly 140 existing refineries were producing what the market itself dismissed as sludge; he imposed engineering rigor on every stage from drilling to retail, and within two years his all Swedish refinery was producing the highest grade kerosene in Russia.
- **Control the entire chain from invention to the customer's hands instead of delegating any piece of it.** Ludwig personally built the pipelines, the dockside terminals, the tanker fleet, the barges, the rail cars, and the retail outlets, acting simultaneously as president, chief engineer, sales manager, and board chairman of a business he created from nothing in ten years.
- **When the market calls your idea too risky and refuses to fund it, build it yourself, then give the idea away.** Ludwig's peers dismissed the ocean going oil tanker as never attempted before and refused to support it, so he built it himself, then declined to patent the design at all, sharing pipeline and tanker techniques with the wider industry on the belief that a strong idea, left alone to develop, keeps expanding well past its original scope.
- **Treat worker welfare as a productivity investment, not a cost, decades before that was standard practice.** Ludwig built housing for his employees, personally subsidized a savings bank for them, refused to hire children, cut the workday from fourteen hours to ten and a half, started the first profit sharing plan in Russia, and ran free education courses, on the conviction that increased care for employees produced increased output.
- **Seize the opportunity actually in front of you, even when it means abandoning the assignment you were given.** Sent with 25,000 rubles to buy walnut wood for rifle stocks, Robert Nobel instead spent every ruble on a small refinery in Baku without asking permission first, a single unauthorized pivot that redirected the entire family fortune from weapons manufacturing into what became the Russian oil industry.
- **Protect what the family built even against the family itself, and even against a king.** When other Nobel relatives tried to overturn Alfred's will, which funded the Nobel Prize, to keep his fortune inside the family, Ludwig's son Emanuel refused to join them and held his position even when Sweden's king personally pressured him to back down.
- **Read the political ground honestly, and act on it before the danger is confirmed, not after.** Nobel executives in Russia, including one who kept a daily diary through the chaos of 1917, assumed the disorder was a passing phenomenon; only once it was clear the Bolsheviks would not fall did Emanuel flee disguised as a peasant while two of his relatives were jailed and every family asset in the country was seized.
