# Jeff Bezos' Shareholder Letters

> **Founder principle (Jeff Bezos):** Bezos ran Amazon on a small set of repeated habits: hire against a specific bar rather than a resume, borrow good ideas openly from people who already solved your problem, sort decisions by whether they are reversible before deciding how fast to make them, and let some of the biggest bets come from deliberate wandering rather than from asking customers what they want. He also treated staying different from every competitor as a constant expenditure of effort, never a decision made once.

*David Senra on Jeff Bezos, reading from Invent and Wander: The Collected Writings of Jeff Bezos. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NFjdllY44J0) · 70 min · published 2024-01-17.*

## Key lessons

- **Hire against three specific questions, not a general impression.** Amazon's hiring meetings asked whether you would admire this person, whether they would raise the average effectiveness of the group they were joining, and along what dimension they might be a superstar, a category broad enough to include a skill entirely unrelated to the job because it makes the whole team better to work with.
- **Borrow shamelessly from someone who already solved your problem, then apply it completely.** After Costco's Jim Sinegal explained his everyday low price membership model to Bezos over coffee, Bezos opened his executive meeting the following Monday determined to rebuild Amazon's entire pricing strategy around it, then proved the result by pricing a major book chain's own hundred bestselling titles and beating its total by roughly a quarter.
- **When the market punishes you, trust your internal numbers over the stock price.** During the dot-com crash, Amazon's stock fell more than eighty percent in a single year while its own internal metrics kept improving, and Bezos traced his ability to stay focused on the business rather than the ticker back to his father's advice from running a diamond business through earlier downturns.
- **Replace a dying metaphor with a better one before you give up on an opportunity.** When the outside world declared the internet gold rush over, Bezos argued that gold, a finite resource, was the wrong comparison, and that electricity, a layer that becomes more valuable as more industries build on top of it, was the right one, placing Amazon at what he called an early stage of that much longer buildout.
- **Sort every decision by whether you can reverse it before deciding how fast to make it.** Most business decisions are reversible and should be made quickly with less information than feels comfortable, while a small number are irreversible and deserve real deliberation, and Bezos warned that companies age badly by treating every decision like the irreversible kind.
- **Let some of your biggest bets come from wandering, not from customer requests.** No customer ever asked for Amazon's voice speaker, and Bezos described both that product and Amazon's cloud computing business as results of following a hunch rather than a plan, adding that a company's failed experiments have to keep growing in size as the company grows or it is not inventing at a scale that matters.
- **Fix the source of a problem, not its visible symptom.** Asked why he insisted on such a clean warehouse instead of simply removing whatever was causing the mess, Bezos treated the question as a small example of a larger habit: hunting for waste everywhere in the business rather than only tidying its effects.
- **Treat being different as something you must keep spending energy on.** In his final letter, Bezos wrote that ordinary forces pull a company toward sameness in countless small ways, and that real differentiation survives only through continuous, deliberate effort, not a single decision made once and left alone.
