# Working with Jeff Bezos

> **Founder principle (Jeff Bezos):** Force every product decision to start from the desired customer experience and work backward from it, rather than starting from the skills your company already has. Give each important initiative exactly one full-time owner and eliminate the dependencies around them instead of trying to coordinate better, and replace slide decks with written narrative documents so half-formed thinking has nowhere left to hide.

*David Senra on Jeff Bezos, reading from Working Backwards: Insights, Stories, and Secrets from Inside Amazon. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DUAQxt_rtiY) · 59 min · published 2024-04-08.*

## Key lessons

- **Set a standard so high that people flinch, then hold every product to it personally.** Bezos told a senior manager the story of Howard Hughes running his fingers along an aircraft's rivets and refusing to accept anything less than perfectly flush, then made clear that standard was now the manager's job too; Bezos personally proofread outgoing customer service emails and insisted that one bad experience could undo hundreds of good ones.
- **Give every important initiative exactly one full-time owner and remove the dependencies around them, instead of trying to coordinate better.** Amazon's single-threaded leadership model assigns one leader to one project only; the company concluded that improving cross-team communication was solving the wrong problem, so it eliminated the need for that communication instead, using documented APIs so teams could build in parallel rather than wait on each other.
- **Make your biggest decisions with about 70 percent of the information you wish you had.** Bezos's own rule was that waiting for 90 percent certainty is usually too slow, and that being fast and good at correcting course beats being slow and right; the real constraint on a fast-moving company is not information, it is a shortage of leaders trustworthy enough to own a single thing completely.
- **Replace slide decks with a six-page written narrative and start every meeting by reading it in silence.** After encountering an essay on how PowerPoint flattens the relative importance of ideas, Bezos banned it for the senior team; a well-written narrative forces the writer to show how ideas connect to each other in a way a bullet list lets people skip past.
- **Work backward from a press release and FAQ for a product that does not exist yet, before building anything.** Instead of pitching Bezos with market-size spreadsheets, teams learned to write the press release first and answer the hardest customer questions in an FAQ; deliberate length limits on both documents forced sharper thinking, and this exact process produced the Kindle. When Kindle development ran far longer and cost far more than planned and an executive asked how much more he would invest, Bezos's only reply was to turn to the CFO and ask "how much money do we have," signaling the project's importance rather than negotiating a ceiling.
- **Choose invention over fast-following, even when copying looks cheaper and quicker.** After Steve Jobs personally demoed iTunes for Windows and suggested Amazon's future was as the last place to buy CDs, Bezos refused to race out a copycat music service; he instead looked for a category where Amazon could be genuinely different, which led directly to the Kindle and Amazon's move into hardware.
- **Acquire the skill a strategy requires instead of outsourcing around the gap.** Amazon had no hardware expertise when it decided to build its own e-reader, and developing that capability internally took longer and cost more than outsourcing would have, but it left the company owning a skill it could reuse repeatedly rather than renting a fast follower's version of someone else's product.
- **Refuse a compromise when both options on offer are mediocre.** Once customers had experienced free shipping, Bezos treated "slow and free or fast and expensive" as a false choice and pushed for fast and free instead, even though Amazon's fulfillment network was not yet built for it; that refusal to settle produced Amazon Prime, conceived, built, and launched in a few months once he sent the internal directive.
- **Borrow a working idea from an unrelated company and redeploy it inside your own constraints.** Watching how a Toyota-style andon cord let any factory worker halt the line over a quality defect, Bezos demanded an andon cord for Amazon customer service; later, noticing how Netflix folded streaming into its existing DVD subscription instead of selling it separately, he applied the same logic to add video free inside Prime rather than launch a standalone streaming product.
