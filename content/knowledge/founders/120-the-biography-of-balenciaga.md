# The Biography of Balenciaga

> **Founder principle (Balenciaga):** Learn to do every step of the craft with your own hands, design, cut, sew, fit, and finish, through decades of private practice before anyone is watching. Go to where the very richest customers already gather, then design for permanence instead of the next season's trend, so the product is treasured and passed down rather than replaced. Protect the workshop's privacy and silence so completely that the work itself, not the founder, is the only public statement.

*David Senra on Balenciaga, reading from Creators by Paul Johnson. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yTt8bX4UfP8) · 36 min · published 2024-04-02.*

## Key lessons

- **Master every step of the craft with your own hands, not just its direction.** He could design, cut, sew, fit, and finish a garment entirely himself, a combination almost no other Paris designer of his era could match. Christian Dior called him "the master of us all," and Coco Chanel said he was the only true couturier, everyone else was simply a fashion designer.
- **Be relentlessly prolific in private, long before anyone is paying attention.** He began sewing in his mother's dressmaking class at three and a half, apprenticed in tailoring at twelve specifically to learn the art of cutting that few designers ever master, and kept a piece of sewing in his own hands every day for 74 years. At the height of his career he could personally complete fittings for 180 outfits in a single day.
- **Go to where your best possible customers already gather, then don't dilute who you're building for.** His first shop opened on a Spanish coast frequented by high society, and once established in Paris he deliberately built a client list of the very richest, in explicit contrast to Dior, who dressed the merely rich.
- **Design for permanence, not for the next season's trend.** While Dior changed his line twice a year, Balenciaga's evening dresses stayed fundamentally the same across decades, made well enough that clients bought them as an investment and passed them down; one client's dress was still being worn by her granddaughter decades after it was made.
- **Make the product physically comfortable even at its most visually extravagant, because comfort is what produces confidence.** His stated design principle was that a client who is comfortable in her clothes is confident, and a confident client wears the clothes with style; he wanted women reluctant to take the dress off at the end of the night, not relieved to be out of it.
- **Treat a genuinely world-class supplier as an equal collaborator, not a vendor.** Because he could do the needlework himself, he could recognize true skill in others; he patronized the best embroiderers and textile makers in the world and often collaborated with them to invent entirely new materials for a single collection.
- **Protect the work by protecting your own privacy; let the work be the only public statement.** He gave almost no interviews, avoided photographs, never went out in society, and ran a workshop so hushed and restricted that at one point people believed Balenciaga might be a pseudonym for someone who didn't actually exist.
- **Hold a contrarian design position on principle, and let time be the judge.** His own maxim was to "cut against the bias," designing deliberately against the prevailing trend even as the industry moved toward the trendy in the 1960s; the pieces he made that way became the ones most admired, collected, and copied decades later.
