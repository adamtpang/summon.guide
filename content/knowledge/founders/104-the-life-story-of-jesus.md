# The Life Story of Jesus

> **Founder principle (Jesus):** Build authority on lived experience and close attention rather than credentials or spectacle, teaching by asking questions and drawing on the details of ordinary life. Answer a corrupt, self-protecting system with an inward transformation rather than an outward revolt.

*David Senra on Jesus, reading from Jesus: A Biography from a Believer, by Paul Johnson. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EGTB5FgXPeU) · 35 min · published 2024-04-19.*

## Key lessons

- **Self-taught mastery shows up in specifics, not credentials.** For roughly eighteen years the record goes silent, and by the time his teaching begins he is dismissed by the educated elite as unschooled, yet he speaks with confident, expert command of trade and agriculture that Paul Johnson argues could only come from real hands-on experience rather than formal study.
- **Teach by asking, not only by telling.** More than three hundred distinct questions are attributed to him across the four gospels, and by one reading his first recorded words are themselves a question, a habit of pulling people toward their own understanding rather than simply handing them a conclusion.
- **Authority that does not need to be asserted carries further than authority that does.** He is repeatedly described as observant and detail oriented, with an intensity of eye contact people remembered, and the text is explicit that the authority he carried was there from the beginning rather than something he had to acquire.
- **Treat wealth as neutral and judge it only by its use.** He never suggested poverty made a person virtuous, taught instead that money creates real opportunity to do good as easily as it creates opportunity for corruption, and used the parable of a merchant who used his profit to help a stranger to make the point concrete.
- **Give the individual your full attention even while addressing a crowd.** Though he regularly spoke to large audiences, the people who heard him described being addressed as though he were speaking to them alone, a discipline of presence rather than a habit of broadcasting.
- **Expect the home crowd to resist you first, and build your circle from whoever stands by you afterward.** His first teaching in his own hometown triggered a mob that tried to throw him off a cliff, and he drew the lesson that no prophet is accepted in his own hometown, then recruited the fishermen who protected him in its aftermath as his first followers.
- **A threatened system protects itself by spreading responsibility, not by owning it.** The trial that ended in his execution combined, in Paul Johnson's account, prejudice and false witness with officials each trying to avoid responsibility by passing the decision to someone else, a pattern of institutional self-protection that shows up whenever authority feels genuinely challenged.
- **Close observation of ordinary life supplies the material that makes teaching land.** He was repeatedly noted for watching small human details, a mother's delight in a newborn, a father's affection, the way children play, and folding those exact observations directly into his lessons rather than reaching for abstraction.
- **When the system around you is corrupt, offer an inward alternative instead of an outward revolt.** Facing a world Paul Johnson calls corrupt, dishonest, and cruel, the response was not political revolution but what the text calls an inner life of humility, love, generosity, and forgiveness, a choice of method as much as a matter of belief.
