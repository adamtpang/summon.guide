# The Relentless Missionary Creating AGI: Demis Hassabis

> **Founder principle (Demis Hassabis):** Build the company around a mission you would work on even without any payoff, not a market opportunity you back into. Protect that mission from anything that could dilute your judgment, including your own charisma, and be willing to change backers, structure, or tactics whenever it is what keeps the mission itself alive.

*David Senra on Demis Hassabis, reading from The Infinity Machine by Sebastian Mallaby. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mHE37R0aBUA) · 55 min · published 2026-04-01.*

## Key lessons

- **Found the company because of the mission, not the other way around.** Early investor Peter Thiel distinguished Hassabis from a mercenary entrepreneur who starts wanting to get rich and casts around for an idea: Hassabis was a missionary who felt compelled to solve one specific problem and started a company only as the mechanism for tackling it. Missionaries do not quit, because the work itself is the point.
- **Interpret "do your best" as total, not partial, effort.** A co-founder described Hassabis as having no 50 percent mode and not even a 99 percent mode, only 100 percent, a standard Hassabis says he took from his father literally as a child and never dialed back. Whatever standard you set for yourself will be read at face value by the people watching you.
- **Turn your own overreach into a permanent process fix.** His first company failed partly because his enthusiasm over inspired his engineers into telling him a wildly ambitious game was achievable when it was not, since none of them were giving him honest signal anymore. At DeepMind he corrected for this directly: build the team quietly first, then set the demanding goal only once real capability existed to support it.
- **Refuse deals that would cost you control of the mission.** Early investors wanted more than half the company in exchange for funding, which Hassabis described as wanting their souls in exchange for the money, and he walked away from that structure repeatedly rather than accept financing that meant losing control.
- **Choose the backer that removes the biggest distraction from your actual work.** Constant fundraising fights left him feeling, in his own words, like his brain was atrophying, so he sold DeepMind to Google specifically to escape the fundraising cycle and secure computing resources at a scale no venture investor could offer, even though it meant giving up independence.
- **Recruit by filtering hard for true believers, not by chasing broad appeal.** The DeepMind founders would openly tell people they were building an artificial general intelligence company, and treated the roughly 80 percent who rolled their eyes and walked away as a fast, efficient way to find the handful who did not.
- **Shift the whole organization into wartime mode when a real competitive threat appears.** When ChatGPT's growth made clear that OpenAI had pulled ahead, Hassabis pared back publishing research that competitors could copy, merged overlapping teams, and pushed the culture from open ended science toward focused engineering and shipping, describing the moment plainly as wartime.
- **Give away the win, then move immediately to the next problem.** When DeepMind solved protein structure prediction, the underlying result was released free to researchers worldwide rather than held for competitive advantage, and Hassabis did not pause to savor the achievement before turning attention to the next target.
