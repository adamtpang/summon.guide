# Nick & Zak's Excellent Adventure: How Nick Sleep & Qais Zakaria Built Their Partnership

> **Founder principle (Nick Sleep and Qais Zakaria):** Treat investing as a search for a small number of businesses built around one repeatable idea: pass efficiency gains back to customers as the company scales, which compounds customer loyalty and competitive advantage together. Ignore news, forecasts, and any information with a short shelf life, and spend the time saved on deep, direct research into management and unit economics instead. Once a business like this is found, concentrate hard and hold for years rather than trade around its price.

*David Senra on Nick Sleep and Qais Zakaria, reading from Richer, Wiser, Happier by William Green. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHvASCrRtd4) · 42 min · published 2024-09-26.*

## Key lessons

- **Dedicate the fund to quality over money from day one.** Nick Sleep credited Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance with convincing him that doing everything with quality was where the real satisfaction was, and he and Qais Zakaria built Nomad Investment Partnership in 2001 explicitly as an act of rebellion against the industry, not as a vehicle for gathering assets.
- **Ignore information with a short shelf life.** They refused calls from stockbrokers pitching ideas, dismissed constant news and short-term data as things that would be worthless within weeks, and spent their time instead on annual reports and direct company research, a practice they called intentional disconnection.
- **Ask three destination questions before committing capital.** Their framework, destination analysis, asked what a business's intended destination looked like in ten or twenty years, what management needed to be doing today to raise the odds of reaching it, and what could prevent the company from arriving there at all.
- **Learn from a costly early mistake instead of repeating it.** A cigar-butt trade in Stagecoach taught them the hard way: they sold a turnaround stock after a six-times gain only to watch it climb much further, which pushed them to stop hunting cheap stocks and start buying wonderful businesses meant to be held for years.
- **Name the business model and let it become your one filter.** Studying Costco's thin markups led them to coin the term scale economies shared, a company that returns efficiency gains to customers as lower prices, which grows revenue, funds further cost cuts, and repeats, and they went looking for the same pattern in Walmart, Dell, Southwest Airlines, and Amazon.
- **Recognize your own thesis when it appears in a new industry.** They had followed Amazon since 1997 but did not commit seriously until Amazon Prime launched in 2005, when Sleep recognized it instantly as Amazon's version of a Costco membership fee, and they began buying aggressively.
- **Concentrate instead of diversify once conviction is earned.** At times Nomad held as much as 70 percent of its assets in a single stock, and after the 2008 crash cut the fund's value by 45.3 percent, they used the panic to concentrate further into Amazon, Costco, and Berkshire Hathaway rather than retreat to safety.
- **Return outside capital once the fund has done its job.** In 2014, after about fourteen years and a tenfold gain in net asset value, Sleep and Zakaria voluntarily dissolved Nomad and returned the money to their investors rather than keep collecting fees on a strategy they felt had run its course.
- **Expect concentration to stay uncomfortable even after it has worked.** By 2018 Amazon alone had grown to more than 70 percent of Sleep's personal net worth, and he sold half his stake in a single day, later saying he felt horribly conflicted and was not sure it had been the right decision.
