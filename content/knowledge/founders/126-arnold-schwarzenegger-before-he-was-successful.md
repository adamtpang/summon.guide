# Arnold Schwarzenegger (Before He Was Successful)

> **Founder principle (Arnold Schwarzenegger):** Long before any fame, Arnold ran on a small set of deliberate habits: turn early rejection into fuel, write down exact and immediate goals with no fallback plan, keep building relationships and studying even while physically training for hours a day, and fund your own independence before you get to be selective. His six-year relationship with Barbara Outland Baker, told from her side, shows the private machinery behind the public myth.

*David Senra on Arnold Schwarzenegger, reading from Arnold and Me: In the Shadow of the Austrian Oak by Barbara Outland Baker. Founders Podcast · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IkkEBlYEXSY) · 43 min · published 2024-03-27.*

## Key lessons

- **Turn early rejection into fuel rather than injury.** At ten years old Arnold was sent alone to his godmother's farm for two months while his parents took his brother on vacation, an exclusion he later said gave him "will, desire, and drive that normal life doesn't create." He converted a specific childhood wound into a permanent source of motivation instead of carrying it as simple resentment.
- **Write exact, immediate goals the moment you land somewhere new.** Arriving in America at twenty one, Arnold wrote down five concrete goals: rent an apartment near Gold's Gym, get a motivating training partner, find a freelance writer for his articles, get access to a car, and get a raise. Each was specific and achievable in the near term, not a vague ambition to be great.
- **Refuse a plan B and say so plainly to the people around you.** Arnold told his girlfriend outright that marriage and children were not part of the plan until he reached the top of bodybuilding, then used that as a launchpad into movies and business. When she wrote him a list of things to change about himself, he read it, discarded it, and ended the relationship immediately rather than negotiate.
- **Build a deliberate network of allies through consistent correspondence, not just charisma.** Contemporaries described him as one of the most calculating people they had ever met, someone who understood from a young age that his career would run on relationships and who wrote to cultivate them intentionally. That same networking instinct is what put him in front of the publisher Joe Weider after his first major contest win.
- **Keep studying past the point where most competitors stop training.** While training up to four times a day, Arnold also accumulated college credits, took philosophy and business classes, worked deliberately to reduce his accent, and expanded his vocabulary by thousands of words. He treated the acquisition of knowledge as a second full-time discipline running alongside his physical one.
- **Fund independent income before you get to be selective.** Arnold and his girlfriend lived in one frugal apartment for five years while he used his salary and side earnings to buy income-generating real estate with a local realtor. That financial independence is what let him later turn down roles that did not fit his ambitions, telling one director plainly that he intended to be a star, not just an actor.
- **Use negative motivation on purpose.** People close to him traced his drive back to a militaristic father who dismissed him, and one mentor described this as a lifelong "I am going to prove you wrong" engine that pushed him to the top of bodybuilding, then film, then politics. He did not resolve that motivation; he kept redirecting it at each new field.
- **Practice optimism as a discipline, not a mood.** Arnold consistently reframed setbacks as solvable, a habit he later linked to Edwin Land's line that "optimism is a moral duty." Friends and partners repeatedly described this as one of his most transferable traits, independent of whatever specific goal he was chasing at the time.
