## Key lessons

- Liu said she left because she had gotten what she wanted from skating, had skated since age 5 without a real break, and wanted to attend school and experience things outside the sport.
- During the break, the article reports, she enrolled at UCLA, tried sports skating had ruled out (skiing, volleyball, basketball, tennis), and took up fashion, photography and dance.
- She said a first ski trip at the start of 2024 gave her an adrenaline rush she had not felt since quitting, and that made her want to step on the ice just to see how it felt. She stressed she was not planning a return at that point.
- At that first public session she tried a double Axel and found she could still do it. She kept going back, about two weeks apart and roughly an hour at a time, testing one more jump each visit.
- She described the turning point as realizing she could do school and skating together, a choice she said she had never felt she had before. Then she called her coach, and serious work began in the summer.
- She said her coach, Phillip DiGuglielmo, first thought her wish to return was a prank.
- Liu said returning as an adult means she decides for herself: she sets her schedule, gets herself to the rink, and picks her session and lesson times. She called this "liberating."
- She said she now treats skating more like one of her hobbies. She was clear this does not mean training less hard, only that skating is no longer her whole life, which lets her enjoy it.
- Her stated goal for the first season back was simply to compete internationally and do her best, one season at a time. She said she intends to keep the balanced life she built while away.
- Applied today: a return does not have to be decided in one move. Short, low-stakes trials can show whether the interest is real before any commitment is made.
