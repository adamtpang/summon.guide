## Key lessons

- NBC Olympics reports Liu won women's singles gold in Milan with 226.79 points, ahead of Kaori Sakamoto (224.90) and Ami Nakai (219.16), moving up from third after the short program. It also reports she won gold in the team event.
- The article states she is the first US woman to win Olympic singles gold since Sarah Hughes in 2002.
- Liu said the titles are huge but she does not want them to overshadow who she is and what she is about. She said winning "isn't all that," and neither is losing.
- She called retirement the best thing she could have done for herself. She said the time away showed her she is a creative person with her own ideas about fashion, choreography and music.
- She said she now does that creative work on her own terms and that nobody tells her what to do.
- She described her state during the free skate as calm, happy and confident, and said she was grateful for the chance to show her art and ideas.
- Her coach Phillip DiGuglielmo told NBC she had not come to win a medal but to skate and enjoy it. That is his description, not hers.
- The article notes the freedom sits on top of disciplined work: after a fall in her first event back in September 2024, it counts 221 jumps without a fall. This is the writer's observation.
- Asked about her path, Liu said she feels lucky and is grateful even for the bad parts, because she would not be where she is without them.
