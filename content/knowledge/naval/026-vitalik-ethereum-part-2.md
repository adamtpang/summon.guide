# Vitalik: Ethereum, Part 2

> **Naval's principle — Fear, not greed, is what breaks communities:** Naval and Haseeb Qureshi press Vitalik Buterin on how his role shifted from scrappy founder to something closer to an elder statesman managing an increasingly formal protocol bureaucracy. Vitalik's throughline is that the ugliest moments in both Bitcoin's and Ethereum's history — block-size wars, the DAO fork and the Ethereum Classic split — weren't driven by greed, which people pursue openly in their own name, but by fear, which people dress up as principle and pursue in everyone else's name.

## Key lessons

- Vitalik argues "protocol politics is less different from regular politics than you might think" — arguments over block size or governance carry the same "religious fervor" as debates over free speech or the welfare state, because both emerge from thousands of people with partly aligned, partly competing incentives trying to agree on shared rules.
- He traces the ugliest chapters in crypto to fear rather than greed: greed is "always done in your own name," while fear is "always done in everybody else's name," which is why both the Bitcoin small-block/big-block war and the Ethereum/Ethereum Classic split turned personal — each side felt the other was trying to "completely destroy" it.
- The DAO hard fork is Vitalik's clearest example of a decision he'd defend but a process he regrets — Ethereum forked to return funds stolen in the DAO hack, splitting the chain into Ethereum and Ethereum Classic, and while he stands by the outcome, he says the community made anyone who opposed the fork feel treated as "evil" or "pro-hacker," a social dynamic he wishes had gone differently.
- Vitalik describes his own influence shrinking on a predictable schedule — "less now than I did six months ago... six months ago I had less than a year ago" — as decisions route through the formalized "all-core-devs call" process, and cites EIP-4488, a proposal to cheapen calldata for rollups, as something he still can't single-handedly push through.
- He holds up Zcash's Zooko Wilcox as a model for public conduct, describing how Zooko retweets even people who insult him — "Zooko stop retweeting useless crap" famously became one such retweet — as evidence that a protocol's most visible figure sets the tone for how its whole community argues.
- Asked for standard-track life advice, Vitalik keeps it simple: "strive to be a better person at the end of every week than you were at the beginning of it," which he pairs with his own compounding, nomadic life — no house, no car, permanent travel since a 2013 Bitcoin trip that "never stopped" — as proof that skipping the conventional path can still compound into something real.
