# On Recruiting

> **Naval's principle — Recruiting is the founder's job, permanently:** Naval argues that recruiting, fundraising, strategy, and product vision are the four things a founder can never delegate, and recruiting is the most important of the four because it sets the ceiling on everything else — "there will never be a better recruiter in the company than the founder," since talented people won't work for someone below their own level. His prescription is to break the normal rules of hiring, hunt for undiscovered talent before it's obvious, and curate ruthlessly for self-motivated, low-ego "geniuses" rather than filling roles.

*Naval Ravikant, on his own channel.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S8x978NnZSI) · 52 min · published 2025-11-08.

## Key lessons

- Recruiting can't be delegated because a founder's own quality caps everyone they can attract: "you're never going to be able to hire anybody who's better than you are," and the moment a company has middle layers of management doing the hiring, the founder is already disconnected from the company's DNA.
- His current company runs on "geniuses only" and breaks a rule with nearly every hire — bending equity, start dates, titles, or reporting lines — because, as he puts it, great people "are not cogs in a machine," and the test for whether someone belongs is whether a recruit could randomly interview anyone else on the team and walk away impressed.
- Undiscovered talent beats obvious talent: his co-founder finds engineers by chasing genuinely interesting side projects — like someone tinkering with unusual ML algorithms for micro weather forecasting — and reaching out with a real technical question, because "by the time someone's famous on Twitter, it's too late to recruit them."
- He argues "every great engineer is also an artist," pointing to the AirPods' industrial design — the magnetic snap, the sculpted ear fit, the G3 curves — as proof that the best engineering is indistinguishable from art, and says at least half his current engineers have serious art practices on the side.
- The company deliberately avoids Slack, because group chat "degenerates" into politicking and noise the way email spam does; forcing one-on-one text communication keeps the team small enough that "people who aren't pulling their weight can't hide" and the founder can curate directly.
- He separates burnout from disengagement — "usually burnout is a sign you're working on something that either isn't working or you don't enjoy the work fundamentally" — and says the two traits he screens hardest for are self-motivation and low ego, since low-ego people are dramatically easier to scale a team around than high performers who need their egos managed.
