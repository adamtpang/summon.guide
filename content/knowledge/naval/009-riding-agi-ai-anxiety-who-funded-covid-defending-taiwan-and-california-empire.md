# Riding AGI, AI Anxiety, Who Funded COVID, Defending Taiwan, and California Empire

> **Naval's principle — Become the AI handler, not the AI's victim:** In a wide-ranging, unscripted conversation with three founders, Naval argues that the crowd-level panic building around AI — protests at data centers, fear of job loss, "AI psychosis" — is a rational economic fear wearing an irrational costume, the same way past waves of technological disruption got blamed on safety rather than displacement. His answer isn't caution, it's engagement: the people being displaced are specifically the ones refusing to use the tools, while the people using AI are more productive and more employed than ever, which is why he frames the winning posture as becoming an "AI handler" — pairing your own taste and judgment with an agent's labor rather than competing against it or hiding from it.

*Naval Ravikant with Garry Tan, Daniel Francis, and Farbood Nivi.* [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6m-ZZBCiiEE) · 68 min · published 2026-07-03.

## Key lessons

- Garry Tan lays out a raw compute thesis: inference capacity could grow roughly "90,000x" over the next 24 to 36 months, which Naval reframes as not just more usage but new emergent capabilities at every order of magnitude — "it's not just what it does in terms of usage but in terms of capabilities."
- Naval argues intelligence itself isn't the bottleneck right now, cost is — illustrated by Daniel Francis's account of driving an OpenClaw-plus-Opus setup from "$100 a month per person" down to "$2.84" by building a custom eval harness and fleet-management system, while still preferring to always pay for the smarter model on any judgment call because small error-rate differences compound massively across repeated agent runs.
- Naval's read on China's AI strategy: "AI is our Chinese against their Chinese" — much of the field's talent pool trained together, works across labs interchangeably, and moves freely between them, while he argues American labs "do not know how to protect secrets," making distillation of leaked or resold frontier weights close to inevitable. He proposes that any lab trained on the open web should be required to open its own models after a fixed window, roughly 12 months.
- Naval states as established fact that the COVID-19 coronavirus originated from NIH-funded gain-of-function research conducted in coordination with Chinese researchers ("designed by NIH in North Carolina, assembled in China"), and uses it to argue that AI-enabled bioweapon risk isn't a categorically new danger, since a "smart biohacker student" could already access comparable capability without AI's help.
- On Taiwan, Naval takes the contrarian position that reunification with China is effectively already underway: he claims wealthy Taiwanese families dodge mandatory conscription by keeping children abroad for three months a year, and that US aircraft carriers are obsolete against China's land-based missiles and drone manufacturing base — "it's like China trying to defend the Florida Keys from us."
- The group jokes about "Universal Basic Robot" instead of UBI, landing on Naval's serious point underneath it: robotics optimists say general-purpose robots are two to three years away, skeptics say five to ten, but "no one thinks it's impossible" — and in the meantime, humans stay economically relevant as AI's handlers and taste-providers, since, in his words, "the only reason you're being displaced by an AI is because you refuse to use the AI."
