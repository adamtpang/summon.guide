## Key lessons

- Baby Step 3 begins once all debt except the mortgage is paid. The money that was going to the debt snowball is redirected into savings.
- The fund is sized on essentials only: housing, utilities, groceries, insurance, transportation, and other vital bills. A person adds up those monthly costs and multiplies by 3 to 6.
- The page suggests 3 months for someone with a steady job and 6 months for someone whose pay varies.
- It describes the fund's purpose with Ramsey's "Murphy" image: things go wrong at bad times, and cash on hand turns a crisis into an inconvenience.
- The page says most people finish this step in about 6 to 12 months, depending on expenses and how much margin the budget produces, and urges the same intensity used during debt payoff.
- It says to pause all investing, including contributions up to an employer match, until this step is complete.
- Where to keep it: the page stresses liquidity and lists a savings account linked to checking, a money market account with debit or check access, or a high-yield savings account. It says not to invest this money, because it is insurance and not a way to get rich.
- Before using the fund, a person should first try to cover the cost within the monthly budget, then ask whether the expense is unexpected, necessary, and urgent. Wants, upgrades, vacations, holidays, and routine car maintenance are named as non-emergencies.
- The fund is not a house down payment. The page describes an unofficial "Baby Step 3b" of saving separately for a down payment, ideally 20% to avoid private mortgage insurance, with 5% to 10% acceptable for first-time buyers.
- Limitation (not from the page, mainstream disagreement): the 3 to 6 month target itself is widely shared, but the instruction to stop all retirement investing, even up to an employer match, until the fund is full is contested. In a 2025 GOBankingRates piece carried by Nasdaq, Robert Johnson, chairman and CEO of Economic Index Associates, calls giving up an employer match one of the worst financial mistakes, while other experts quoted there support a temporary pause only for people overwhelmed by high-interest debt.
