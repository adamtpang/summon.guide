## Key lessons

- The page sets two preconditions: no debt other than the mortgage, and 3 to 6 months of expenses saved. It argues that investing while in debt is like running a race with the feet chained.
- The 15% is calculated on gross (before-tax) household income, and an employer's matching contribution does not count toward it. A household earning $100,000 would aim for $15,000 a year.
- The page's account order is summarized as "Match beats Roth beats traditional": contribute up to any employer match first, then use a Roth IRA, then return to the workplace plan until 15% is reached. Self-employed people are pointed to a Roth IRA and then plans such as a solo 401(k) or SEP-IRA.
- Retirement accounts are described as containers. The account supplies tax advantages, and the investments inside it supply the growth.
- Ramsey's organization tells readers to spread money evenly across four categories of stock mutual funds (growth and income, growth, aggressive growth, and international) and to look for funds with long records. This is reported here as what the page teaches, not as a recommendation.
- The page lists what it tells readers to avoid: single stocks, cryptocurrency, commodities such as gold, day trading, margin trading, and unproven start-ups. It also says never to invest in something one does not understand.
- It advises staying invested during market drops, stating that people who sell in a downturn usually miss the recovery, and reviewing the plan once or twice a year.
- It warns against cashing out or borrowing from retirement accounts early, citing income taxes plus a 10% penalty and the loss of compounding.
- If the emergency fund is spent on a real emergency, the page says to pause investing until the fund is refilled, then resume the 15%.
- It also describes retirement as a financial number rather than an age, and says 15% leaves room for college savings (Step 5) and extra mortgage payments (Step 6).
- Limitation (not from the page, mainstream disagreement): Ramsey's return assumptions are disputed. A Forbes column by fee-only planner Robert Schmansky carries the title "Dave Ramsey's Plan For 12% Returns Is Not Achievable"; only its title was confirmed for this note, not its argument. Treat any growth example on the page as an illustration, not a forecast.
