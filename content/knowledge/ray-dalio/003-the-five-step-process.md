# Principles for Success: "The Five Step Process" | Episode 3

> **Key principle — Ray Dalio:** Reflecting carefully after pain led him to see that being successful "simply consisted of five steps," repeated over and over as a personal evolutionary loop: know your goals, encounter the problems in your way, diagnose their root cause, design a plan, and execute it. He's explicit that most people can't do all five steps equally well, which is why the process only works if you're honest about which step you're weak at.

*Ray Dalio, Principles for Success, Episode 3 of 8. [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ryD8lfSEGio) · 3 min · 670K views.*

## Key lessons

- **The five steps, named precisely:** know your goals and run after them; encounter the problems that stand in the way (usually painful, and left untreated some "can lead to your ruin"); diagnose the problems to their root cause rather than jumping to solutions; design a plan to eliminate them; execute the designs.
- **What "diagnose" means specifically: don't jump to solutions.** "Take a step back and reflect in order to really distinguish the symptoms from the disease" — the diagnosis step exists precisely to slow down the impulse to fix things before understanding them.
- **The five steps repeat as a loop, not a one-time sequence.** "A successful life essentially consists of doing these five steps over and over again. This is your personal evolution" — and he frames this as a general law of nature, true of "any product, any organization, or any person."
- **Success doesn't make the struggle easier — it raises the size of the challenge.** "My struggling never became easier because the more capable I became, the greater the challenges I would take on," which he presents as expected, not a sign of doing something wrong.
- **Nobody is strong at all five steps, and that's the actual risk.** "Because different people are strong and weak at different things, most people can't do all five steps well. Not facing this reality means you could stretch further than you should" — with proportionally bigger falls as the stakes rise.
- **The episode explicitly sets up the next one on his own life**, referencing "something like this happened to me in 1982," a direct pointer to the failure (his 1982 wipeout betting against a debt-driven crash that didn't come) that forced him to build this whole system in the first place.
