# Recognize Your Two Barriers: Top 5 Most Valuable Principles #5

> **Key principle — Ray Dalio:** He frames the ego barrier and the blind-spot barrier as literal brain architecture, not character flaws: the amygdala (subliminal, defensive, reacts to criticism as an attack) fighting the prefrontal cortex (logical, capable of real reflection), described as "like Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde." Voted by his own audience as the #5 most valuable principle in his book, which he treats as evidence that naming the mechanism, not just the moral ("be humble"), is what actually changes behavior.

*Ray Dalio, Principles (Top 5 Most Valuable Principles series). [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zI9I6MaS5OE) · 10 min · 217K views.*

## Key lessons

- **The ego barrier is literally in the brain, not a personality trait.** Deep-seated needs and fears (to be loved, to survive, to matter) "reside in primitive parts of your brain such as the amygdala," which are inaccessible to conscious awareness, "oversimplify things," and "respond to criticism as an attack even when the higher-level parts of the brain understand that constructive criticism is good for you."
- **He names the internal conflict directly: "your higher level you" versus "your lower level you," "like Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde."** You can watch this fight happen in real time — "when someone gets angry with himself his prefrontal cortex is sparring with his amygdala."
- **The practical failure mode: your explanations stop making sense** when the ego barrier is active, "because your lower level you is trying to speak through your upper level you." The fix isn't willpower, it's noticing the mechanism: "you must not let your need to be right be more important than your need to find out what's true."
- **The blind-spot barrier is about cognitive range, using a sensory analogy.** "Just as we all have different ranges for hearing pitch and seeing colors, we have different ranges for seeing and understanding things" — some people naturally see big pictures and miss detail, others the reverse, and "people can't appreciate what they can't see."
- **Blind spots are harder to detect than physical ones because there's no obvious test.** "Colorblind people eventually find out that they're colorblind, whereas most people never see or understand the ways in which their ways of thinking are making them blind."
- **The fix for blind spots is structural, not willpower-based: build compensating systems or borrow other people's strengths.** He gives concrete mechanisms — "programmed reminders," relying on people who are strong where you're weak — and calls these two barriers together "the fatal flaws that keep intelligent hardworking people from living up to their potentials," explicitly invoking Aristotle's definition of tragedy.
