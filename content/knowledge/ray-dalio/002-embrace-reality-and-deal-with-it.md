# Principles for Success: "Embrace Reality and Deal With It" | Episode 2

> **Key principle — Ray Dalio:** He feared boredom and mediocrity more than failure, so he ran after what he wanted, crashed, learned, and ran again. That process taught him his central formula: pain plus reflection equals progress. He calls himself a "hyperrealist," someone who has discovered the rewards of deeply understanding, accepting, and working with reality exactly as it is, which he says is not the opposite of having big dreams but the actual mechanism for achieving them.

*Ray Dalio, Principles for Success, Episode 2 of 8. [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tfrrubw7pcE) · 4 min · 875K views.*

## Key lessons

- **"Truth is the essential foundation for producing good outcomes."** By truth he means nothing more than how the world actually works — "the laws of reality," which humans didn't create but can use to their advantage once they accept them.
- **Being a hyperrealist is not the same as being unambitious.** "People sometimes think I'm saying that dreams can't come true. That's absolutely not true... hyper-realism is the best way to choose one's dreams and then achieve them." His formula: big dreams plus embracing reality plus determination equals a successful life.
- **Success is self-defined, not a fixed target.** "I don't care whether you want to be a master of the universe or to live under a palm tree... each of us chooses goals based on our values," but everyone still needs a working method for facing problems, mistakes, and weaknesses along the way.
- **Pain is reframed as information, not something to avoid.** He learned to treat harsh realities as puzzles that reward you for solving them, and "to treat pain as a cue that a great learning opportunity is at hand" — which produced his most-repeated formula, pain plus reflection equals progress.
- **Meditation is named specifically as what made this reframe possible**, helping him calm down enough to actually embrace realities and deal with them rather than avoid them, after which "the rewards brought me pleasure and the pain faded."
- **The five-step process (covered in the next episode) is presented as the operational version of this principle** — reflecting on pain doesn't stay abstract, it becomes a repeatable method for "directing your own personal evolution."
