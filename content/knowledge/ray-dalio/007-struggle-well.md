# Principles for Success: "Struggle Well" | Episode 8

> **Key principle — Ray Dalio:** The closing episode of the miniseries reframes everything before it: reaching a goal never produced lasting satisfaction for him, so he concluded the goal itself was never really the point. "The things we are striving for are just the bait, struggling to get them forces us to evolve, and it is this struggle toward personal evolution with others that is the reward." The ambition doesn't disappear, it redirects, from "get across the jungle" to "find greater and greater challenges to go after."

*Ray Dalio, Principles for Success, Episode 8 of 8 (final episode). [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYY-1KxXnSg) · 4 min · 425K views.*

## Key lessons

- **Reaching a goal didn't deliver lasting satisfaction, and that observation is what the whole episode is built on.** "I found that when I reached each new higher level of success I really remain[ed un]satisfied" — success is not a matter of attaining one's goals.
- **The reframe, stated as directly as anywhere in his body of work:** "the things we are striving for are just the bait, struggling to get them forces us to evolve, and it is this struggle toward personal evolution with others that is the reward."
- **Struggling hard for big things guarantees painful falls, and those falls function as a sorting mechanism.** "Striving hard for big things is bound to lead you to painful falls, it's just part of the process. Such setbacks will test you, they sort people." Some reflect, learn, and keep going; others "decide that this game is not for them and get off the field."
- **Strengths and weaknesses are described as structurally linked, not separate traits.** "By nature most people's greatest strengths are also connected to their most significant weaknesses" — echoing the designer/manager framing (see [Episode: idea meritocracy](005-designer-of-your-machine-vs-worker-in-it.md)) that the fix is compensating systems and other people, not eliminating the weakness.
- **Ambition doesn't get smaller as you succeed, it gets redirected toward other people.** "I no longer wanted to get across the jungle but instead wanted to find greater and greater challenges to go after, surrounded by great people... the success of the mission and the well-being of the people alongside me came more important than my own success."
- **The closing frame is explicitly about mortality, not motivation.** He names wanting others to succeed after he's gone, and calls it a live struggle, not a resolved one: "I struggle with this now." Machines break down, their parts "go back into the system to become parts of new machines that also evolve through time" — sad from inside the attachment, "really beautiful" from the higher level.
- **The final instruction is explicitly to discard authority, not defer to it:** "Forget about where these principles came from, just assess whether or not they are useful to you and evolve them to suit your own needs."
