## Key lessons

- Situation: Shapiro, Gottman and Carrere followed 130 newlywed couples for 6 years. Forty-three became parents and 39 childless couples served as a comparison, to see which early features of the marital friendship predict whether satisfaction holds up through the arrival of a first baby.
- What they found: mothers' stable or rising satisfaction was predicted by the husband's expressed fondness for her, his awareness of her and of the relationship, and her awareness of him and the relationship.
- What predicted decline: the husband's negativity toward his wife, his disappointment in the marriage, or either partner describing life as chaotic.
- Transferable lesson: the transition to parenthood puts pressure on the friendship, and small habits of fondness and staying aware of your partner's world are what the data associate with holding steady. Findings are from a specific 1990s sample of different-sex couples and are associations, not proof of what any couple should do.
