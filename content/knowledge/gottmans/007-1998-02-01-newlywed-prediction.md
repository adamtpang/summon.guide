## Key lessons

- Situation: Gottman, Coan, Carrere and Swanson observed 130 newlywed couples and asked which interaction processes separate couples who divorce from couples who stay together, and which separate happy stable couples from unhappy stable ones.
- What they did: they tested seven types of process models and discussed the results. The ERIC record states that divorce versus stability was predicted with 83% accuracy and satisfaction with 80% accuracy.
- Caveat that belongs with those numbers: Heyman and Smith Slep later argued that divorce-prediction equations like these must be cross-validated on new samples and that accuracy falls sharply when they are (abstract at https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17066126/). The 83% describes fit in the study sample, not how well the model would sort strangers.
- Transferable lesson: use the findings to notice what is worth changing (how conflicts start, whether partners soothe or escalate), never to score a marriage or predict its end. If the guide quotes the figure, it says 83% in one sample of 130 couples, with the limits above.
