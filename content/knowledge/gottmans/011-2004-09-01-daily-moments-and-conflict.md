## Key lessons

- Situation: Driver and Gottman asked whether ordinary, fleeting moments in a couple's day shape how they treat each other when they disagree. They observed 49 newlywed couples in a 10-minute dinnertime interaction and a 15-minute conflict discussion.
- What they did: they coded the conflict with the Specific Affect Coding System and built a new observational system for the dinnertime, then tested path models linking the two.
- What they found: a stronger path model when the direction ran from daily moments to the conflict discussion, meaning playfulness and enthusiasm at dinner related to humor and affection during conflict. They described this as preliminary support for the importance of daily moments.
- Boundary: the authors state the research "was strictly observational and therefore correlational" and that causation needs further research. Transferable lesson: small daily connection is a plausible investment, but no causal claim is proven here.
