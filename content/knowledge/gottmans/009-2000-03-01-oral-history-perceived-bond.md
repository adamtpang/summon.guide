## Key lessons

- Situation: Carrere, Buehlman, Gottman, Coan and Ruckstuhl followed 95 newlywed couples and asked whether an Oral History Interview, in which couples describe their history and philosophy of marriage, could predict who stays married.
- What they found: a principal-components analysis pulled out a latent variable they called "perceived marital bond". A discriminant function analysis of the interview data classified couples with 87.4% accuracy at the second data point and 81% at the third, within the first five years of marriage.
- The authors read this as support for causal links between perceptual bias, selective attention and the path of a marriage. That is their interpretation of a correlational design; the abstract does not report cross-validation on a new sample.
- Transferable lesson: the story partners tell about each other (fondness and shared history versus disappointment and chaos) shows up alongside outcomes. Rehearsing appreciation of what is good in the history is a reasonable practice, but the study does not show that doing so changes outcomes.
