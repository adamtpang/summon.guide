## Key lessons

- Situation: Gottman and Levenson studied 73 married couples in 1983 and again in 1987, measuring behavior, physiology and health, to see which processes go with a marriage that later dissolves.
- What they argued: using a "balance theory" of marriage, they split couples into regulated and nonregulated groups. A "cascade" model of dissolution, a stepwise scale of precursors, received what the authors called preliminary support.
- Facts from the abstract: compared with regulated couples, nonregulated couples had more severe marital problems, lower satisfaction, poorer health at the second time point, more negative and less positive emotional expression, more stubbornness and withdrawal, greater defensiveness, and a greater risk of dissolution (lower satisfaction, more thoughts of ending, more actual separation).
- Transferable lesson: the trouble is not that negative feelings appear but whether the couple can regulate them and keep positive expression in the mix. Note the size and age of the sample; "preliminary support" was the authors' own phrase.
