## Key lessons

- Situation: Gottman and Krokoff ran two longitudinal studies in which couples tried to resolve a high-conflict issue while researchers coded what they did. They compared what predicted how happy couples were at that moment with what predicted how satisfaction changed over three years.
- What they found: different behaviors answered the two questions. Disagreement and anger exchanges went with unhappiness and negativity at home in the present, yet they predicted improved satisfaction over the following years.
- Three patterns did predict deterioration over time: defensiveness (including whining), stubbornness, and withdrawal from interaction. The authors wrote "defensiveness (which includes whining), stubborness [sic], and withdrawal from interaction".
- Transferable lesson: a heated argument is not, by itself, a sign that a marriage is failing. Watch for the pattern where one person deflects, digs in, or shuts down, and treat that as the thing to change. This is a finding about groups of couples, not a rule for one couple, and the study is from the late 1980s.
