## Key lessons

- Situation: she is explaining how a room of free-moving small children can be orderly.
- What she argued: a person made silent and motionless is not disciplined but "annihilated". A disciplined person is master of himself and can follow a rule when needed. As she puts it: "Discipline must come through liberty."
- Concrete limits: the child's liberty ends at the collective interest and ordinary good manners. Whatever offends or hurts others, or is rough or ill-bred, is stopped. Whatever is useful should be allowed and watched. Dangerous or useless acts are suppressed.
- Lesson: aim for a child who can regulate themselves, not one who is merely quiet. The adult's job includes watching to tell which acts to stop and which to leave alone. Her own teachers found the watching role uncomfortable at first.
