## Key lessons

- Situation: she sets out how an adult presents an activity to a young child once the environment and materials are prepared.
- What she argued: lessons should be individual, short, simple, and objective, so the adult's personality recedes and the object is what the child notices. She borrows a line of Dante to make the point: count your words.
- Concrete rule: if the child does not take up the lesson, do not insist and do not repeat it, and take care "not to make the child feel that he has made a mistake". Pressure would change the natural state she wants to observe.
- Lesson: show once, say little, and let a puzzled or uninterested child be. Return later or try a different approach. Her setting is a classroom of three to seven year olds, so treat this as a principle to adapt at home, not a script.
