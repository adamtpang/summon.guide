## Key lessons

- Situation: she describes the furniture and setup of the children's houses: light tables and chairs small children can carry, low washstands a three-year-old can use, and low cupboards whose contents the children look after.
- What she argued: children should be able to act for themselves, and dependence on servants or attentive adults breeds helplessness and later a domineering habit. She names "the abolition of desks, and benches or stationary chairs" as the main change to the classroom.
- Concrete facts: children handled real objects and cared for the materials. She also reports dropping prizes and punishments after seeing a child wearing a silver cross ignore it while a punished child happily borrowed it.
- Lesson: make the everyday world reachable for a small child (low hooks, shelves, real tools), and let effort itself be the reward. Caution: her isolation of a disruptive child in a corner armchair, and her fainting-woman example, are dated.
