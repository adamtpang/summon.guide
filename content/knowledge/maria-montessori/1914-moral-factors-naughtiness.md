## Key lessons

- Situation: she addresses the old question of whether children are born good or evil, because critics feared that freedom would let children's bad tendencies run wild.
- What she argued: many tendencies adults call evil in children aged three to six are simply what annoys adults, such as touching everything and moving constantly. When children are prevented, they rebel, and that rebellion forms almost the whole of their naughtiness.
- Concrete claim: she reports that in her schools "the children have shown a love of work which no one suspected to be in them" once given the means to work, and that outbursts of rage gave way to outbursts of joy. Disorder is still stopped.
- Lesson: before punishing, ask what the child is trying to do and whether the environment gives them a way to do it. Her evidence is classroom observation in a prepared setting, not a controlled study, and she does not address serious behavior or mental health difficulties.
