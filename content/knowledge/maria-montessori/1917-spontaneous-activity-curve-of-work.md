## Key lessons

- Situation: she describes what a morning of freely chosen work looks like in a class of children who have come to concentrate.
- What she observed: a child starts with an easy task, then a harder one held for around half an hour, then seems restless and walks about, then takes on a demanding task and becomes deeply absorbed. At the end the child is calm, satisfied, and wants to share with an adult.
- Concrete claim: the middle restlessness looks like fatigue or disorder, but is a preparation for the main effort. A teacher who steps in and calls the children to rest, she says, leaves them restless and prevents the later work.
- Lesson: distinguish real tiredness or trouble from the pause before absorption, and be slow to interrupt a child who is settling in. This is her classroom observation from the early 1900s, so treat it as a hypothesis to watch for, not a rule.
