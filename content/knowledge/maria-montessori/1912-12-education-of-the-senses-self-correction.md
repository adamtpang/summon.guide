## Key lessons

- Situation: she describes a child working with a set of cylinders that fit only in matching holes, so an error shows itself when one piece is left over.
- What she argued: the value is in the child's own comparison and self-correction, not in reaching a correct result quickly. The teacher must not interfere, even when the child frowns and struggles, because the adult's help replaces the child's own effort.
- Concrete details: children repeat the exercise many times, from about five or six repeats to twenty or more. She records a four-year-old who repeated it sixteen times and kept going while other children sang around her.
- Lesson: when a child is absorbed and struggling with something achievable, wait. She notes that her old-style teachers found this hard because pity made them want to help. Her examples are materials she designed, not household tasks.
