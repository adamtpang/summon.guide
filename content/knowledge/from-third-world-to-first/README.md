# from-third-world-to-first

Original Summon syntheses, written 2026-10-01.

- Source: From Third World to First: The Singapore Story 1965-2000, Memoirs of Lee Kuan Yew (first published 2000; this copy is the eBook edition with an Author's Note dated August 2014 and a foreword by Henry A. Kissinger). Read from the owner's private library file 'bookbox.ink/.private-library/books/done!/from third world to first.md' (PDF extraction, 701 pages, about 260,000 words). Identity confirmed from the opening pages: title page, dedication to the old-guard colleagues, table of contents (Parts I to III, 43 chapters) and the Kissinger foreword.
- Read in full: no
- Parts read: Read in full: the title pages, contents, the start of the eBook Author's Note and the Foreword, plus eight chapters of Part I: 1 Going It Alone; 2 Building an Army from Scratch; 5 Creating a Financial Centre; 6 Winning Over the Unions; 7 A Fair, Not Welfare, Society; 10 Nurturing and Attracting Talent; 12 Keeping the Government Clean; 14 Managing the Media. One note per chapter.
- Parts not read: Everything else is not covered: the Preface and Acknowledgements; Part I chapters 3 (Britain Pulls Out), 4 (Surviving Without a Hinterland), 8 (The Communists Self-destruct), 9 (Straddling the Middle Ground), 11 (Many Tongues, One Language), 13 (Greening Singapore), 15 (Conductor of an Orchestra); all of Part II (chapters 16 to 40, regional and international relations); all of Part III (chapters 41 to 43); the photo captions and the index.

## Caveats

- This is a memoir: every figure, motive and judgment is Lee Kuan Yew's own account. Opponents' and critics' views appear only as he reports them.
- Detention without trial is not treated in depth in the eight chapters read. It appears only in passing (a 1959 reference to the Preservation of Public Security Ordinance and the 1987 arrest of 22 people in what Lee calls a Marxist conspiracy, both in chapter 14). The chapters most likely to discuss it (8 and 9) were not read, so no note covers it.
- Press controls and the libel suit against the Far Eastern Economic Review are reported from chapter 14 in neutral wording. Libel suits against opposition politicians are not discussed in the chapters read.
- Chapter 10 rests on Lee's claims that intelligence is about 80 per cent inherited (Minnesota twin studies, The Bell Curve). These claims are disputed, and the note reports them as his views, not as settled fact.
- The file is a rough PDF extraction with very long lines, missing hyphens in some compound words and photo captions mixed between chapters. The Read tool cannot page inside such lines, so the chapters were read by printing character ranges to the terminal. Nothing was copied to another file.
- Each note contains at most one short phrase in double quotes, taken from the text as extracted.

No source text is stored here. Every lesson is a paraphrase.
