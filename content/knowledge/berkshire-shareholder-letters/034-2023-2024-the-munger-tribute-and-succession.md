# The Munger Tribute and Succession

> **Key principle:** Buffett names Charlie Munger the architect of Berkshire's investing philosophy while, across these two letters, making Greg Abel's succession as CEO steadily more explicit and public.

*Synthesized from Berkshire Hathaway's 2023 and 2024 Chairman's Letters to shareholders, by Warren E. Buffett.*

## Key lessons

- The 2023 letter opens, before the standard "To the Shareholders" salutation, with a dedicated page titled "Charlie Munger – The Architect of Berkshire Hathaway," written after Munger died on November 28, 2023, just 33 days short of his 100th birthday.
- Buffett dates the relationship precisely: they first met in 1959, though Munger had grown up in Omaha; Munger only began managing money himself in 1962, and in 1965 told Buffett bluntly that buying control of Berkshire had been a "dumb decision," even though Munger had no money in the partnership that made the purchase.
- Munger's specific advice, in Buffett's own recollection, was to abandon Ben Graham's cigar-butt method of buying "fair businesses at wonderful prices" and instead build Berkshire by buying "wonderful businesses at fair prices" — the strategic pivot Buffett credits with reshaping how Berkshire was run for the rest of his career.
- Buffett's central metaphor for the partnership: Munger was the "architect" of the present Berkshire, while Buffett himself was only the "general contractor" carrying out the day-to-day construction of Munger's vision, and Munger deliberately let Buffett "take the bows" rather than claim credit.
- Buffett describes the relationship as "part older brother, part loving father" — Munger gave Buffett the reins even when he knew he was right, and never reminded Buffett of his mistakes after the fact.
- The 2024 letter adds a second, sharper memory: Munger's term for the one unforgivable failure in running a business was "thumb-sucking" — delaying the correction of a known mistake — with Munger's own line, "Problems... cannot be wished away."
- The 2024 letter also ranks Munger among Buffett's three luckiest strokes in building Berkshire, alongside the GEICO purchase and hiring Ajit Jain, calling Munger "a one-of-a-kind partner, personal advisor and steadfast friend."
- Succession becomes explicit well before the formal 2024 announcement: the 2023 letter's closing section (dated February 2024) previews the annual meeting stage as Greg Abel, Ajit Jain, and — pointedly — no longer Charlie Munger, and states outright that Abel, who runs all of Berkshire's non-insurance operations, "in all respects is ready to be CEO of Berkshire tomorrow."
- The 2024 letter makes the timeline explicit in Buffett's own words: "At 94, it won't be long before Greg Abel replaces me as CEO and will be writing the annual letters," adding that Abel shares the same commitment to writing owners a full "report" rather than a promotional letter.
- The 2024 letter narrows the succession spotlight further in its meeting-format description: Greg Abel and Ajit Jain join Buffett for the first stretch of shareholder Q&A, but after the mid-morning break "only Greg will join me on stage" for the closing session.
- Abel's operating record is cited as evidence, not just title: he traveled with Buffett to Tokyo to meet the management of Berkshire's five Japanese trading-company holdings (Itochu, Marubeni, Mitsubishi, Mitsui, Sumitomo), has since met with them repeatedly on his own, and Buffett states he expects "Greg and his eventual successors" to hold the Japanese position for decades.
- Beyond the Munger and Abel material, the letters carry real operating specifics: 2023 insurance underwriting swung to a $5.4 billion profit from a $30 million loss in 2022, while 2024 operating earnings reached $47.4 billion, aided by GEICO's turnaround under Todd Combs and a record federal tax payment of $26.8 billion — about 5% of all U.S. corporate income tax paid that year.
- Insurance float, built under Ajit Jain since he joined in 1986, is reported growing from $46 billion to $171 billion over the prior two decades, with $32 billion of cumulative after-tax underwriting profit over the same span.
- BNSF and Berkshire Hathaway Energy are both singled out as 2023 disappointments — BNSF's earnings fell as wage costs from federally mediated negotiations outran revenue, while BHE absorbed forest-fire liability losses and faced a shifting utility-regulation climate that Buffett calls "a costly mistake" not to have anticipated.
- The 2024 letter memorializes Pete Liegl, founder of RV maker Forest River, who died in November 2024 still working at 80; Buffett recounts negotiating Liegl's purchase and compensation directly in 2005, when Liegl set his own salary at $100,000 a year plus a bonus tied to earnings growth — offered as one of Buffett's examples of trusting an owner-operator completely.
- Both letters close with reflections tying Buffett's own long tenure to Berkshire's identity: the 2023 letter closes on Munger's and Buffett's shared Omaha roots and Bertie's decades of patient, low-turnover investing; the 2024 letter closes by describing a commemorative book, "60 Years of Berkshire Hathaway," built around Munger's photos, quotes, and previously unpublished stories, sold to fund a South Omaha homeless shelter that Buffett personally matches.
