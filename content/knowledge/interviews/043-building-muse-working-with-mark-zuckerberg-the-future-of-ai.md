# Building Muse, Working with Mark Zuckerberg & The Future of AI | Alexandr Wang, Meta

> **Key principle (Alexandr Wang):** Wang describes Muse, Meta's personal agent, as the product of a contrarian bet: while the industry crowded into coding agents, Meta Superintelligence Labs aimed its whole model roadmap at personal agents, the consumer territory he believes Meta's heritage and distribution make it uniquely suited to win. He credits the result to restraint more than speed: a sub-200-person team with one dominant point of view, a spreadsheet of more than a hundred required behaviors with launch-blocking thresholds, and seven months of sanding before release. He contrasts this with Scale AI, where he was the auteur in an operational "skyscraper" business, and his role now as steward of an environment for researchers in a "diamond mining" business.

## Key lessons

- **Take early adopters' magic moments seriously, then prototype in days.** Wang said Nat Friedman was the first in the lab to go all in on the open-source personal agent OpenClaw, describing an experience between terrifying and euphoric. Given Friedman's record as an early Stripe customer and a creator of GitHub Copilot, Wang treated that as a signal, immersed himself for weeks, and in February the two wrote memos to Meta's board, including Friedman's "The Claw is the Law" and "Trust is a Must." The team had a working prototype in one to two weeks, and many final Muse features, including its mascot, were already in that first demo.
- **Win where you are uniquely positioned, not where the industry is crowding.** Wang said the conventional wisdom early in 2026 was that coding agents were the only capability that mattered, with Anthropic far ahead. He had told Mark Zuckerberg, before the Scale deal, that if AI handles what people have to do, Meta's apps already represent what people want to do. Combined with distribution to 3.5 billion daily users, that made personal and consumer AI the natural zone for Meta.
- **The unglamorous part is the moat: sand out every detail.** Wang praised OpenClaw's creator, Peter Steinberger, as a visionary, but said the category faded because the experience was magical sometimes and broken most of the time. Muse's edge was grinding: a spreadsheet of more than a hundred specific behaviors a personal agent needs, evals for each, and recurring reviews targeting the weakest rows. He described this as an AI-era version of Meta's culture of optimizing every step of onboarding and conversion.
- **Define a numerical launch bar and do not blink.** Each row in that spreadsheet had a launch-blocking threshold and a "good" threshold. Wang said many checkpoints were green on 80 rows and red on 20, which was not enough; only when a specially trained model checkpoint went green across the board, and felt clearly better in hands-on use, did they ship, seven months after the prototype.
- **Patience in AI time requires leadership cover.** Senra cited Jeremy Stern's Colossus profile, where Zuckerberg describes his unsexy edge as building teams and improving a product slowly over a long period without losing control. Wang credited Zuckerberg's restraint through months when Wall Street questioned Meta's AI spending, calling seven months in AI possibly a decade in normal time.
- **Keep the team small so the product has one point of view.** Muse was built by fewer than 200 people across model and product work, despite being seen internally as one of Meta's most important launches of the year. Wang warned that big organizations produce a Frankenstein of every PM's priorities blended together, and said if any single person's taste came through in Muse, it was Friedman's.
- **Show the benefit, not the brand.** Wang said a team meeting concluded Meta's distribution was not enough for Muse to break through a torrent of AI releases. He started posting memes himself, often off the cuff, recalling that the internet rewards risk and surprise, and the jolly mascot proved endlessly adaptable. He retweeted users' wow moments heavily, and he and Senra agreed most people do not care about labs or research, only what a product does for them. Senra connected this to Claude Hopkins's "Scientific Advertising" and noted that modern consumer products must work as screenshots shared in group chats.
- **Onboard users into a trust fall.** Wang described a retention loop where users hand the agent a small task, see it succeed, then give it something bigger, escalating as trust builds. Because most people have only used chatbots, the graduation to an agent has to be explained deliberately, and A/B tests showed the experience is highly sensitive to model reliability and to how the agent checks back with users.
- **Frame AI as an escalator of agency.** Wang said Muse can give people the adulthood they dreamed of as children, before work and commitments turn them into what he called zombies. In a short post he argued the world has been shaped by a few fanatics who made their wants real, and asked what happens when everyone's agency is switched on.
- **Recognize when a "partnership" pitch is a talent bet.** Wang said an intro through Meta's Alex Schultz took about a year to become a first meeting. After Llama 4 disappointed internally, Zuckerberg called asking for advice, and Wang initially doubted a deal made sense because Scale was an enterprise and government business. The structure that emerged, Meta owning 49 percent of Scale with Scale continuing and Wang plus a few people joining Meta, came together over roughly five to six weeks. He said giving up the company he considered his life's work for nine years was emotionally hard, but rebuilding a lab seen as damaged goods appealed to the founder in him.
- **Match management style to the payoff distribution.** Wang called Scale a skyscraper business (like data annotation or package delivery) with linear payoffs where you squeeze out efficiency, and said he was the auteur there, able to do every job and also the bottleneck. A research lab is diamond mining, where most ideas fail and a few pay off enormously, so he hires the best people, sets a north star (personal superintelligence) and protects their room to work. He recalled that his 19th birthday party, during an internship at the trading firm HRT, included future founders of Hyperliquid, Cognition and Decagon.
- **Flatten to fight bureaucracy.** He said more than 200 researchers in the lab's TBD group report directly to him, a deliberate anti-bureaucracy statement. Technical leads of pods break ties on technical decisions, and big choices are debated as a group, because brilliant people need an environment rather than managers.
