# Lessons From Backing The Best Founders In Fintech | Micky Malka

> **Key principle (Micky Malka):** Malka rejects fixed labels for himself, entrepreneur, investor, on purpose, describing himself instead as "an entrepreneur at heart, an investor by design" so he can keep learning without a mold to defend. His central operating idea is the infinite game: there is no winning or losing, only being ahead or behind, and he says he learns more and performs better when he is behind, because being ahead tends to breed complacency rather than the innovation that got him there.

*David Senra in conversation with Micky Malka, founder of Ribbit Capital. [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CjLhd1WZwTE) · 76 min · published 2026-08-02.*

## Key lessons

- **Writing forces conviction that talking never does.** Malka said Ribbit's investment essays, some taking a year or more to write, start at twenty pages but should be explainable on a napkin once the thinking is actually done. He said the essays are shared publicly with founders and LPs specifically so people can hold the firm accountable to a thesis, rather than letting conviction drift with market mood swings.
- **Study a pattern of decision-making, not the decisions themselves.** Malka bought his first share of Berkshire Hathaway at 13 (borrowing the money from his grandfather, who charged him interest "to teach me") and said what he learned from decades of Buffett's letters was not any single call but the same repeatable discipline for filtering noise down to the few things that matter, regardless of whether inflation, rates, or the wider economy had changed.
- **Money is the next frontier for AI, and it will arrive through founder-led incumbents, not startups.** Malka described every company becoming a "token factory" that ingests information, value, and intelligence, and said money has lagged behind knowledge in this shift for years. He said he expects Stripe, Robinhood, Revolut, and Nubank, not new entrants, to lead this next stage precisely because their founders are still running the company day to day with "day one" hunger.
- **Treat every outcome as ahead or behind, never as winning or losing.** Malka said this framing removes fear of missing out and lets him evaluate his own performance independent of anyone else's. He specifically said he performs better when behind, because being ahead triggers an instinct to change the rules on himself before complacency sets in.
- **Understand founder DNA well enough to be the person they call when they cannot sleep.** Malka said Ribbit spends most of its relationship-building time earning the right to get that call, and that the job on the call is never to answer the founder's question for them, only to help them think it through. He cited a decade of watching Nick Storonsky (Revolut) work the same relentless, mission-first way from his twenties into his thirties as the kind of behavioral consistency Ribbit looks for across an entire organization, not just the founder.
- **A single early insight can pay off more than a decade later, through a completely different company.** Malka built Lemon Bank in Brazil starting in 2003, a hybrid of in-store kiosks and franchise locations that banked 15 million previously unbanked customers before selling to a major Brazilian bank in 2008. Twelve years later, that same underlying insight, that a large retailer's existing foot traffic is an unbanked-customer opportunity, became the basis for OnePay, the joint venture Ribbit built with Walmart that now runs Walmart's entire consumer financial product line.
- **Silicon Valley lost its feel for beauty, and getting it back is a competitive advantage.** Malka argued the last fifteen years optimized purely for scale and efficiency at the expense of how a product makes someone feel, citing Dyson products and the iPhone SE as rare recent examples people still love. He said founders who can restore genuine taste and authenticity to their products, not just their marketing, are the ones who will win the next generation of company building.
- **Codify the founder traits you actually look for, in language simple enough to fit on a napkin.** Ribbit's internal filter, developed over roughly a decade, blends a scientist's energy, a missionary's conviction, a partner's heart, an athlete's drive, and an owner's obsession. Malka said authenticity is the deciding factor among founders who otherwise look similar: the ones who stay true to their original mission are the ones worth staying in business with for decades.
- **Reputation is the only asset that survives everything else.** Malka called reputation the only asset that truly matters, citing how the recorded Buffett deposition on protecting Berkshire's reputation over money was replayed at every annual meeting he attended starting at age 18. He said his own decision to meet with someone he had never met, based purely on that person's reputation among people he already trusted, is a direct expression of the same principle.
- **Refuse a single deal owner; let the whole team share the relationship.** Malka said Ribbit deliberately avoids assigning one partner as the point of contact for a portfolio company, so founders call whoever they need for a given question, and the entire team shares visibility into calendars, inboxes, and decisions. He called this structure, along with running the firm on internal Star Wars-themed team names, a direct expression of refusing to be "labeled" as a conventional investment firm.
