# Managing $525B & Building the World’s Largest Multifamily Office | Kurt MacAlpine, Corient

> **Key principle (Kurt MacAlpine):** MacAlpine argues that wealth management was built on a flawed structure, advisors operating as competing independent contractors under a shared brand, and that the client is the biggest loser of that design. Corient's answer was to import the professional services partnership (the model of McKinsey, law firms and accounting firms) into ultra-high-net-worth wealth management, then audit every element of the business model, compensation, P&L, brand and integration, to remove anything that creates silos. He describes his real obsession not as incentives but as alignment, strategic, cultural and financial, and he defends that design with conviction even when public markets punish it.

*David Senra in conversation with Kurt MacAlpine, partner and CEO of Corient. [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u1cyLVmRMx8) · 80 min · published 2026-10-18.*

## Key lessons

- **Pick an industry with tailwinds, white space and a broken operating model.** MacAlpine said three things made ultra-high-net-worth wealth management attractive when he became CEO of CI Financial at 39: more wealth, more concentrated, more complex, more global and changing generations faster than ever; a "barbell" structure with bureaucratic big banks at one end and subscale local firms at the other, leaving white space in the middle; and an operating model he considered fundamentally flawed. He had spent ten years as a McKinsey consultant and about four and a half as an asset management operator before he got the chance to act on that view.
- **Diagnose the incentive tensions that make the client lose.** He described three layers of conflict in a typical wealth firm: advisors competing with each other for clients inside the same firm; specialists such as tax or estate planners treated as a cost center the advisor hesitates to bring into a meeting; and advisors paid to generate revenue and hoard it, while support staff are paid on firm results. The outcome, he said, is that firms become platforms individuals plug into rather than companies, and the client only ever gets the expertise of whoever originated the relationship.
- **Remove every structural barrier to collaboration on day one.** Corient runs about 3,000 people on one compensation plan with no exceptions, one P&L across the firm and no internal chargebacks, all as a fee-only fiduciary. MacAlpine's reasoning was that once two colleagues are paid differently, or a region loses revenue when a client consolidates assets elsewhere, they cannot serve the client objectively. He walked through a billion-dollar family moving assets to Miami at a multi-jurisdictional bank, where three advisors' pay goes to zero and one quadruples, as the reason most "global" firms are really regional P&Ls.
- **Offer services that are not profit centers if they deepen the relationship.** Corient embeds more than 100 accountants on advisory teams, runs trust companies, a family office, bill pay, concierge lending, art management and even an aviation business that owns aircraft for clients' shared use, taking the balance sheet risk itself. Senra compared it to Costco-style shared economies of scale; MacAlpine framed the goal as connecting the dots across financial planning, wealth transfer, tax and investments so the client does not carry that burden alone.
- **Make the structure your screening mechanism.** Because the model is so different, MacAlpine said Corient tends to be someone's first choice or their last, rarely second. Advisors who want ownership and collaboration gravitate to it; those wanting to recreate their old silo self-select out. He said the firm skews young as a result: the industry's median advisor is in their early 60s, while Corient's median partner is in their mid-40s and median employee in their 30s, because it is doing acquisitions to add partners with long runways, not to fund retirements.
- **Acquire for alignment, not assets under management.** Corient has done close to 50 acquisitions, and MacAlpine said he never screens on AUM or scale, only on strategic, cultural and financial fit. Scale still benefits partners: past roughly a few billion dollars, clients demand services that are expensive to build, so joining gives a firm those capabilities immediately instead of betting a decade of investment on future assets.
- **Integrate fully, and let people who have lived it sell it.** On day one an acquired firm moves to Corient's brand, technology, offices, compensation and benefits. MacAlpine said he expected partners to prefer the early, loosely integrated phase, but they are most comfortable with full integration because they know exactly what they are getting and can call any of 3,000 colleagues to ask what life is like.
- **Give people a real voice in emotional decisions.** To name the firm, he held a town hall, said he did not care what the name would be, and invited anyone to join focus groups; about 75 people did. Finalists were mocked up in identical branding so no logo image biased the vote, and the partner group chose the name live. His point was that people embrace change when they can see their input reflected in the result.
- **Hold conviction when the share price disagrees with the business.** He said CI was one of the best performing North American financial stocks in 2021 and among the worst in 2022, which he tied to a float about 75 percent held by active hedge funds and value mutual funds facing redemptions. He funded the build with free cash flow plus roughly three to three and a half turns of debt, emphasizing it was fixed-rate, covenant-free and long-dated, some out to 30 years. Rather than retreat, CI bought back shares he called "criminally undervalued," many at around 12 to 13 dollars.
- **Go private when the public structure blocks the strategy.** Mubadala Capital called inbound, estimating it would take about nine years to buy back the shares he wanted, with trade-offs to the Corient build along the way. The take-private was announced in November 2024 and completed August 12, 2025, with no added debt, no cost synergies and no change in strategy. Within weeks, Corient announced acquisitions of what he described as the two largest ultra-high-net-worth wealth managers in Europe, a move he said would have been impossible as a public company.
- **Build a brand on a forever time horizon.** MacAlpine advertises in every market Corient operates in, favoring live business television and sports where viewers are focused, plus micro-targeted digital and client events. Senra noticed the firm as the sole sponsor across four ad breaks of one program. MacAlpine said temporary owners think in 18 to 36 month increments and optimize for exit, while a forever owner can invest in brand ahead of any measurable return.
- **Start from a blank sheet and study every predecessor.** He chose Miami as headquarters at the end of 2020 precisely because Corient had no legacy ties anywhere, citing former mayor Francis Suarez's personal role in welcoming companies. He applied the same approach to the partnership itself, setting the goal that its structure and features be better than any partnership that came before, by studying what worked and failed in other industries.
