# Chapter 12: The "Most Feared" Hedge Fund

> **Key principle:** Starboard Value, the activist hedge fund led by Jeff Smith, took a stake in Nvidia in 2013 when its stock was flat and pushed for buybacks; years later, the same fund's pressure on Mellanox triggered a bidding war that Nvidia won, delivering the InfiniBand networking technology that would prove essential to its AI data-center business.

*Synthesized from Chapter 12 of The Nvidia Way by Tae Kim.*

## Key lessons

- Jeff Smith founded Starboard Value in 2011 out of Ramius Capital/Cowen, building a reputation as the "most feared" activist investor for campaigns like the nearly 300-slide Darden Restaurants turnaround pitch (which even targeted free breadsticks) that replaced Darden's entire board in 2014.
- In mid-2013, with Nvidia's stock flat for four years and its market cap only about $8 billion against $3 billion in net cash, Starboard bought a 4.4-million-share stake (about $62 million) betting the core GPU business was undervalued once cash was backed out.
- Nvidia executives worried Starboard would force a board seat and push cuts to CUDA investment; one executive described tension peaking at "DEFCON 3," not a full crisis, and Starboard exited by March 2014 after Nvidia committed to a $1 billion buyback (plus another $1 billion authorized) in November 2013, which drove the stock up about 20 percent.
- Separately, Starboard took an 11 percent stake in Mellanox (an Israeli networking company behind the InfiniBand standard) in January 2017, criticizing years of weak stock performance despite a 470 percent rise in the sector index, and pushed the company toward a board shake-up or sale.
- Mellanox drew a multi-bidder auction in 2018 between Nvidia, Intel, and Xilinx; Nvidia won on March 7, 2019, with an all-cash offer of $125/share ($6.9 billion total), narrowly beating Intel and Xilinx's roughly $122.50 bids.
- Jensen framed the deal publicly around the idea that "the entire data center would become the computer," predicting AI workloads would require tens of thousands of servers networked together, with Mellanox's InfiniBand technology as the critical link.
- By May 2024, the former Mellanox business was generating over $12 billion in annualized revenue (up from $3.2 billion quarterly, more than seven times its last quarter as a standalone public company), described by a Nvidia executive as "frankly a wonderful thing thrown in our lap by activists."
- Smith himself, looking back after Nvidia's rise, said of Starboard's early exit from its Nvidia stake: "We never should have exited the position."
