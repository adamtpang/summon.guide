# Appendix: Jensen-isms

> **Key principle:** The appendix collects the short, repeatable phrases Jensen uses across meetings to transmit Nvidia's operating philosophy without having to re-explain it each time, covering frugality, meeting discipline, hiring, feedback, strategy, and standards.

*Synthesized from the Appendix of The Nvidia Way by Tae Kim.*

## Key lessons

- Meeting and resource discipline: "As many as needed, as few as possible" keeps meetings limited to people who genuinely need to be there, and "AMAN, ALAP" (as much as needed, as little as possible) applies the same frugality to time and company resources generally.
- Hiring and feedback: "Always hire the best" reflects a bet that smart, capable people can adapt to any new problem, while "Criticism is a gift" frames blunt, direct feedback as a benefit to the recipient, not an attack, and "Is it world class?" requires benchmarking people, products, and practices against the best in the industry, not just against Nvidia's own past.
- Standards over vanity metrics: "Don't worry about the score. Worry about how you play the game" tells employees to ignore stock-price swings and focus on the work itself, while "If you measure it, you can improve it, but you have to measure the right thing!" warns against optimizing for the wrong metric.
- Physical and competitive absolutes: "Speed of Light" sets the goal as the theoretical physics-based maximum, not comparison to competitors or the company's own history, and "Second place is the first loser" makes the expectation to win, not merely compete, explicit.
- Manufacturing pragmatism: "Floor sweeping" and "Ship the whole cow" describe designing chips with built-in redundancy so that even defective units can be resold as lower-tier, still-profitable parts rather than scrapped.
- Strategy as subtraction: "Strategy is about the things you give up" and "Small steps, big vision" both push toward ruthless prioritization, doing the single most important thing well rather than spreading effort, while "Let's go back to first principles" pushes teams to rebuild a problem from scratch rather than default to precedent.
- Market-creation over market-share thinking: "We don't steal market share, we create markets" captures Nvidia's preference for building categories with no existing competitors rather than fighting incumbents for a slice of an existing one, echoing the "$0 billion market" logic behind bets like CUDA.
- Team and mission framing: "The mission is the boss" subordinates internal politics to customer-serving decisions, "No one loses alone" requires flagging trouble early so teammates can help, "Pilot-in-Command" designates a single accountable leader who gets priority company support on a given project, and "Your strength is your weakness" warns that excessive kindness or tact can itself become an obstacle to progress.
