# Chapter 1: Pain and Suffering

> **Key principle:** Jensen's parents, immigrants from Taiwan chasing the American dream, unknowingly enrolled him at age nine in a reform school for troubled teens in Oneida, Kentucky, believing it was a prep school. There, bullied and rooming with an older, tattooed ex-convict, Jensen learned to fight, to clean toilets without complaint, and to tolerate suffering. He carried that ethic through table tennis, a Denny's diner job, and an engineering career, arriving at his lifelong creed: "I wish upon you ample doses of pain and suffering."

*Synthesized from Chapter 1 of The Nvidia Way by Tae Kim.*

## Key lessons

- Jensen's family moved from Taiwan to Thailand to escape unrest, then sent Jensen and his older brother to live with an aunt and uncle in Tacoma before enrolling them, sight unseen, in what turned out to be the Oneida Baptist Institute, a Kentucky reform school founded to separate feuding families, not a boarding prep school.
- At Oneida, young and an ethnic minority, Jensen was beaten up often; he eventually taught his intimidating older roommate to read in exchange for weightlifting lessons, crediting that period for his "street-fighter mentality": "I will never start a fight, but I will never walk away from one."
- He discovered table tennis at Portland's Paddle Palace, earning a mention in Sports Illustrated at fourteen as the "most promising junior" in the Pacific Northwest, but choked at a Las Vegas tournament after staying up all night on the Strip, a failure he says he still regrets decades later.
- At fifteen, painfully shy, Jensen took a job at a Denny's, working up from dishwasher and bathroom cleaner to waiter; he says the job "pulled him out of his shell" and taught him to find pride in menial tasks done to the highest standard, down to trying to out-carry every other server on cups of coffee.
- He skipped grades twice, graduated Aloha High School at sixteen, studied electrical engineering at Oregon State (choosing it partly for in-state tuition), and used the pickup line "Do you want to see my homework?" on his future wife, Lori Mills, one of only three women in a 250-person engineering class.
- After being rejected by Texas Instruments, he joined AMD to design chips by day while pursuing a Stanford master's degree at night, a process that took eight years around work and a growing family: "I have a very long-term horizon... I can plug away."
- Restless with AMD's manual chip-design process, he moved to LSI Logic, where he was assigned to a secretive Sun Microsystems project and met two engineers, Curtis Priem and Chris Malachowsky, setting up the partnership that would become Nvidia. The chapter closes on his defining maxim: greatness comes not from intelligence but from character built through pain, and his advice to anyone chasing success is "ample doses of pain and suffering."
