# Introduction

> **Key principle:** Kim argues that Nvidia's success cannot be explained by technical prowess, financial resources, or foresight alone. Its real engine is an organizational culture, forged entirely by Jensen Huang, that combines maximum independence for each employee with maximum standards, treats complacency (not any competitor) as the company's true enemy, and demands, in Jensen's own words, "sheer will."

*Synthesized from the Introduction of The Nvidia Way by Tae Kim.*

## Key lessons

- Jensen's preferred tool is the whiteboard, not the slide deck: every conference room at Nvidia has one, and colleagues call him "Professor Jensen" because whiteboarding forces real-time thinking with nowhere to hide, a symbol of the company's belief that even a brilliant idea must eventually be erased for a new one.
- Kim frames the book's origin scene at Caltech's 2024 commencement, four days before Nvidia became the world's most valuable company; Jensen deadpans to Kim's story about profiting from Nvidia stock, "Nvidia is my first big winner too."
- Jensen resists nostalgia about Nvidia's founding and insists the company "wasn't a great company on day one," listing the NV1 and NV2 as products built before Nvidia was "great," and says of the culture's origin, "we survived ourselves. We were our own worst enemy."
- Nvidia was founded by three men, not one: Jensen Huang, Curtis Priem, and Chris Malachowsky, in a back booth at a Denny's in 1993; Kim insists Priem's chip-architecture prowess and Malachowsky's manufacturing expertise were as essential as Jensen's business acumen, even though the book, and the culture, centers on Jensen.
- The culture's central insight, in Jensen's words, is that companies rot from internal politics, not external competition, as employees start optimizing for their bosses' careers instead of the mission; his answer is a flat structure and public accountability, "I've got no trouble calling people out. You do that once or twice, nobody's going to go near that again."
- Kim reports that despite the culture's intensity, former employees rarely become dissenters, citing the relief of direct, blunt communication versus the office politics they encountered elsewhere.
- The book closes its frame by asking whether Nvidia can be separated from its CEO at all: Jensen has run the company for his entire adult life, and Kim leaves open whether "the Nvidia Way" survives him, since "a whiteboard is only as useful as the person who holds the marker."
