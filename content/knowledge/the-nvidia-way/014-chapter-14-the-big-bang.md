# Chapter 14: The Big Bang

> **Key principle:** Nvidia's blockbuster May 2023 earnings guidance, which Wall Street analysts called unprecedented, was the visible surface of years of quiet preparation: manufacturing-capacity buildouts with partners, early bets on the Transformer architecture, and a decades-old pricing philosophy that let Nvidia's average selling prices rise rather than fall.

*Synthesized from Chapter 14 of The Nvidia Way by Tae Kim.*

## Key lessons

- On May 24, 2023, Nvidia guided second-quarter revenue to $11 billion versus Wall Street's $7.18 billion estimate, a roughly $4 billion beat; trader Connors Manguino's reaction ("Holy shit. What a raise!") illustrates how the market was caught off guard even after ChatGPT's late-2022 launch had already made AI a media sensation.
- Analysts called it unprecedented: Bernstein's Stacy Rasgon titled his note "The Big Bang" and called the guide "cosmological," while veteran investor Gavin Baker said he'd never seen a beat at that scale, comparing it to Google's and Facebook's biggest historic earnings surprises. Nvidia shares rose 24 percent the next day, adding $184 billion in market value in a single day.
- Nvidia wasn't lucky, according to Jeff Fisher: it had anticipated AI demand years ahead, sending "tiger teams" into manufacturing partners like Foxconn, Wistron, and TSMC to add capacity and compress the chip release cadence from two years to one.
- Jensen had pushed engineers to build a "Transformer Engine" into the Hopper architecture (released 2022, a month before ChatGPT) after grasping the significance of Google's 2017 "Attention Is All You Need" paper almost immediately, well before rivals had comparable Transformer-optimized hardware.
- Nvidia's pricing philosophy, dating back to Jensen overruling a proposed $10 price for the 1997 RIVA 128 in favor of $15 ("I think that's too cheap"), has meant average selling prices climb over time rather than fall like typical commodity hardware; the flagship Blackwell GB200 server system now costs $2-3 million.
- Jensen compared his long-horizon instinct to Netflix's Reed Hastings anticipating streaming, saying he was convinced as early as the 1990s that gaming would explode; more recently he named "digital biology" (AI-driven drug and protein design, illustrated via start-up Generate:Biomedicines) as the next major frontier after AI.
- The chapter closes by noting the main risk to Nvidia's run is the AI "scaling laws" eventually hitting diminishing returns on model size, compute, and data (roughly 60 percent of Nvidia's data-center GPUs were then sold for training versus 40 percent for inference), timing unknown, but frames Nvidia as historically prepared to adapt to whatever comes next.
