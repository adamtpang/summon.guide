# Technological Supermen and Values

> **Key principle:** These sections shift the manifesto's register from describing forces at the level of civilization to prescribing behavior at the level of the individual reader: build technical skill, embrace a specific list of virtues, and treat material abundance not as an end in itself but as the thing that frees people to pursue their own religion, politics, and definition of a meaningful life.

*Synthesized from the "Becoming Technological Supermen," "Technological Values," and "The Meaning of Life" sections of "The Techno-Optimist Manifesto" by Marc Andreessen, published at a16z.com, October 2023.*

## Key lessons

- "Becoming Technological Supermen": he calls for readers to actively transform themselves through technical education, hands-on building skill, and teamwork, framing the technological frontier as still wide open in a way the physical geographic frontier no longer is, and explicitly rejects a victim-of-technology posture in favor of one where humans are technology's masters, not its subjects.
- "Technological Values": he lists a long set of virtues the manifesto endorses, among them ambition, aggression, persistence, merit, achievement, bravery, pride, free thought and speech, the scientific method, individualism, competition, and what he calls "radical competence," and explicitly favors abundance and cheapness over scarcity and expense as economic values.
- He draws a distinction between intrinsic and extrinsic motivation, favoring the intrinsic rewards of creation, teamwork, and self-improvement over pure external reward, and uses the Greek phrase "eudaimonia through arete," flourishing through excellence, to summarize the kind of satisfaction he thinks this value set produces.
- He describes these values as universalist, meaning indifferent to race, gender, religion, nationality, or other demographic traits, and separately argues the United States and its allied liberal democracies should remain technologically dominant specifically to secure global security and liberty, tying the individual values section back to a geopolitical stake.
- "The Meaning of Life": he positions techno-optimism explicitly as a material philosophy rather than a political one, neither inherently left nor right, arguing material abundance is what actually gives people the freedom to choose their own religion, politics, and lifestyle rather than having those choices constrained by scarcity.
- Framing technology as liberatory, expanding the range of human potential and the very definitions of freedom and fulfillment available to a person, is the closing move of this cluster of sections, it reframes everything covered earlier in the manifesto, markets, AI, energy, abundance, as instrumental to this deeper, individual-level payoff rather than as ends in themselves.
- Moving from civilizational-scale claims (Technology, Markets, Intelligence, Energy, Abundance) to a personal values list is a deliberate manifesto structure: it asks the reader not just to believe the argument but to adopt a specific identity and behavior in response to it, which is what distinguishes a manifesto from an essay making the same underlying claims.
