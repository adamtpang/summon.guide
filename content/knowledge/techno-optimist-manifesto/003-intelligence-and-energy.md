# Intelligence and Energy

> **Key principle:** These two sections carry the manifesto's most concrete technology bets. Intelligence, meaning both artificial and human-augmented intelligence, is framed as the ultimate engine of progress and problem-solving, with a notably aggressive claim that slowing AI development costs lives that could otherwise have been saved. Energy is framed as the foundation everything else depends on, with nuclear fission specifically named as the available, underused solution being blocked by the very precautionary instincts the manifesto argues against elsewhere.

*Synthesized from the "Intelligence" and "Energy" sections of "The Techno-Optimist Manifesto" by Marc Andreessen, published at a16z.com, October 2023.*

## Key lessons

- Intelligence: Andreessen positions intelligence, not any single specific technology, as the ultimate engine of progress, and frames AI in near-alchemical terms, a general-purpose tool for solving problems across medicine, science, and everyday life that he believes could save enormous numbers of lives if developed and deployed without unnecessary delay.
- He states one of the manifesto's most deliberately provocative claims here: framing the deliberate slowing or prevention of AI development as a form of murder, on the logic that lives which could have been saved by AI-driven medical or scientific progress are instead lost to the delay, a claim meant to invert the usual framing of AI risk discourse rather than soften it.
- He champions both artificial intelligence and what he calls augmented intelligence, humans working symbiotically with machine tools, as complementary rather than competing paths to the same end, more capability applied to more problems.
- Energy: he treats abundant, cheap energy as foundational to essentially every other form of civilizational progress, arguing that expanding energy production, not conserving or rationing it, is the correct response to rising demand.
- Nuclear fission is named specifically as what he calls a "silver bullet" for achieving zero-emissions power at scale, explicitly invoking the concept behind Nixon-era energy independence proposals, and he treats nuclear fusion as a further, longer-horizon solution behind it rather than a replacement for pursuing fission now.
- He directly rejects the premise that technological and energy growth are inherently harmful to the environment, arguing instead that continued technological advancement is what actually improves environmental outcomes over time, a claim that sets up his later, more explicit attack on "sustainability" and the "precautionary principle" as ideas he treats as enemies of progress.
- Placing Intelligence and Energy together, rather than separately among the manifesto's many short sections, signals these are the two technologies he considers most consequential and most current, AI and nuclear power are the twin concrete bets underneath the manifesto's otherwise abstract optimism.
