The drawings themselves are not in the text extraction, so diagram-dependent passages could only be read as text.

## Caveats

- This is the copyrighted 2005 Suh compilation, not the Richter edition on Project Gutenberg, so the Gutenberg URL was not used. youtube_url points to a neutral catalogue record for this exact edition (Open Library, ISBN 9781579129460). The edition's wording often resembles older public-domain translations, but the book credits no translator, so that link is unconfirmed.
- The file was read through shell output in fixed-width slices, not the Read tool, because its lines run to tens of thousands of characters and exceed the Read tool's limit. Nothing was copied or written to disk.
- The edition is a thematic anthology: the editor chose, cut and reordered passages from many manuscripts and gives no manuscript references, so the notes cannot cite codex or folio.
- Notes 002, 005 and 006 each combine two related chapters. The task asked for one note per section; these groupings were needed to cover eleven chapters in eight notes.
- The brief allows one bullet of the note-writer's own application per note; only note 003 has one, labelled 'Applied today'. The closing sentence of the last bullet in notes 002 and 005, and of the Devatdar bullet in 005, are the note-writer's cautions about accuracy, not Leonardo's statements.
- Leonardo's science is of its time. His accounts of water rising in mountains, of a mother's soul shaping the fetus, of force, and the bestiary lore are reported as his views, not as facts.
- Hard material is present and reported neutrally: detailed instructions for painting battle and flood scenes, dissection of corpses, a letter offering weapons of war, and fables that end in death.
- The letters to the Devatdar of Syria about Mount Taurus appear without editorial explanation; whether they describe a real journey is not settled by this book.
- Biographical statements in the editor's introductions (dates, self-taught background, unfinished horse commission) were not used in the notes, which draw only on Leonardo's own text.
