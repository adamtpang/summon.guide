## Key lessons

- He compares people who love practice without knowledge to a sailor who boards a ship with no rudder or compass and cannot know where he is heading. For drawing, he names perspective as the guide and gateway to sound theory.
- He describes perspective as looking at a scene through a flat, clear sheet of glass and marking on it what lies behind. Images reach the eye in converging pyramids, and he calls the eye "the universal judge of all objects".
- He divides perspective into three branches: objects shrink with distance, their colors change, and their outlines lose finish. He gives a rule that an object twice as far away appears half the size.
- He names aerial perspective: distant buildings and mountains look bluer and less defined because of the air in between, so a painter should make a building five times farther away five times bluer. Thin parts vanish first, so a distant horse loses its legs before its trunk.
- He proposes a way to calibrate this by eye: trace trees at measured intervals of a hundred braccia on a pane of glass, match their color, and keep the results as a standard for later pictures.
- He argues the sky's blue is not the air's own color but lit moisture seen against darkness, and supports it with trials: thin smoke in sunlight in front of black velvet looks blue, and a very thin white glaze looks bluest over black paint.
- He points to a small opening in a dark room that throws images of outside objects upside down as proof that every object fills the surrounding air with images of itself. He also argues against the idea that the eye sends out a power of its own, noting that nothing is seen without light.
- He describes the pupil widening in the dark and narrowing in light, tells the reader to watch it happen by moving a candle toward someone's eye, and compares the eyes of owls and cats with human ones. He notes that a bright light leaves an afterimage.
- He records popular beliefs about the power of a glance (the wolf, the basilisk) as things that are said, and ends in open wonder that so small an organ can hold images of the whole universe.
