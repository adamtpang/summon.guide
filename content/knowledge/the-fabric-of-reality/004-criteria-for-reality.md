# Criteria for Reality

> **Key principle:** Deutsch argues that doctrines like solipsism, instrumentalism, and positivism only look like leaner alternatives to realism; taken seriously as explanations, they secretly smuggle in all of realism's complexity and then add extra, unexplained machinery on top. His replacement test for reality, borrowed from Samuel Johnson's foot-first refutation of Bishop Berkeley, is that anything complex and autonomous enough to "kick back" at us unpredictably should be regarded as real.

*Synthesized from Chapter 4 of The Fabric of Reality by David Deutsch.*

## Key lessons

- Galileo pioneered mathematical, experimentally-tested physics and used his telescope to gather heliocentric evidence, but the Inquisition's 1633 trial was not really a dispute over planetary layout: both sides were realists, and what the Church actually punished was Galileo's claim that scientific reasoning could outrank tradition and revelation as a path to reliable knowledge, forcing his recantation and house arrest (the "eppur si muove" line attributed to him afterward is probably legend).
- Deutsch constructs the "Inquisition's theory" (Earth fixed, planets looping in whatever complex paths make them look heliocentric from Earth) and shows it fails on its own terms: to explain any specific planetary loop it must appeal to "how it would look if the heliocentric theory were true," meaning it cannot be understood without first understanding the very heliocentric theory it claims to replace, making it a convoluted elaboration of realism rather than a genuine rival.
- The classic joke about a solipsism professor whose students rush up to agree with him demonstrates that solipsism cannot even be consistently affirmed by an audience: if the students accept the professor's view, they concede they don't exist and so cannot be agreeing; if each believes only they exist, none of them are agreeing with the professor or each other.
- A solipsist who takes the position seriously and tries to explain "dream-people" who behave independently must admit those dream-people are either genuinely external (conceding realism) or part of an unconscious portion of the self so much larger, more complex, and more law-governed than the conscious self that the theory collapses into realism wearing a disguise, with solipsism reduced to an arbitrary renaming scheme plus unexplained extra assumptions.
- Deutsch extends the same self-refutation to other boundary-drawing doctrines: positivism declares all non-observational statements meaningless, which is itself a non-observational statement, so it refutes itself; behaviourism draws the same solipsist/reality boundary but forbids reasoning about the inside instead of the outside.
- Dr. Johnson's kick at a rock to "refute" Berkeley's idealism illustrates Deutsch's proposed criterion: something is real if it "kicks back," meaning it affects us in ways complex and autonomous enough to require independent explanation, not because the kick is forceful but because of the explanatory role the object plays; Galileo's telescope let light from planets "kick back" at his retina in the same evidential sense.
- Deutsch sharpens this into a computational-complexity test: an entity counts as real if, according to the simplest available explanation, faking its effects would require a substantial hidden computation; mirror reflections are not real people because their apparent complexity is simply borrowed from the real objects in front of the mirror, whereas reproducing everything a hidden shadow photon or an angel pushing a planet would have to do demands as much computation as the phenomenon itself, so positing the entity is simpler than positing the fake.
- The chapter closes by generalizing Galileo's discovery: physical reality is saturated with accessible evidence everywhere and at all times, open to anyone who looks in the right way with good theories in mind, and this "self-similarity" between reality's structure and the symbols (books, theories, minds) that can represent it is what makes scientific knowledge possible at all.
