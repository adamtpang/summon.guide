# The Supply and Demand of AI Tokens | Dylan Patel Interview

> **Key principle:** SemiAnalysis founder Dylan Patel argues that as AI implementation gets radically cheaper, the bottleneck in any business stops being "can we build it" and becomes "did we pick the right thing to build, can we get the tokens to build it fast enough, and can we capture the value once it exists." He treats his own firm's exploding internal AI spend as a live case study in what happens to every information-heavy business as this shift takes hold.

*Dylan Patel on the economics of AI compute and token demand, Invest Like the Best · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LF3aUIM57uw) · 46 min · published 2026-04-23.*

## Key lessons

- SemiAnalysis's own AI spend went from tens of thousands of dollars a year in 2025 to a run rate of roughly $7 million a year on Claude Code alone by the time of the interview (up from $5 million just a week earlier), now more than 25% of the firm's roughly $25 million annual salary expense and, on the current trajectory, projected to exceed 100% of salary spend by year end.
- Concrete internal case studies illustrate the shift: a former Intel employee spent a few thousand dollars in Claude tokens to build a GPU-accelerated chip-analysis dashboard that automatically labels each material in a scanned chip cross-section, work he says used to require an entire dedicated team; an in-house economist single-handedly graded roughly 2,000 Bureau of Labor Statistics task categories for AI-automatability (finding about 3% currently doable) and built a new benchmark and "phantom GDP" framework, a project he estimated would have taken a 200-person team a year; and an energy analyst spent about $6,000 a day for three weeks to scrape every US power plant and major transmission line into a live grid supply-and-demand map that customers said beat a product built over a decade by a 100-person incumbent.
- Patel frames Anthropic's finances as evidence of extreme pricing power: revenue climbed from roughly $9 billion to somewhere around $40-45 billion within about a year while compute capacity grew far more slowly, implying gross margins near a floor of 72% even after accounting for research spend, up from gross margins in the 30s percent that reportedly leaked from earlier funding documents.
- He describes Anthropic's next-generation model (referred to in the episode by the internal nickname "Mythos") as internally available since February and already benchmarking as roughly the jump from an "L4" to an "L6" software engineer in about two months, so good that Anthropic initially limited its release to select customers for cybersecurity use cases at 5 to 10 times normal token pricing, and according to Patel even deliberately weakened the publicly released Opus 4.7 model's cyber-related capability before shipping it.
- On the hardware supply side, Patel argues the common claim that GPUs have a five-year useful life is wrong: he cites Hopper and A100-generation clusters being re-signed for three to four additional years of service, which pushes effective cluster gross margins above their nominal 35%; separately, DRAM memory is set to double or triple in price again because incremental fab capacity, even at a full sprint, cannot arrive before 2027 or 2028, while TSMC's capital expenditure (already $56-57 billion this year) is on a trajectory Patel expects could reach roughly $100 billion a year by 2028.
- He identifies CPUs, not GPUs, as an underappreciated sold-out bottleneck: reinforcement-learning training loops rely on CPU-run "environments" that test and score a model's attempted actions before those results get trained on, and once a model is deployed, its generated output (code, files, agent actions) typically routes through CPU-based application infrastructure rather than going straight from GPU to user.
- Patel's blunt framing of the coming economic sorting: using more tokens, generating real economic value from them, and actually capturing that value are three separate steps, and he argues that skipping any of them (using AI to work fewer hours instead of producing more output at the same hours, for instance) risks falling into what he calls a "permanent underclass" as capability and pricing power concentrate among model labs and the businesses fastest to exploit access.
- Asked to predict three months out, Patel forecasts large-scale public protests against AI and against Anthropic specifically, pointing to Pew survey data showing AI polling less popular than politicians and to hostile reactions online (including the Molotov cocktail incidents at Sam Altman's house) as early signs of a broader backlash that AI lab leaders are, in his view, making worse by talking publicly about future capability rather than present-day, tangible benefits.
