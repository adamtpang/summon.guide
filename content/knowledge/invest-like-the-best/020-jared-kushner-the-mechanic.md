# Jared Kushner - The Mechanic

> **Key principle:** Jared Kushner traces his effectiveness across real estate, government, and now Affinity Partners to one repeated habit: entering high-stakes situations without pretending to have expertise he lacks, listening until the other party tells him what actually matters to them, and then building a structure that grows the whole pie rather than fighting over slices of a fixed one. He calls the result of applying that habit under pressure being "the mechanic."

*Jared Kushner on real estate turnarounds, the Abraham Accords, Operation Warp Speed, and building Affinity Partners, Invest Like the Best · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EagrRPukYdc) · 173 min · published 2024-12-20.*

## Key lessons

- The 666 Fifth Avenue deal, bought for $1.8 billion in 2007 (then the largest price ever paid for a single US asset) just before the market crashed, was salvaged not through legal leverage but through radical honesty: Kushner met distressed-debt investor Tom Barrack with no lawyers and no threats, laid out the real numbers, and got Barrack to become an advocate; the retail condo was later sold 50% to Carlyle and the Chera family for $525 million, paying down debt and eventually creating over a billion dollars of value.
- His first independent institutional deal, converting the old 200 Lafayette Street sweatshop building for tech tenants, closed when investor Al Shams told him on the roof, "I'm not going to invest in this building, I'm investing in you"; Kushner renovated it in under a year (despite Hurricane Sandy) against the 18-month timeline his own team expected, then sold it for about $160 million roughly a year later.
- His Abraham Accords approach began with a two-and-a-half-hour meeting where he asked UAE's Mohammed bin Zayed a single open question, what would you do in America's position, rather than arriving with a fixed US position; MBZ told him afterward that he was the first senior American official MBZ could recall who actually asked questions instead of reading off talking points or trying to extract concessions.
- To break the Saudi-Qatar rift that was blocking Bahrain's entry into the Abraham Accords, Kushner personally patched a live phone call between Mohammed bin Salman and Qatar's Emir Tamim (who had refused to call directly himself after a prior falling-out), then spent the next roughly 100 days speaking with Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed about three times a day to hold the channel together through repeated near-collapses.
- Affinity Partners underwrites on four criteria in order: the strength of the macro trend behind a deal, the quality of the people running the target company, whether the business has a real structural moat, and "why us" (why Affinity specifically, rather than another investor, should be the one making that bet), a discipline meant to keep a roughly billion-dollars-a-year fund selective inside a market with over $100 trillion of annual global GDP to choose from.
- Affinity's Brazil investment (Burger King, Popeyes, and later a bankrupt Subway and Starbucks) was bought around 5x cash flow after COVID emptied the malls that housed most locations and Brazilian interest rates spiked from about 2% to 14%, scaring off other investors; management then used AI-driven computer vision to cut food waste by roughly 35%, improving margins in what Kushner calls one of the least "AI-native" businesses in the portfolio.
- Its Israeli investment in insurer Phoenix (4.9% plus a fixed-price option near 37.5 shekels, later trading above 50) was built on a repricing thesis: Kushner argued the market was valuing Phoenix like a traditional book-value insurance company when its roughly $150 billion of managed assets and joint ventures with BlackRock, Ares, and Apollo justified an earnings-multiple valuation instead, a re-underwriting that took about a year of research.
- During Operation Warp Speed, a late-night call from Ken Griffin (manufacture every leading vaccine candidate in parallel during Phase 3 trials, so a six-month manufacturing head start is banked on whichever one succeeds) shaped the strategy directly; the team also discovered mid-effort that the country had only 1.2 million testing swabs on hand (enough for 37 sites), forcing a military airlift of swabs from an Italian factory, which Kushner cites as proof that the cheapest, most overlooked supply item becomes the real bottleneck in a crisis.
- Kushner underwent 33 hours of testimony across House, Senate, special counsel, and grand jury proceedings during the Russia investigation, paid millions of dollars in legal fees personally, and took no government salary; he frames the lesson less as a grievance than a discipline, that the way to survive a high-scrutiny period is the same "look at your feet, not the top of the hill" approach he uses in deals: solve the next concrete step and avoid any action that hands opponents a technicality.
