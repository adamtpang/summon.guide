# How Brian Chesky Is Redesigning Airbnb for the AI Era

> **Key principle:** Chesky argues that founders are born but CEOs have to be trained, and that the training only works through deliberate, hands-on immersion in a company's details, not delegation learned by trial and error. He now applies that same discipline to Airbnb's next act: shrinking a $100 billion company into small pilot teams, one city or one problem at a time, before industrializing anything.

*Brian Chesky on Airbnb's shift from Founder Mode to CEO Mode in the AI era, Invest Like the Best · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eURcW5_uS60) · 83 min · published 2026-05-05.*

## Key lessons

- Founder Mode was born from crisis, not theory. By 2019, running a company that had grown to around 7,000 employees, Chesky says he felt disconnected from what was actually happening, as if steering a car that no longer responded to the wheel. The real break came with the pandemic, when Airbnb lost 80 percent of its business in eight weeks, forcing him from what he calls peacetime into wartime and into total, hands-on control of every part of the company.
- Hiroki Asai, Steve Jobs' longtime creative director, whom Chesky brought in around the same period, taught him two principles he says shaped Founder Mode: simplicity as distillation rather than subtraction, and an obsession with granular craft borrowed from Bill Walsh's book "the score takes care of itself" and John Wooden's habit of spending his UCLA team's first practice hour teaching players how to put on their socks.
- Project Hawaii carved a roughly 10-to-12-person team out of a company of thousands, run like a startup on a crawl-walk-run-fly sequence and focused purely on improving search-to-book conversion. Chesky says that single team generated about $200 million in incremental internal revenue in its first year, $400-500 million in its second, and that the resulting product changes now run at roughly 600 basis points of Airbnb's $134 billion in gross bookings.
- New Airbnb business lines follow a strict one-to-ten-to-many rollout: pilot in a single city, prove it, expand to ten cities, and only then industrialize, mirroring how Airbnb itself launched in just New York with about 100 users. Chesky says it took Airbnb 16 years to get a second and third business line working, and that Services and Experiences only found traction after he abandoned an initial hundred-city launch and shrank it back down to one market.
- His "11-star experience" exercise pushes a normal five-star Airbnb stay to an absurd extreme step by step, from a handwritten note and wine at six stars up through an elephant parade and a Beatlemania-style airport welcome, so that a genuinely differentiated six- or seven-star version stops sounding unreasonable and becomes something a team can actually build and scale.
- His hiring philosophy rejects search firms entirely in favor of constant pipeline-building: track the best people in a field, ask everyone met for two or three more introductions, and start from admired output (an ad, a product) and work backward to the person who made it. Chesky says he still personally acts as co-hiring manager for Airbnb's top 200 hires and spends two to three hours a day on recruiting, making it the first and last phone call of most workdays.
- Chesky credits competitive bodybuilding (starting at 135 pounds at 13 after late puberty ended his hockey ambitions, competing nationally by 19) with teaching him progressive overload as a leadership model: the body doesn't strengthen during a workout but through adaptive recovery afterward, so daily, compounding, roughly one percent improvement beats sporadic all-out pushes, a discipline he says maps directly onto how founders should build companies.
- Chesky's stated next horizon is shifting Airbnb's "atomic unit" from a home to a person: building the most authenticated identity and preference profile on the internet, and expanding the platform's offerings the way Amazon grew from books to everything, from roughly three categories today (homes, Experiences, Services) toward what he describes as 50 to 100.
