# Finding the Next Figma, Wiz, & Stripe Before It's Obvious | Neil Mehta Interview

> **Key principle:** Neil Mehta built Greenoaks around the idea that a "jaw-dropping customer experience," a product so good it makes customers emotional, comes from founders who break trade-offs everyone else treats as fixed, and that finding those founders is a repeatable discipline rather than a mysterious talent. Because only 10 to 15 founders a year meet that bar in his view, Greenoaks stays deliberately small and concentrated instead of scaling into a coverage-driven, multi-partner venture factory.

*Neil Mehta on Greenoaks Capital's investment philosophy, evaluating founders, and finding jaw-dropping customer experiences, Invest Like the Best · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=502sB_IbjpQ) · 104 min · published 2025-04-15.*

## Key lessons

- Mehta coined the term "jaw-dropping customer experience" (JDCE) while investing in Coupang: founder Bom Kim built a same-day delivery network in Korea (now called Rocket) that lifted cohort retention from roughly the industry's 30s into the 60s, and customer interviews captured mothers crying on camera because the diapers arrived before they ran out, evidence Mehta treats as a stronger signal than any NPS score.
- Greenoaks' Coupang position shows what real conviction-scale investing looks like: the firm invested just under $1 billion across roughly 10 years, led 5 of 8 funding rounds, has held a board seat for about 15 years, and was still buying additional public shares as recently as the quarter before this interview.
- When evaluating a company, Mehta says the single question he'd ask every employee if he could ask only one is whether their best days feel ahead of them or behind them, ranking that signal above year-over-year growth, margins, or any formal competitive-moat framework.
- Greenoaks' worst self-diagnosed mistake was almost passing on backing Elon Musk early at SpaceX because secondhand diligence (fires people fast, micromanages down to the smallest detail, disappears then reappears and changes everything) sounded disqualifying when relayed by mentors; Mehta now treats those same traits as positive founder signals and no longer outsources primary diligence on anyone the firm is seriously considering.
- The firm intentionally avoids the "private equitized" venture model of specialized teams chasing every deal in a sector-and-stage matrix; because it targets only about 10 to 15 founders a year worldwide, Mehta personally takes first meetings, prepares so deeply beforehand that a literal first meeting "should feel like a fifth or sixth" to the founder, and argues this makes Greenoaks less competitive for its actual targets even as total venture capital in the industry has grown roughly 100x since Sequoia backed Nvidia.
- His Tencent/QQ discovery moment, watching phone screens light up faces during the 2008 Beijing Olympics broadcast (his own BlackBerry couldn't do the same), and later learning QQ was adding about 30 million subscribers a month, a scale he says dwarfed the 150-year-old, 30-million-total-depositor European bank he was assigned to analyze at the time, redirected him from distressed-debt work toward backing consumer internet platforms in emerging markets, which became Greenoaks' founding thesis.
- On growth itself, he tells the story of Bom Kim deliberately reaccelerating Coupang off an 18% year-over-year growth year, over internal pushback that "Amazon never grew more than 30%, you're fine," because Kim believed unreasonably high growth expectations are themselves a competitive advantage; Mehta cites security company Wiz posting one of its best quarters even during the period when a large share of its go-to-market and engineering staff left temporarily to serve in Israel's military.
- His framing for evaluating AI-era businesses rejects hype-chasing in favor of first principles, comparing it to the Wright brothers realizing they didn't need to replicate a bird's wing-flap, just the underlying laws of aerodynamics: Greenoaks tracks technical benchmarks (citing DeepSeek's roughly 35x reduction in per-token cost versus comparable reasoning models) but insists the same old questions still decide outcomes, whether a product delights customers, breaks a real trade-off, and has defensible economics, the same discipline that kept the firm out of crypto deals pitched on the premise that boards and auditors "don't apply this time."
