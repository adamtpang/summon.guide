# Inside Anthropic's $100 Billion AI Compute Commitment | CFO Krishna Rao

> **Key principle:** Anthropic CFO Krishna Rao spends 30-40% of his time on compute because, in his framing, buying too little compute means losing the frontier and the customers that come with it, while buying too much means going out of business, and the only way to navigate that is to model a wide "cone of uncertainty" around exponential growth and deliberately buy toward its high end. The interview is a rare inside look at how a company burning through tens of billions of dollars of committed compute actually plans, prices, and forecasts a business whose revenue quadrupled from roughly $9 billion to $30 billion run rate in a single quarter.

*Krishna Rao on financing a $100 billion AI compute commitment, Invest Like the Best · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wEEZPpx8qow) · 82 min · published 2026-05-13.*

## Key lessons

- Anthropic's revenue run rate went from about $9 billion at the start of the year to north of $30 billion by the end of that quarter, a jump Rao attributes directly to model intelligence leaps unlocking new addressable use cases rather than to sales-force expansion; when he joined the company roughly two years earlier it was at $250 million run rate with a plan to reach $1 billion, and he recalls asking "in what year?" as an example of the linear thinking he had to unlearn.
- The company runs compute fungibly across three chip platforms, Amazon's Trainium, Google's TPUs, and Nvidia's GPUs, which took years of investment (building custom compilers and an orchestration layer down to near bare metal) so that the same pool of compute can shift between model training, internal R&D and product acceleration, and customer-facing inference depending on where the return is highest at any given moment.
- Recent compute commitments include a 5-gigawatt TPU deal with Google and Broadcom starting in 2027, a Trainium deal with Amazon for up to 5 gigawatts, and a combined commitment north of $100 billion between the two, on top of a near-term compute partnership with xAI and SpaceX's Colossus facility in Memphis to fill capacity gaps while longer-term capacity ramps in.
- Rao's "cone of uncertainty" framework means modeling a wide range of exponential growth scenarios 1-2 years out and buying compute toward the top end of that range rather than the median, accepting the real risk of visible overcapacity, because in a business scaling this fast, underbuying (falling off the frontier, being unable to serve customers) is judged the more expensive failure mode.
- Anthropic has raised about $75 billion since Rao joined two years ago, with another $50 billion committed via the Amazon and Google compute deals; the company reports net dollar retention over 500% annualized and counts nine of the Fortune 10 as customers, and Rao frames the fundraising as driven more by the need to hedge exponential uncertainty than by funding current operating losses.
- On pricing, Anthropic cut the price of its Opus-tier model at the Opus 4.5 launch because efficiency gains let it serve Opus-class capability much more cheaply, and observed a Jevons paradox: lower prices caused consumption to rise by far more than the price cut alone would predict, reinforcing the company's general preference for pricing stability across model generations rather than constant repricing.
- Internally, Claude Code now writes more than 90% of Anthropic's own code, and the finance function runs on roughly 70 finance-specific Claude "skills," including one that produces the company's monthly financial review 90-95% complete before human review, compressing work that used to take hours into about 30 minutes and shifting the team's time from reconciling numbers to interpreting them.
- The company deliberately staggered the public release of its Mythos model because its cyber capability spiked sharply relative to prior models (finding 250 vulnerabilities in an open-source codebase versus 22 found by an earlier model), using a phased rollout to expand access gradually rather than a standard full release, a template Rao says the company expects to reuse for future high-capability models.
- Anthropic says it lost only two employees to competitors' aggressive compensation offers during a period when other labs reportedly lost dozens, crediting a hard culture-fit interview gate (technically brilliant candidates can still be rejected on culture), full transparency (Dario Amodei takes unscripted questions from the whole company every two weeks), and the fact that all seven co-founders and most of the first 20-30 employees remain at the company.
