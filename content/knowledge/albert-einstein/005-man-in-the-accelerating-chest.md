## Key lessons

- He builds the scene step by step: a large region of empty space far from stars, and in it a chest like a room, with an observer inside who has apparatus. There is no gravity, so the observer must tie himself to the floor.
- He adds one change. A hook and rope are fixed to the lid, and a "being" (he says it does not matter what kind) pulls with constant force, so the chest accelerates uniformly "upwards" as judged from a reference body that is not being pulled.
- He then switches viewpoint and asks how the man inside regards the process. The floor presses on his legs as in a room on earth, and any object he releases approaches the floor with accelerated motion.
- The observer finds the acceleration toward the floor is always the same, whatever kind of body he uses. Knowing how gravitational fields behave, he concludes that he and the chest are in a gravitational field, and on finding the hook and rope he decides the chest hangs at rest in that field.
- Einstein asks whether we should smile at the man and say he is wrong. He answers no: the man's view "violates neither reason nor known mechanical laws", so the chest may be regarded as at rest even though it is accelerated relative to the first reference body.
- He states what the argument depends on: the experimental fact that a gravitational field gives all bodies the same acceleration, which is the equality of inertial and gravitational mass. Without that law the man's interpretation would fail.
- He tests the idea with a second case. A body hung from a rope inside the chest stretches the rope. The man inside attributes the tension to gravitational mass, and an observer floating freely outside attributes it to inertial mass. The extended principle of relativity therefore requires the two masses to be equal, which gives that law a physical interpretation.
- He warns against overreading the result. Not every gravitational field is merely apparent. Only fields of a quite special form can be removed by choosing another reference body, and the earth's field as a whole cannot.
- He uses the result to answer his own earlier objection from Section XVIII. A passenger jerked forward when a train brakes is not forced to call it a real acceleration. He may regard the carriage as at rest with a temporary gravitational field acting.
