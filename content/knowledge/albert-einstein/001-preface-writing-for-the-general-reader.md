## Key lessons

- He names his audience precisely: readers interested in the theory from a general scientific and philosophical angle who do not know the mathematical tools of theoretical physics.
- He states what he expects of the reader in return: roughly a university entrance level of education, plus a fair amount of patience and force of will, even though the book is short.
- He says he took great pains to present the main ideas in the simplest and most intelligible form he could.
- He presents the ideas, on the whole, in the sequence and connection in which they actually originated, so the reader follows the path of the reasoning and not only its result.
- He chooses clearness over polish: he repeats himself often and says he paid no attention to the elegance of the presentation.
- He credits this rule to the physicist L. Boltzmann, who held that elegance should be left to the tailor and the cobbler.
- He does not pretend the subject is easy. He says he has not withheld the difficulties that are inherent in it.
- He deliberately gives little space to the empirical physical foundations, so that a reader unfamiliar with physics does not lose sight of the forest among the trees.
- He closes with a modest hope for the book: that it brings someone "a few happy hours of suggestive thought".
