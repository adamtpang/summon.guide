## Key lessons

- The meeting ran from 12:15 to 2:22 a.m. on May 27, 1974, after about four weeks of travel between Jerusalem and Damascus. The editors note he had met the Israeli team that morning and Asad earlier the same evening.
- Asad argues that disengagement by itself could be read as serving Israel, because it relaxes Arab military and political pressure, and says he cannot take it alone as proof of American intentions. Kissinger does not dispute the logic and treats the frankness as a sign of confidence.
- Kissinger answers that the United States would have no reason to care about disengagement lines unless it wanted to start movement toward a settlement, and that a direct assault on an entrenched pattern is often the least effective way to change it. He describes working around a problem instead.
- He says he concluded at the start of the October 1973 war that it should be used as an opening toward peace, and calls the war a strategic defeat for Israel despite its tactical successes. Both men say they expect another war within a year if there is no progress.
- He describes his own role with an image: he is "like a doctor", trying to judge what is possible without breaking it. He tells Asad that on each trip to Israel he asked for more concessions and never proposed anything that moved the other way.
- Asad asks for a US letter committing to full implementation of Security Council Resolution 338 and to the 1967 frontiers. Kissinger says privately that no Arab state would accept less than the 1967 lines, but declines to put that in a letter, saying the President cannot sign something he could not live with politically if it were published.
- He holds to the wording legitimate interests of the Palestinians and refuses the word rights, explaining the distinction he draws, and gives as a personal view that a Palestinian entity on the West Bank linked to Gaza was conceivable.
- On the disputed red line near Kuneitra, he tells Asad the Israeli cabinet will not move further, says he believes he could on his own authority obtain a change in where the artillery limit is measured from and perhaps another kilometer, and admits a mistake he made on the southern sector.
- He says that if he were the Israeli side and chose to negotiate, he would do it generously and not grudgingly. He also tells Asad the United States prefers dealing directly with the Arab leaders over settlements arranged with Moscow and imposed on them.
