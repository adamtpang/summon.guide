## Key lessons

- The source is a State Department telegram from the US Embassy in Jakarta giving the memorandum of conversation of a meeting between President Ford and President Suharto, with Kissinger, Foreign Minister Malik, and Ambassador Newsom present.
- Most of the meeting covers other subjects: China's aims after Vietnam, Indochina, insurgency in Thailand and Malaysia, ASEAN cooperation, an Indonesian request for help building an M-16 rifle plant (which Kissinger and Ford favor), and oil company profits.
- Suharto raises Timor himself. He says Indonesia has no territorial ambitions, that Fretilin had declared independence unilaterally, that other parties had asked for integration with Indonesia, and that Portugal could not control the situation. He asks for US understanding if Indonesia judged it necessary to take rapid or drastic action.
- Ford replies that the United States understands and will not press Indonesia on the issue.
- Kissinger then points out that the use of US made arms could create problems, and says it depends on how the action is construed, whether as self defense or as a foreign operation. Ford adds that there could be technical and legal problems and mentions Cyprus.
- Kissinger says it is important that whatever Indonesia does "succeeds quickly", and that the US could better influence the American reaction if it happened after the President returned home, giving the return time as Monday at 2:00 p.m. Jakarta time.
- He adds that the United States would try to handle whatever Indonesia did in the best way possible and would do its best to keep everyone quiet until the President was home.
- He asks whether Suharto expects a long guerrilla war. Suharto answers that there would probably be a small one and that local rulers were on Indonesia's side.
- The telegram records no US objection to military action in Timor and no statement of concern for the population there. It does not describe what Indonesia did afterward; the State Department files it in a chapter on Indonesia and the East Timor crisis.
