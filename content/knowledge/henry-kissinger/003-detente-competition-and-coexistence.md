## Key lessons

- He says US and Soviet differences are not misunderstandings or matters of personality. They come from history, opposing values and ideologies, diverging national interests, and commitments to allies.
- He states the dilemma as a paradox: pursuing peace to the exclusion of everything else sacrifices other values, but unconstrained rivalry that ends in nuclear war destroys those values along with everything else.
- He defines detente as a search for a more constructive relationship and as a process, not a finished condition reached at one moment. He says successive American leaders have pursued it by different means.
- He sets out guiding principles: policy cannot rest on Moscow's good intentions, but progress cannot wait until the two sides' purposes converge. The method is systematic resistance to pressure combined with conciliatory responses to moderate behavior.
- He says the United States is not neutral between freedom and tyranny, but that there are limits on its ability to change other countries internally, and that recognizing those limits reflects the necessity of peace and not indifference.
- He argues a stable relationship must give advantages to both sides, and that the balance of gains cannot be struck issue by issue every day, only across the whole relationship over time.
- He lists an agenda: restraints on military competition, restraint in crises, cooperation on the causes of crises (he cites the Middle East), continuous negotiation, and no superpower agreements made over the heads of other nations with a stake.
- He warns that demanding unilateral gain from every agreement, seeking "superiority", or trying to transform the Soviet system by pressure would revive 1950s doctrines he says failed. He equally lists Soviet actions that would destroy detente, such as using it to build military strength or sharpen crises.
- Applied today: his point that a durable deal must leave each party seeing some gain, judged over the whole relationship and not per item, carries over to long negotiations between rivals of any kind.
