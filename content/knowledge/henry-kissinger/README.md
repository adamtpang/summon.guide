# henry-kissinger

Original Summon syntheses, written 2026-10-01.

- Source: Seven US government documents read online at history.state.gov (Office of the Historian, US Department of State), Foreign Relations of the United States, 1969-1976: Vol. XVII China, Doc. 139 (memcon with Zhou Enlai, July 9, 1971); Vol. XXXVIII Part 1, Doc. 19 (Pacem in Terris address, October 8, 1973) and Doc. 45 (detente statement to the Senate Foreign Relations Committee, September 19, 1974); Vol. XXVI Arab-Israeli Dispute, Doc. 74 (memcon with Asad, May 27, 1974); Vol. XXI Chile, Doc. 172 (memo to Nixon, November 5, 1970); Vol. XI South Asia Crisis, Doc. 36 (memo to Nixon, April 28, 1971); Vol. E-12, Doc. 141 (Ford-Suharto meeting telegram, December 6, 1975).
- Read in full: yes
- Parts read: Each of the seven documents as printed by the Office of the Historian, including editorial footnotes, read in full from the saved page text.
- Parts not read: Doc. 45 is an excerpt: the editors omit the historical overview and the sections on specific US-Soviet agreements, so linkage is not covered in detail. Also fetched and read but not turned into notes: Vol. XVII Doc. 144 (Kissinger's report to Nixon on the Beijing talks, mostly omitted by the editors), the 1973 Nobel acceptance statement at nobelprize.org, and Vol. VI Doc. 41 (editorial note on the March 1969 decision to bomb Cambodian sanctuaries). Fetched but not read: Vol. XXXVIII Part 1 Doc. 59 (Moral Foundations of Foreign Policy, July 1975). No books by Kissinger were used. Only the first of the July 1971 Beijing meetings was read.

## Caveats

- Notes 005, 006 and 007 cover documented controversies using primary records only. Each reports what that one document shows and does not narrate later events (the 1973 coup in Chile, the scale of killing in East Pakistan, the Indonesian invasion of East Timor), because those are not in the documents read.
- No note covers the secret bombing of Cambodia. The editorial note fetched (Vol. VI Doc. 41) was read but is thin and partly relies on memoirs; a fuller primary source should be added.
- Memoranda of conversation are US staff transcripts, approved by Kissinger in the China case; they are not verbatim recordings and reflect the American record of what was said.
- The Jakarta telegram contains typos and an apparent date error in its footnote (September 6 for December 6); the note follows the document date.
- FRUS uses older romanization (Chou En-lai, Peking); notes use Zhou Enlai and Beijing.
- Raw fetched pages (_page-N.html, _page-N.txt, _read-N.txt) were saved in this folder by curl as the task instructed; they are working files, not notes.

No source text is stored here. Every lesson is a paraphrase.
