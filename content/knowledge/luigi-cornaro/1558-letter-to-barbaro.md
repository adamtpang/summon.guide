## Key lessons

- Situation: a letter to Barbaro, Patriarch-elect of Aquileia, written when he claims to be ninety-one. Learned physicians visit him and conclude he must be specially blessed.
- What he did: he rejected that view, arguing his condition is open to any person. He admits the change was difficult for him too, and says he prayed for the strength to keep it.
- Lesson: he says people with a stronger constitution may live more freely than he does; his rules were shaped by his weakness. He also notes he cannot tolerate wine for two summer months, which weakens him each year. The point is that a routine has to fit one's own body.
- Caution: his talk of special grace and his boasting are self-serving. His own account of yearly weakness sits oddly with a story of perfect health.
