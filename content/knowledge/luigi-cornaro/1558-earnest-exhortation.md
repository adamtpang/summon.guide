## Key lessons

- Situation: he writes, claiming to be in his ninety-fifth year, to persuade readers that a regular life leads to a cheerful old age. The edition's preface and text disagree about which of his late pieces came first.
- What he argued: many people ruin old age by carrying youthful habits into it, while a steadier life spares them the sickness, sorrow and fear of death he says haunt others. He also says he is certain of reaching a hundred.
- Lesson: he found joy in serving his city, particularly by advocating for the Venetian lagoon. Purpose steadied him.
- Caution: certainty about a long life is not a virtue and is not evidence. He also says grief passes him quickly; the guide should not present emotional numbness as wisdom.
