## Key lessons

- Situation: Cornaro says he had a hot temper and describes being pulled into expensive lawsuits by powerful men, which so distressed his brother and other relatives that he believes it shortened their lives.
- What he did: he chose to see the lawsuits as a trial of his strength, kept his spirits up, and won a favorable decision. Elsewhere he says he resolved to make his choleric disposition give way to reason.
- Lesson: he treated temper and worry as things you can work on deliberately, and framing a hardship as a test kept him steady.
- Caution: attributing relatives' deaths to grief is his 16th century reading. It should not be used to blame anyone's illness on their feelings.
