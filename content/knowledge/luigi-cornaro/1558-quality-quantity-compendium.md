## Key lessons

- Situation: written when he claimed to be eighty-six, this short piece addresses people who think a weak constitution is a life sentence, and people who think a strong one is a free pass.
- What he argued: the heavens give an inclination but do not compel, so reason can compensate; he says he did this with his temper too. He answers excuses one by one, such as that a shorter life is worth the pleasure.
- Lesson: he boils sobriety down to watching what harms you and avoiding excess, and says a person should know their own limits well by middle age. In his words, "we are all human beings, and endowed with reason, consequently we are masters of our actions."
- Caution: he mixes sound intuition with humors theory and astrological medicine, and treats a peaceful death in old age as controllable, which it is not.
