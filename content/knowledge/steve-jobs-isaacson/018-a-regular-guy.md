# A Regular Guy

> **Key principle:** Jobs was capable of real romantic devotion and real emotional damage in the same relationship. He tracked down his birth mother Joanne Schieble (later Simpson) and discovered a full sister, novelist Mona Simpson, but flatly refused to meet his birth father Abdulfattah Jandali even after learning they had once met unknowingly. His longest and most serious relationship, with Tina Redse, ended when she declined his 1989 marriage proposal and later concluded he showed signs of Narcissistic Personality Disorder.

*Synthesized from Chapter 20, "A Regular Guy," of Steve Jobs by Walter Isaacson.*

## Key lessons

- Jobs dated folksinger Joan Baez for about three years starting in 1982; the relationship stalled partly because Baez, in her forties with a teenage son, didn't want more children, and Jobs later said, "I thought I was in love with her, but I really just liked her a lot."
- The "red dress" episode captured his mix of generosity and withholding: he drove Baez to a Ralph Lauren store, urged her to buy an expensive dress, and then said nothing and let her leave without it — "I felt a bit strange about it," she recalled decades later.
- On his mother Clara's deathbed in the mid-1980s, Jobs asked pointed questions about his origins; soon after, a dying San Francisco doctor's letter and a private detective led him to his birth mother, Joanne Schieble Simpson, whom he visited only after Clara died so as not to hurt his adoptive parents.
- Joanne revealed Jobs had a full biological sister, novelist Mona Simpson, then living in Manhattan; the two met at the St. Regis Hotel, bonded immediately over shared artistic sensibility, and Jobs later said, "My sister's a writer! ... I don't know what I'd do without her."
- Mona separately tracked down their birth father, Abdulfattah Jandali, working in a small Sacramento restaurant; he mentioned, unprompted, that a wealthy tech customer named Steve Jobs used to eat at a restaurant he'd once managed — neither of them realized at the time they were talking about his own son, and Jobs, once told, still refused any contact: "I was a wealthy man by then, and I didn't trust him not to try to blackmail me."
- Jobs's relationship with his own daughter, Lisa Brennan, deepened during this period as NeXT's calmer schedule let him visit more; a trip to Tokyo where they shared unagi sushi became, in Lisa's later account, a rare moment of real warmth and "once inaccessible space" opening between them.
- A relationship with Jennifer Egan (later a novelist) in 1983–84 surfaced a recurring philosophical tension: Jobs preached Buddhist nonattachment to material things while building products people coveted, and Egan pushed back, calling it a dichotomy that visibly irritated him.
- Tina Redse, whom Jobs met in early 1985, became "the first person I was truly in love with," he later said; their five-year relationship was marked by intense public affection and equally intense fights, with Redse once writing "Neglect is a form of abuse" on the wall outside their bedroom.
- On a bridge in Paris in 1985, during his exile from Apple, the two half-seriously discussed abandoning their old lives together; Jobs declined, telling her, "What could I do? I've made myself unemployable" — a line Redse recalled poignantly in an email to him twenty-five years later.
- Jobs proposed marriage to Redse in the summer of 1989; she turned him down, later explaining she "could not have been a good wife to 'Steve Jobs,' the icon" and could not abide his unkindness to others. She subsequently read about Narcissistic Personality Disorder and concluded it explained him: "the capacity for empathy is lacking."
