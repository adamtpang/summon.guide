# Think Different

> **Key principle:** In mid-1997, before shipping a single new product, Jobs commissioned Lee Clow's agency to build a brand campaign, "Think Different," aimed as much at demoralized Apple employees as at customers: "We at Apple had forgotten who we were. One way to remember who you are is to remember who your heroes are."

*Synthesized from Chapter 25, "Think Different," of Steve Jobs by Walter Isaacson.*

## Key lessons

- Jobs personally recruited Lee Clow of TBWA/Chiat-Day (who had made the 1984 Macintosh ad) to compete for Apple's account even though Clow said he didn't pitch for business; Jobs called him the day Gil Amelio resigned and begged him to come to Cupertino, later crying while recounting how much Clow "loved Apple."
- The campaign was explicitly not about product specs. "This wasn't about processor speed or memory," Jobs said. "It was about creativity" — a brand-image campaign celebrating what creative people could do with Apple computers, not the computers themselves.
- Clow's team drafted the "Here's to the crazy ones" text after failed attempts to license a Seal song and Robert Frost's "The Road Not Taken"; Jobs was a brutal editor of early drafts ("This is shit! It's advertising agency shit and I hate it") but also wrote some lines himself, including "They push the human race forward."
- Jobs insisted on "different" as a noun rather than the grammatically conventional "differently," comparing it to "think big": "It's not think the same, it's think different."
- The print campaign paired unlabeled black-and-white portraits of icons (Einstein, Gandhi, Lennon, Dylan, Picasso, and others less famous, like Martha Graham and Richard Feynman) with just the Apple logo; Jobs personally chased down the right photographs, including calling Time Inc.'s editor for a Gandhi photo and getting a private replacement Lennon photo from Yoko Ono at a New York restaurant.
- Jobs recorded his own voice-over for the TV spot but chose to ship the version narrated by Richard Dreyfuss instead, reasoning: "If we use my voice, when people find out they will say it's about me. It's not. It's about Apple."
- The same week the campaign launched, Jobs formally took the title of "interim CEO" (iCEO) on September 16, 1997, still drawing no salary and signing no contract, telling staff Apple needed to "get back to the basics of great products, great marketing, and great distribution."
