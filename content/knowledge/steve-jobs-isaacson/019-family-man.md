# Family Man

> **Key principle:** Jobs married Laurene Powell in March 1991 at the Ahwahnee Lodge, a match multiple friends described as finally durable because she could sustain his "ups and downs and tempestuous personality" without being consumed by them. The chapter follows the household this produced — three more children, a modest storybook-style house, Lisa Brennan moving in for high school, and a family culture that made a point of avoiding ostentation.

*Synthesized from Chapter 21, "Family Man," of Steve Jobs by Walter Isaacson.*

## Key lessons

- Jobs met Laurene Powell, a Stanford Business School student, when she and a friend commandeered front-row seats at his October 1989 "View from the Top" lecture; he chased her down in the parking lot afterward, switched their date to that same night, and they talked for four hours at a vegetarian restaurant — "We've been together ever since," he said.
- Powell had a hard early life — her Marine pilot father died flying a crippled plane away from a residential area, and her mother's second marriage was troubled — which she said taught her, "I always wanted to be self-sufficient"; she'd worked as a Goldman Sachs trading strategist before business school.
- Jobs proposed on New Year's Day 1990, then went silent about it for months, then oscillated — at one point sending roses to ex-girlfriend Tina Redse to see if she'd take him back — before Powell got pregnant during a Hawaii vacation and he committed for good.
- They married on March 18, 1991 at the Ahwahnee Lodge in Yosemite, with about fifty guests arriving by chartered bus; longtime Zen teacher Kobun Chino performed a ceremony most guests found incomprehensible, and the strictly vegan wedding cake, shaped like Half Dome, struck many as inedible.
- Instead of Jobs's unfurnished Woodside mansion, the couple settled into a modest 1930s "storybook style" house in old Palo Alto with neighbors like John Doerr, Larry Page, and Mark Zuckerberg — deliberately unpretentious, with no gates, no security detail, and an unlocked back door even after Jobs became famous again.
- Furnishing the house became a philosophical project rather than a shopping trip: Powell recalled, "We spoke about furniture in theory for eight years," and Jobs described spending two weeks of dinner-table debate deciding on a European washing machine over trade-offs of water use, cycle time, and fabric wear.
- Lisa Brennan moved in with Jobs and Powell partway through eighth grade after problems at her mother Chrisann's house; she lived there through all four years of Palo Alto High School, using the name Lisa Brennan-Jobs, though the relationship stayed volatile — arguments could shut down communication for weeks or months, and Jobs skipped her 2000 Harvard graduation, saying "she didn't even invite me."
- Mona Simpson's 1996 novel A Regular Guy, based partly on Jobs, wounded Lisa when she recognized her own life inside it — "sandwiched between the truths was invention — lies to me" — and she wrote a pained response for the Harvard Advocate before eventually reconciling with Simpson.
- The couple had three children: Reed Paul Jobs (1991, described as sweet-mannered and self-effacing despite his father's intensity), Erin Siena Jobs (1995, quieter and more distant from her father's attention), and Eve Jobs (1998, strong-willed enough that Jobs joked she might run Apple someday).
- Jobs deliberately avoided the trappings of extreme wealth for his kids' sake — no security guards, no gated estate — telling a reporter, "Every other CEO I know has a security detail... It's a nutso way to live. We just decided that's not how we wanted to raise our kids."
