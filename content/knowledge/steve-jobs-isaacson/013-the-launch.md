# The Launch

> **Key principle:** With the code barely finished on deadline (after Jobs refused to accept a two-week slip: "There's no way we're slipping!"), Apple's launch combined three deliberately engineered ingredients — a landmark television ad, cultivated media coverage, and a dramatic live unveiling — to make the Macintosh's debut feel like a historic event rather than a product release.

*Synthesized from Chapter 15, "The Launch," of Steve Jobs by Walter Isaacson.*

## Key lessons

- At the October 1983 Hawaii sales conference, Jobs staged a "Dating Game" skit with Bill Gates as a contestant, who predicted (accurately, for the moment) that Microsoft would get half its 1984 revenue from Mac software — even as Business Week's cover that same week declared IBM the decisive winner of the PC market.
- One week before the January 16 ship date, engineers said they needed two more weeks; Jobs refused flatly and told them "I'm going to ship the code a week from Monday, with your names on it" — they finished after all-nighters fueled by chocolate-covered espresso beans.
- The "1984" ad, directed by Ridley Scott fresh off Blade Runner, cast Apple as a rebel smashing an Orwellian Big Brother; Jobs saw it as reclaiming his hacker/counterculture identity even as the Mac itself — closed case, no expansion slots, no free manuals — violated the actual hacker ethic.
- Apple's board hated the ad after a December 1983 screening — Markkula reportedly asked "Who wants to move to find a new agency?" — and Sculley tried to sell back the two Super Bowl ad slots Chiat/Day had bought; the agency quietly failed to sell the 60-second spot, and marketing head Bill Campbell decided to "go for it."
- The ad aired once, during Super Bowl XVIII's third quarter, was seen by roughly 96 million people, generated same-night news coverage on all three networks, and was later named the greatest commercial of all time by both TV Guide and Advertising Age.
- Jobs cultivated press personally and specifically — flying Andy Hertzfeld and Burrell Smith to meet Katharine Graham at Newsweek, and pitching Rolling Stone's Steven Levy on a cover story he never landed, telling Levy the magazine needed the Mac as its "salvation."
- At the January 24, 1984 shareholders' meeting, Jobs opened with a Bob Dylan poem, delivered his IBM/1984 battle speech, then unveiled the Mac live: it introduced itself in a synthesized voice ("Hello. I'm Macintosh... Never trust a computer you can't lift"), ending with a five-minute standing ovation.
- Asked by a Popular Science reporter what market research had informed the Mac, Jobs shot back: "Did Alexander Graham Bell do any market research before he invented the telephone?"
