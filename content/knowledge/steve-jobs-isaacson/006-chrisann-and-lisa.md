# Chrisann and Lisa

> **Key principle:** Jobs's on-and-off relationship with Chrisann Brennan produced a daughter, Lisa Nicole Brennan, born May 17, 1978, whom Jobs initially refused to acknowledge as his, fighting a San Mateo County paternity suit and telling a reporter that "28% of the male population in the United States" could be the father, even after a UCLA DNA test put his own paternity probability at 94.41%. He later called his handling of it something he regretted, one of the few admissions of remorse in his life.

*Synthesized from Chapter 7, "Chrisann and Lisa," of Steve Jobs by Walter Isaacson.*

## Key lessons

- Jobs and Brennan's relationship, dating back to his final year at Homestead High, cycled on and off for roughly five years, complicated by both living (along with Daniel Kottke) in a shared rental house nicknamed "Rancho Suburbia" in Cupertino. Brennan became pregnant in 1977 while technically not "going out" with Jobs, having just her own bedroom in the house.
- Jobs's response to the pregnancy was denial and disengagement; friend Greg Calhoun recalled, "Steve was just not dealing with Chrisann or the pregnancy... There was a side to him that was frighteningly cold." Jobs told Isaacson he doubted paternity because he believed Brennan was seeing other men, which she and friends denied.
- Jobs opposed adoption for the child, given his own history, but said he was open to abortion; Brennan chose to keep the baby and gave birth at Robert Friedland's All One Farm commune in Oregon, assisted by a midwife, with Jobs flying up three days later to help name her. He insisted on an American name rather than a commune-style spiritual one; they named her Lisa Nicole Brennan, without the surname Jobs.
- The San Mateo County paternity suit forced the issue: Jobs initially fought it, having his lawyers try to get Kottke to testify he'd never seen the couple in bed together and seeking evidence Brennan had slept with others. A year after Lisa's birth, with Apple's IPO approaching, Jobs agreed to a UCLA DNA test that returned a 94.41% probability of paternity.
- Despite the test, Jobs continued to publicly minimize the finding, telling board member Arthur Rock there was "a large probability that he wasn't the father" and telling a Time reporter that 28% of American men statistically could have fathered the child, a claim Brennan initially and mistakenly took as an accusation that she'd slept with that share of the male population.
- Courts ordered Jobs to pay $385 a month in child support, sign an admission of paternity, and reimburse San Mateo County $5,856 in back welfare payments Brennan had received while raising Lisa alone.
- Years later Jobs expressed regret directly to Isaacson: "I wish I had handled it differently... if I could do it over, I would do a better job," noting he eventually supported Lisa financially through age eighteen, bought a house for her and Brennan to live in rent-free, and paid for her schooling.
- Around the same period, Jobs's personal habits shifted: he cut back on drugs and Zen retreats, started getting professional haircuts and suits from Wilkes Bashford, and began dating Regis McKenna employee Barbara Jasinski, while retaining some rebellious streaks (skinny-dipping in Felt Lake, an orange-tasseled BMW motorcycle, dressing as Jesus Christ at Apple's 1979 Halloween party).
- His first proper house purchase, in the Los Gatos hills, showed his perfectionism turning into near-paralysis: obsessive about furnishings, he left it largely barren, sleeping on a mattress on the floor beneath framed pictures of Einstein and his old guru Neem Karoli Baba (Maharaj-ji), with an Apple II on the floor.
