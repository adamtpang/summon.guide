# Africa

> **Key principle:** Long before Tesla or SpaceX, a twelve-year-old Musk sold source code for a video game he wrote himself; the chapter argues that same fusion of self-taught technical obsession and refusal to be stopped was hammered into him by a broken home, a difficult father, relentless bullies, and a family tradition of reckless adventure.

*Synthesized from Chapter 2 of Elon Musk: Tesla, SpaceX, and the Quest for a Fantastic Future by Ashlee Vance.*

## Key lessons

- In 1984, South African magazine PC and Office Technology published the source code to Blastar, a 167-line space shoot-em-up game Musk designed at age twelve under the byline "E. R. Musk"; it netted him $500 and previewed both his sci-fi imagination and his taste for grand-conquest themes.
- Musk's maternal line traces to Joshua Norman Haldeman, an eccentric Canadian chiropractor and self-taught pilot who lost five thousand acres of farmland in the Depression, then in 1950 sold everything and moved the family to Pretoria, later flying a single-engine plane 22,000 miles to Scotland and Norway and 30,000 miles to Australia and back; Musk credits this grandfather, who died in a 1974 plane-landing accident, as the likely source of his own risk tolerance.
- Musk's mother, Maye, was a science-minded teenager and later a modeling finalist for Miss South Africa; his father, Errol, pursued her for roughly seven years before marriage. Maye became pregnant on their honeymoon and gave birth to Elon on June 28, 1971, with Kimbal and sister Tosca following soon after.
- As a young child Elon would drift into trance-like states so complete that his parents initially suspected deafness and had his adenoids removed; Musk later described it as a kind of internal "graphics chip" that let him visualize objects, mechanics, and their interrelationships with unusual clarity.
- His reading was compulsive: Kimbal recalled him reading up to ten hours a day and two books in a single sitting on weekends; after exhausting the school and neighborhood libraries by around third or fourth grade he started reading the Encyclopaedia Britannica cover to cover, developing a near-photographic recall that made him the family's go-to answer machine ("just ask genius boy").
- His parents divorced when he was around eight or nine; Maye took the children to Durban, but Elon chose to move in with Errol, reasoning his father was alone while his mother had three kids. Both Elon and Kimbal describe the years with Errol, an intense engineer who would lecture them for three or four hours straight, as a form of psychological torture neither would detail explicitly, though Musk called it flatly "not a happy childhood... it was like misery."
- Around age ten, Musk saw his first computer at a hi-fi store in Sandton City Mall and badgered his father into buying him a Commodore VIC-20; he burned through a BASIC programming workbook meant to take six months in three sleepless days.
- With his brother and Rive cousins (Russ, Lyndon, Peter), Elon ran childhood ventures like door-to-door Easter egg sales and homemade explosives and rockets improvised from saltpeter, sulfur, charcoal, and other chemicals since South Africa had no Estes rocket kits; the boys also won a Dungeons & Dragons tournament in Johannesburg with Elon as Dungeon Master.
- At Bryanston High School he was beaten badly enough by a gang, kicked in the head and thrown down a flight of concrete stairs, that he was hospitalized for about a week; the bullying continued for three or four years, and the gang even coerced his best friend into luring him out of hiding to be beaten again, an episode Musk still visibly struggled to describe as an adult.
- At seventeen, using a change in Canadian law that let Maye pass her citizenship to her children, Musk left South Africa for Canada, citing both a longstanding pull toward America and a wish to avoid mandatory military service that would have enforced apartheid; he had already dropped out of a brief five-month stint at the University of Pretoria.
