# Canada

> **Key principle:** Musk's year of drifting through odd jobs across Canada, followed by his transformation into an intensely ambitious college student, shows the same pattern that defines his later career: undersupplied and underprepared, he simply refuses to accept a closed door.

*Synthesized from Chapter 3 of Elon Musk: Tesla, SpaceX, and the Quest for a Fantastic Future by Ashlee Vance.*

## Key lessons

- Musk landed in Montreal in June 1988 with no real plan, discovered by pay phone that the great-uncle he intended to stay with had already moved to Minnesota, and spent his first nights in a youth hostel before buying a $100 countrywide bus pass and riding 1,900 miles to Swift Current, Saskatchewan, to find a second cousin he called cold from the bus station.
- He spent roughly a year working odd jobs: tending vegetables and shoveling grain bins at a cousin's farm in Waldeck (where he turned eighteen), cutting logs with a chainsaw in Vancouver, and, worst of all, a lumber mill boiler-room job at $18 an hour that required crawling through a tunnel in a hazmat suit to shovel out steaming residue; thirty workers started that week, and by the end only Musk and two others remained.
- Once his mother Maye, brother Kimbal, and sister Tosca joined him in Canada, Elon enrolled at Queen's University in Kingston, Ontario in 1989, reportedly choosing it over the University of Waterloo partly because he judged the women better-looking there.
- At Queen's, Elon and Kimbal cold-called interesting people from the newspaper to request lunch meetings; one target, Bank of Nova Scotia executive Peter Nicholson, took six months to book but was won over by the brothers' persistence and ended up giving Elon a summer internship and becoming a lasting mentor.
- Musk met Justine Wilson at Queen's and pursued her doggedly after being stood up on their first date, tracking down her study spot and favorite ice cream flavor through her best friend; Justine later described him as relentless, "like the Terminator," and said "bit by bit, he won me over."
- Roommate and friend Navaid Farooq recalled Musk selling custom-built and repaired PCs out of their dorm to make money, dominating an economics exam so thoroughly it stood out even among a high-achieving cohort, and losing himself for hours playing Civilization.
- In 1992, after two years at Queen's, Musk transferred to the University of Pennsylvania on scholarship to pursue dual degrees, economics at Wharton and physics; there he became close friends with Adeo Ressi, and the two rented a ten-bedroom former frat house that they turned into an unlicensed weekend nightclub charging five dollars for all-you-can-drink parties of up to 500 people, with Maye once working the door as coat-check and cash collector.
- At Penn, Musk wrote a December 1994 business-school paper titled "The Importance of Being Solar" predicting the rise of large-scale solar power and sketching orbital solar arrays beaming energy to Earth via microwave, earning a 98; a related paper on ultracapacitors as a next-generation energy-storage technology scored 97 and drew praise for its financial rigor.
- Musk has said that by his college years he had already settled on the Internet, renewable energy, and space as the three fields most likely to reshape the world, and resolved to pursue projects in all three, an origin story he insists predates his actual ventures rather than being invented afterward to seem visionary.
