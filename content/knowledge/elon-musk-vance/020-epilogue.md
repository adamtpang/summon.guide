# Epilogue: A Body That Remains Very Much in Motion

> **Key principle:** Vance closes by arguing Musk is not a serial entrepreneur chasing new markets but "a man on a quest" who has become almost addicted to expanding that quest's scope, and whose apparent aloofness is really an unusual form of empathy aimed at the human species rather than at any one person in front of him.

*Synthesized from the Epilogue of Elon Musk: Tesla, SpaceX, and the Quest for a Fantastic Future by Ashlee Vance.*

## Key lessons

- As the book was being finished, Musk announced plans for a space-based internet: thousands of small satellites close to Earth beaming down high-speed bandwidth, aimed at underserved regions and at giving Mars a future global communications network; he called it a project "I don't see anyone else doing."
- SpaceX secured $1 billion from Google and Fidelity to fund the effort, which Musk estimated would cost more than $10 billion to build and, unusually for him, declined to give a firm delivery date, saying only "not sooner than five years," and framing it as a long-term revenue source to help fund a city on Mars.
- Other companies kept moving in parallel: SolarCity bought a new R&D facility next to the Tesla factory (notably the former Solyndra plant), the Nevada Gigafactory construction continued, Tesla's charging network had by then saved an estimated four million gallons of gas, and Straubel promised home battery systems would ship in 2015.
- Musk's ambition kept escalating in public statements too: he predicted Tesla could eventually surpass Apple in value and become the first trillion-dollar company, while outside groups began building prototype Hyperloop systems around California and Musk made a cameo in a Simpsons episode.
- The expansion had real cracks by early 2015: Tesla's China sales were weak enough to revive Wall Street skepticism about long-term Model S demand, the stock slumped, and Musk himself sounded flustered defending the company for the first time in a while.
- The personal cost showed too: Musk told Vance he was divorcing Talulah Riley again, saying he'd tried to talk her out of it but she wanted "a simpler, smaller life" away from Los Angeles.
- Vance's final assessment is that Musk's brand of quest is more consuming than most people's, that he's become almost addicted to announcing new fronts (Hyperloop, the space internet), and that his apparent emotional aloofness toward individuals coexists with a deep feeling for humanity as a whole, an unusual, species-scale kind of empathy Vance argues may be exactly what it takes to make a project like a space internet real.
