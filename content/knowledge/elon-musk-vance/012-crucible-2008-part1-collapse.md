# The Crucible of 2008, Part 1: Collapse

> **Key principle:** By mid-2008 Musk's Hollywood-elevated public image, his marriage, and the cash reserves of both SpaceX and Tesla were all failing at once, setting up the single hardest year of his life.

*Synthesized from Chapter 8 of Elon Musk: Tesla, SpaceX, and the Quest for a Fantastic Future by Ashlee Vance.*

## Key lessons

- In March 2007, Robert Downey Jr. toured SpaceX's El Segundo facility before filming Iron Man and came away so struck by Musk that he asked director Jon Favreau to put a Tesla Roadster in Tony Stark's workshop, cementing the "real-life Tony Stark" comparison that made Musk a public figure beyond "that PayPal guy."
- Musk's rising fame came with a Hollywood lifestyle: a Bel Air house next to Quincy Jones and Girls Gone Wild founder Joe Francis, black-tie fundraisers alongside Paris Hilton and Leonardo DiCaprio, and Musk's own estimate that his life had become "10 percent playboy and 90 percent engineer." Wife Justine's blog documented it all, including Francis handing over a yellow envelope with $100,000 cash to buy a Roadster.
- Justine felt reduced to a "trophy wife" and an "arm ornament" during this period; she'd had triplets (Kai, Damian, Saxon) near the end of 2006, on top of sons Griffin and Xavier, and by her account suffered postpartum depression while Musk worked seven days a week split between Los Angeles and San Francisco.
- The press turned hard in late 2007: Valleywag's Owen Thomas mined Musk's Zip2 and PayPal ousters to brand him a "master manipulator," and Valleywag named the delayed Tesla Roadster its No. 1 tech fail of 2007 as the New York Times and auto blogs piled on Tesla's transmission problems.
- Musk filed for divorce on June 16, 2008, reportedly after giving Justine a same-day ultimatum to "fix" the marriage or be divorced the next morning, then cutting off her credit card once he'd filed. The eventual settlement gave Justine the house, $2 million cash, $80,000/month in alimony and child support for seventeen years, and a Roadster.
- SpaceX engineer Kevin Brogan described Musk's burn-rate obsession going into 2008: Musk would refuse to approve a $2,000 part purchase and demand something cheaper, but would casually approve a $90,000 charter flight to Kwajalein if it saved a day of schedule, framing every decision against an expected future revenue of "ten million dollars a day."
- Days after filing for divorce, Musk's friend Bill Lee took him to London to cheer him up; at the club Whisky Mist in early July 2008 Musk met 22-year-old actress Talulah Riley, and within roughly five days of her follow-up visit to Los Angeles he proposed to her at the Peninsula Hotel in Beverly Hills ("We can shake on it if you like. And we did.").
- By late July 2008, Musk calculated he had just barely enough cash on hand to survive to the end of the year, with both SpaceX and Tesla needing fresh cash infusions amid a collapsing global financial market that had frozen new investment. Riley recalled: "He would come home every day, and there would be some calamity... He was under immense pressure from all quarters. It was horrendous."
- In a dinner earlier that year with close friend and Tesla investor Antonio Gracias, Musk laid out how far he'd go: "I will spend my last dollar on these companies. If we have to move into Justine's parents' basement, we'll do it."
