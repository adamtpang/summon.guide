# Elon's First Start-Up

> **Key principle:** Zip2 shows the founding pattern Musk would repeat for the rest of his career: total physical commitment (sleeping on a beanbag, showering at the YMCA), a genuinely useful but unglamorous idea (a Yellow Pages merged with digital maps), and a bruising lesson about what happens to a founder's authority once venture capital and a "real" CEO come in.

*Synthesized from Chapter 4 of Elon Musk: Tesla, SpaceX, and the Quest for a Fantastic Future by Ashlee Vance.*

## Key lessons

- The idea came out of a cross-country road trip Musk took with his brother Kimbal in August 1994 in a beat-up 1970s BMW 320i, funded by Kimbal selling off his stake in a College Pro Painters franchise. Along the way they brainstormed business ideas, including an online network for doctors that never went anywhere.
- The real spark was a failed sales pitch: a Yellow Pages salesman tried to sell online listings to a Silicon Valley start-up where Musk was interning and clearly didn't understand the internet himself. Musk's takeaway was that if the salesman didn't get it, there was an opening, and he called Kimbal to say so.
- In 1995 the brothers founded Global Link Information Network in Palo Alto, later renamed Zip2: a searchable business directory tied to maps and turn-by-turn directions, essentially an early combination of Yelp and Google Maps. Musk pitched it through the example of finding the nearest pizza parlor.
- They ran the company on almost nothing: a $28,000 loan from their father Errol Musk, a pirated fast internet connection Musk rigged by drilling through the office wall to an ISP one floor down, and a licensed business-listings database merged with free mapping data Musk talked Navteq into handing over.
- Musk and Kimbal lived at the Zip2 office for the first three months, showering at the YMCA and eating repeatedly at Jack in the Box. Musk slept on a beanbag next to his desk and asked early employees to physically wake him up when they arrived in the morning.
- Early sales were brutal door-to-door work pitching skeptical small businesses on paying for an internet listing; most owners thought the whole idea was ridiculous. Canadian businessman Greg Kouri joined as a cofounder in 1996, bankrolling the company with a wad of cash from his bathrobe pocket and serving as the "adult supervision," including physically breaking up fistfights between Elon and Kimbal.
- Mohr Davidow Ventures invested $3 million in early 1996, which triggered the founding wound of Musk's career: the VCs pushed him out of the CEO role into chief technology officer and installed Rich Sorkin as CEO, a demotion Musk resented for the rest of his life.
- Zip2 pivoted from selling directly to businesses toward licensing its mapping/directory software to newspapers (The New York Times, Knight Ridder, Hearst), who needed a fast way to build online classifieds and real estate listings, bringing in $50 million in additional funding.
- A planned 1998 merger with rival CitySearch collapsed amid disputes over the numbers and job cuts; Musk pushed the board to fire Sorkin and reinstate him as CEO, and the board refused, instead stripping Musk of his chairman title and installing Derek Proudian. Months later, in February 1999, Compaq bought Zip2 outright for $307 million in cash, netting Musk $22 million and Kimbal $15 million.
- Looking back, Musk admitted his managerial failures at Zip2 directly: he assumed employees shared his own information and instincts, rewrote colleagues' code without asking (including correcting an engineer's quantum-mechanics equation on a whiteboard), and set unrealistic one-hour deadlines that staff learned to interpret as one to two weeks.
