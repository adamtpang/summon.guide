# The Crucible of 2008, Part 2: The December Save

> **Key principle:** With both companies nearly out of cash by December 2008, Musk personally funded both rescues in parallel, and a NASA contract and a bankruptcy-eve financing round landed within days of each other to save SpaceX and Tesla simultaneously.

*Synthesized from Chapter 8 of Elon Musk: Tesla, SpaceX, and the Quest for a Fantastic Future by Ashlee Vance.*

## Key lessons

- On July 30, 2008, the new Falcon 9 rocket had a successful nine-engine test fire in Texas producing 850,000 pounds of thrust, even as the older Falcon 1's third Kwajalein launch attempt was still looming as SpaceX's most urgent problem.
- Falcon 1's third flight (August 2, 2008) failed when a redesigned Merlin engine cooling system produced unexpected thrust during stage separation, damaging the rocket. Musk called it a disaster: with roughly $100 million already spent and nothing left over from Tesla's drain, he said flatly, "Flight four was it" — SpaceX had no money for a fifth attempt if flight four failed too.
- Preparing for the fourth launch, engineers flying the rocket body from Los Angeles to Hawaii on a military cargo plane discovered the pressurized cabin was crushing the rocket's thin (less than one-eighth-inch) skin mid-flight; the SpaceX team used pocketknives to cut shrink wrap and wrenches to open pressure-relief nuts in the air, saving the airframe from what could have been a three-month repair.
- The fourth Falcon 1 launch succeeded on September 28, 2008, becoming the first privately built rocket to reach orbit after six years and roughly 500 people's work. Musk raced back from a Disneyland outing with brother Kimbal to reach the control room about two minutes before liftoff.
- Even as SpaceX celebrated, Tesla's press crisis deepened: the "Tesla Death Watch" blog launched on Truth About Cars in May 2008, Top Gear aired a damaging test, and in October 2008 Valleywag published a leaked employee email revealing Tesla had laid off staff, closed its Detroit office, and had only $9 million left in the bank.
- Musk described hitting a personal low during this stretch: "I was just getting pistol-whipped... I felt like a pile of shit. I didn't think we would overcome it. I thought things were probably fucking doomed." He weighed splitting his remaining money between the two companies versus committing it all to just one, reasoning that splitting it risked killing both.
- To keep Tesla alive, Musk leaned on friends for personal loans (Bill Lee invested $2 million, Sergey Brin $500,000), gave up his private jet for Southwest flights, and burned through roughly $4 million a month at Tesla while Kimbal sold his remaining investments and came close to personal bankruptcy to help fund the company.
- In December 2008, Musk scraped together roughly $20 million of his own money (via a NASA-approved loan from SpaceX, selling SolarCity shares, and about $15 million from Dell's acquisition of his cousins' startup Everdream) to match a Tesla financing round; when VantagePoint's Alan Salzman balked over the valuation days before the deadline, Musk recharacterized the raise as a debt deal to strip VantagePoint's blocking power and bluffed he'd personally fund the entire $40 million. The round closed on Christmas Eve, hours before Tesla would have gone bankrupt, with Musk personally contributing $12 million.
- On December 23, 2008, SpaceX won NASA's Commercial Resupply Services contract worth $1.6 billion for twelve flights to the International Space Station, despite friction with NASA administrator Michael Griffin. Musk broke down in tears at Kimbal's home in Boulder, Colorado, and, having had no time to buy Talulah Riley a real Christmas gift, ran out and bought her plastic "see no evil, hear no evil" monkey trinkets from the only store still open. Investor Antonio Gracias's verdict on the year: "He didn't just survive. He kept working and stayed focused... I've just never seen anything like his ability to take pain."
