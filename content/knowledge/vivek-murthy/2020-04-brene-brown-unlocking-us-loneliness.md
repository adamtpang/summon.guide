## Key lessons

- Situation: Murthy speaks of his own loneliness in two periods: childhood, when he was loved at home but lacked school friendships and dreaded the cafeteria, and after he left government in 2017, when he says he felt lost, burned out, ashamed, and "really, really lonely."
- What he did: he could not fix it alone. He describes opening up to close friends about struggling, working on self-acceptance, and building community. He says this experience is why he focused later work on loneliness and social connection.
- He adds that even now he sometimes feels ashamed to ask for help because he feels he should manage alone. Rights note: the transcript is copyrighted by Brene Brown Education and Research Group; this note paraphrases and quotes minimally.
- Transferable lesson: shame turns people inward, and the way out runs through one honest conversation with someone safe, not through toughing it out.
