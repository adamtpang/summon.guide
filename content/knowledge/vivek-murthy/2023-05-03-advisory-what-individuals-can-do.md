## Key lessons

- Situation: after the population-level analysis, the advisory closes with a list of actions any person can take today, alongside recommendations for institutions.
- What it recommends: reach out to a friend or family member each day; put the phone away during meals and important conversations; look for ways to serve others; be responsive and practice gratitude; join groups such as fitness, faith, hobby or volunteer groups; and cut back on practices that breed disconnection, such as heavy social media use or time in unhealthy relationships.
- It also tells people to seek help when loneliness is heavy: reach out to family, a friend, a counselor or health care provider, or the 988 crisis line, and be open with a doctor about big social changes in life.
- Transferable lesson: quality beats volume. Frequent, brief, fully present contact and helping someone else are low-cost moves that work even when you do not feel confident. The advisory frames these as first steps, not a cure for chronic loneliness.
