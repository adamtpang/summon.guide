## Key lessons

- Situation: Murthy began his first term not thinking of loneliness as a public health issue. A cross-country listening tour changed that, because people kept saying things like they had to shoulder life's burdens alone, or that nobody would notice if they disappeared.
- What the advisory argues: loneliness and isolation are related but different. Isolation is objectively having few relationships or little contact. Loneliness is the subjective, distressing gap between the connection a person wants and the connection they have, so a person can be lonely in a crowd or content alone.
- Concrete facts from the text: about half of US adults report loneliness, with high rates among young adults; the advisory reports 26% and 29% higher risk of premature death for loneliness and isolation respectively, and compares the mortality impact of disconnection to smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day. It also notes fewer than 20% of people who often feel lonely see it as a major problem.
- Transferable lesson: name the feeling accurately before trying to fix it. Wanting more connection is a signal, like hunger, and says nothing bad about your worth. In the letter Murthy writes that the keys to human connection are "simple, but extraordinarily powerful." (Advisory letter, 2023)
