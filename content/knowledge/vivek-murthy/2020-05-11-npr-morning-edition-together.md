## Key lessons

- Situation: on his listening tour Murthy heard stories of addiction and depression, and behind them, threads of loneliness. People rarely said the word; they said they had to shoulder burdens alone or that no one would notice if they disappeared.
- What he argued: there is a tremendous sense of shame around loneliness, because admitting it feels like admitting you are unlikable. He says he felt that shame as a child and at points in adulthood. Short-term loneliness pushes us to seek people; prolonged loneliness becomes chronic stress that harms body and mind.
- He says connection with others begins with connection with yourself: knowing your own value makes it easier to listen and to be authentic without fear of being judged.
- Transferable lesson: treat loneliness like hunger or thirst, as information. He told NPR, "loneliness is a natural signal that our body gives us, similar to hunger, thirst." (Murthy, NPR, 2020)
