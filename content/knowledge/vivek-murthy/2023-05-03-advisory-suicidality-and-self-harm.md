## Key lessons

- Situation: chapter 2 reviews how social connection affects health, including a short section on suicide and self-harm, where loneliness often sits alongside other pain.
- What it says: research over more than a century links lack of social connection to death by suicide, especially for men. In one study of over 500,000 middle-aged adults, the probability of dying by suicide more than doubled among men who lived alone, and loneliness was tied to hospitalization for self-harm among women. The advisory says the link may run through a low sense of belonging and perceiving oneself as a burden.
- The advisory concludes that social connection "may be one of the strongest protective factors against self-harm and suicide." (Advisory, chapter 2)
- Transferable lesson for this guide: when someone describes loneliness together with hopelessness or thoughts of ending their life, connection is part of the answer but not enough on its own. The right move is a human crisis service now (988 in the US), not advice about friendships.
