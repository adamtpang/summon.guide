## Key lessons

- The statement was issued through UC Berkeley as reports emerged from China, in the week of the Second International Summit on Human Genome Editing in Hong Kong.
- She says the scientists responsible must fully explain why they broke from the "global consensus" that germline editing with CRISPR-Cas9 should not go ahead at present.
- She asks the public to keep in mind that the clinical report had not appeared in the peer-reviewed literature.
- She adds that without peer-reviewed data, nobody can judge how accurate the editing actually was.
- She says the reported work strengthens the case for restricting embryo editing to situations with a clear unmet medical need and no other workable medical option, as the National Academy of Sciences had recommended.
- She insists the news should not overshadow legitimate clinical work using CRISPR to treat disease in adults and children.
- She calls for public and transparent discussion of genome editing to continue, and the page notes she was due to moderate a summit panel on public engagement that week.
