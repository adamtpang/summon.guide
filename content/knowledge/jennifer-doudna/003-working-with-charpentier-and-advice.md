## Key lessons

- She says her interest began with math, puzzles with her father, and curiosity about how plants and animals had evolved on Hawaii, which led her to want to study the chemistry of living things.
- She credits mentors for her path: her father, who was not a scientist but asked detailed questions about her lab work, encouraging school teachers, a college professor who gave her a summer lab place, and Jack Szostak in graduate school.
- She recounts meeting Charpentier at a microbiology conference in Puerto Rico in spring 2011, a meeting she says she was an unlikely invitee to because she is not a microbiologist. A colleague introduced them.
- She describes the two of them walking through old San Juan discussing a protein then called Csn1, later renamed Cas9. Some suspected it cut DNA, but nobody had shown it, and how it recognized viral DNA was unknown.
- She says the nine-hour time difference between California and Umea, Sweden made the data analysis and writing feel nearly continuous, since each picked up when the other went to bed. She calls the work intense and fun, and remembers Charpentier's dry humor.
- Her central advice is to "go for it": embrace what interests you and give it full effort. She observes that the most successful people in her lab are those who can handle their fears and the failures that come with trying.
- She says people who only dabble and pull back early never give themselves a chance, and tells young people to find supportive mentors for the hard stretches and keep pursuing a good idea even if they cannot predict how it will work out.
- She describes three ways she copes with setbacks: taking a long view of how a problem will look in months or years, relying on a network of supportive friends and colleagues, and drawing on a self-reliance she built while dealing with bullying as a child.
- She argues diversity produces better science because different minds bring different approaches, and says she hears women trainees underestimate themselves more often than men, so women should encourage each other to set that voice aside.
