Read as the Project Gutenberg plain text, ebook 46976 (https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46976/pg46976.txt), downloaded in this session and verified by its title, author and translator header.

- Parts read: Read in full: Arrian's Preface; Book 1 chs. 12-16 (Ilium, Arrian's statement of purpose, Memnon's advice, the Granicus); Book 2 chs. 6-12 (Issus and Darius's family) and chs. 16-24 (siege and capture of Tyre); Book 3 chs. 8-15 (Gaugamela, called Arbela in this edition); Book 4 chs. 8-14 (Clitus, prostration, Callisthenes, the pages' conspiracy); Book 5 chs. 9-19 (Hydaspes, Porus, Bucephalas) and chs. 25-29 (Hyphasis mutiny and return); Book 6 chs. 24-26 (Gedrosian desert); Book 7 chs. 8-12 (Opis, reconciliation, veterans sent home) and chs. 24-30 (omen, illness, death, poison rumours, character and closing assessment).

## Caveats

- Translation is Chinnock's 1884 English; name spellings follow it (Clitus, Porus, Gadrosia for Gedrosia, Arbela for Gaugamela in chapter headings). Chapter headings are the translator's, not Arrian's.
- Army sizes and casualty figures (600,000 at Issus, 1,000,000 infantry and 300,000 dead at Gaugamela, 100,000 dead at Issus) are Arrian's reported figures, usually introduced with 'it was said', and are not reliable counts.
- The speeches (Alexander before Issus, at Tyre, at the Hyphasis, at Opis; Coenus; Callisthenes) are Arrian's literary compositions, written centuries after the events.
- Arrian flags source disagreement in the passages read: the weapon and blame in Clitus's death; the fate of Callisthenes (Aristobulus versus Ptolemy); three versions of the fight with Porus's son; whether the water-pouring incident happened in Gedrosia; the Hephaestion and Darius's mother story; the last words and poison rumours.
- Note 008 covers three separate stretches (Book 6 chs. 24-26, Book 7 chs. 8-12 and 24-30) in one note, so it is denser than the others.
- The Philotas and Parmenio executions, the Mallian campaign, the destruction of Thebes and Persepolis were not read, so no note covers them; they are mentioned only where a read passage refers to them (Hermolaus's reported charges, Book 4 ch. 14).

No source text is stored here. Every lesson is a paraphrase.
