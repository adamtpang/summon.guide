## Key lessons

- Situation: an essay first published on 1729.com on 26 May 2021.
- His distinction: you can found an institution or inherit it. He counts winning an election as a form of inheriting, since the winner takes over something they could not have built from scratch.
- Read-only culture is his term for being able to repeat what was handed down without being able to recreate it from first principles, like understanding a language you cannot write.
- He uses stock and flow: the artifacts around us are the stock, and if nobody can still produce them the flow has stopped, even though nothing looks wrong yet.
- His reading of 2020 is "Where heirs failed, founders succeeded": he argues many public institutions faltered under COVID-19 while internet companies kept working. This is his interpretation and a partisan one; many would dispute how he scores each institution.
- He admits the limit: regulated functions like education, healthcare and city government cannot be fixed by a typical startup, which is what leads him to ask how to found cities and states.
- Transferable lesson: test whether you could rebuild the thing you run from scratch. If not, learn the parts you only know how to operate.
