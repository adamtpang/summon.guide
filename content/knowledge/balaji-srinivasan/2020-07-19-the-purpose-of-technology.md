## Key lessons

- Situation: an essay on his own site, published 19 July 2020.
- His argument: breakthroughs are described as faster, smaller, cheaper, better, which all mean doing more with less, so the near-term purpose of technology is to reduce scarcity.
- He extends it: mortality is the main source of scarcity because time is finite, so he calls life extension the most important thing we can invent. This is his view and a contested one; he links studies but the essay is advocacy, not a medical review.
- He says people with technical backgrounds have not treated writing about progress as a duty, and that builders are usually too busy building to explain their work to the public or the next generation.
- His call is for technologists to learn to write, report, publish and direct, and to tell full stories with progress at the center, not only tweets and conference talks.
- On startups he writes that the point of doing one is to "build something you can't buy", and that a company is a means to a goal, not the goal.
- Transferable lesson: if your work matters, explaining it clearly and repeatedly is part of the work, not a distraction from it.
