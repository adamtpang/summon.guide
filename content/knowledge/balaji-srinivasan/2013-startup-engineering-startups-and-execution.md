## Key lessons

- Situation: a later section of the same 2013 lecture notes, on what kind of business to build and how to execute.
- He separates startups from small businesses. A startup, in his definition, is about growth, aiming at global reach, usually with new technology and an unproven model. A small business serves a limited market and usually has to make a profit right away.
- On venture capital he uses the image of strapping a jetpack to someone running at a trot: it can end in a wipeout or a flight to the moon. The deciding factor is your own ambition and whether you can tolerate the business failing.
- His rule, in his exact words, starts "if you can't go to zero" and ends that you should not try to go to infinity.
- A startup has to show an economy of scale: he works a small table where per-unit cost falls as volume rises, to show why shifting per-unit costs into fixed costs (for example through software) matters.
- The execution mindset: do the next thing on the list at all times, and rewrite the list every day and week. He says this means saying no to other people, to distractions and to fun.
- He cites two execution habits from others: Peter Thiel's rule that everyone knows their one thing, and Marc Andreessen's anti-todo list of writing down what you just did.
- Transferable lesson: name which game you are playing before you raise money, and keep one visible priority per person.
