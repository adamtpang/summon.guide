## Key lessons

- Situation: an essay first published on 1729.com on 11 May 2021, describing the step between an online community and a network state.
- He starts from Robert Putnam's observation that Americans were bowling alone as unions, clubs and religious groups declined, and says internet groups are quietly rebuilding community.
- His complaint about today's social networks is that they are mostly used for idle sharing and dunking. A network union adds a leader, a stated purpose and a way to pay and coordinate members.
- Network unions, he writes, prioritize "daily action over occasional elections": bargaining for bulk purchases, getting a deplatformed member reinstated, helping a member find a job, funding a member's startup.
- Unlike a traditional union it can take the side of consumers, users or producers, not only labor, depending on who its members are.
- He leans heavily on crypto tokens for payments, membership and measuring a community's value. Treat this as his design proposal; it is not investment advice and token values can go to zero.
- Transferable lesson: give a community one purpose and a regular shared task, and measure it by what members do for each other.
