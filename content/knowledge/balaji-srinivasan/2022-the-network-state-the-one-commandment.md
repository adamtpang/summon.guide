## Key lessons

- Situation: a section of his 2022 book The Network State, in the chapter on history, published free to read online.
- His heading says communities are causes first and companies second: a startup society needs a moral premise at its core, not only a business model.
- He advises against writing a full set of commandments. Pick one issue where history and science have convinced you the mainstream is wrong, and where you can make the case in articles, videos and talks.
- The founder is a moral entrepreneur: the pitch is a better way of life to future members, not a faster or cheaper product to investors.
- Changing one rule keeps the complexity manageable, and it attracts what he calls "single-issue movers", people who will relocate or reorganize their lives for one cause.
- His examples scale with what the cause needs: a purely online union (a cancel-proof society offering a guild and insurance), a community with physical places (Keto Kosher, a sugar-free society; a Digital Sabbath society that goes offline part of the time), and ones that would need legal recognition.
- Transferable lesson for founders of anything: state the one thing you believe the world has wrong, and check whether your goal needs only a community, a physical place, or a change in law.
