# Running Your Company by Patrick Collison

> **Key principle:** Before product-market fit, the entire job is tightening the feedback loop between observed user behavior and shipped fixes, illustrated by Stripe's early team personally emailing every business that abandoned integration to ask what went wrong. After product-market fit, growth stops being a curve you nudge and becomes an addressable market you deliberately map and build an organization to serve.

*Patrick Collison (Stripe co-founder and CEO) on running your company, Y Combinator Startup School · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NprBQi0cSHU) · 57 min.*

## Key lessons

- Stripe took nearly two years from first line of code to public launch (largely due to required banking partnerships), which Collison explicitly does not recommend; during that time the founders deliberately expanded their user base a little each month rather than waiting for one big reveal, reaching about 100 users before the public launch.
- The "Collison installation," personally visiting a prospective user's office to install the product live, served two purposes at once: it exposed brutal, humbling UX problems no amount of internal design review would catch, and it created urgency, since a scheduled call almost always got postponed while an in-person visit forced a real "why not now" moment.
- Collison's operational definition of product-market fit: the moment the company's bottleneck flips from generating user demand to keeping up with it. Before that point, roughly 100 users produces some retained fraction that asymptotes downward; after that point, the same 100 users tell enough other people that growth becomes self-sustaining and compounding.
- Pre-product-market-fit metrics are described as "relatively unhelpful" compared to direct, granular qualitative inspection; early Stripe routed every single API request to an internal email, and any error triggered a high-priority alert so the team could personally follow up, sometimes within 15 minutes, even when the mistake was the user's own typo.
- On splitting CEO duties with his brother and co-founder John: rather than a rigid decision hierarchy, they lean on which of them has more relevant domain focus for a given area, and when they genuinely disagree, they weight the decision toward whichever of them feels more strongly about it, on the theory that stronger personal conviction correlates with better execution of that choice.
- Collison frames startup work through "fulfillment" rather than "happiness": the day-to-day experience of scaling Stripe was often stressful and far from pleasant in the moment, but reviewing progress on a monthly or longer time horizon (rather than the noisy day-to-day) revealed real movement and made the underlying effort feel worthwhile.
- The single most consequential early hire wasn't an engineer: on a mentor's advice, Stripe brought on a non-technical partnerships lead (Billy Alvarado) specifically to handle relationships with banks, a function the all-engineer founding team had no instinct for and had been struggling with badly; that hire is described as an immediate inflection point for the company.
- The biggest self-identified mistake in Stripe's own scaling was staying too consensus-oriented for too long as headcount grew; Collison's retrospective advice is that somewhere around 10-15 people, a company needs to deliberately shift toward clearer, more hierarchical decision rights (even though "hierarchical" sounds pejorative) because pure consensus stops being efficient and doesn't scale, while still preserving individual ownership and the ability for anyone to surface new ideas.
- To keep a growing company from calcifying into orthodoxy, Stripe runs an internal "crazy ideas" process where employees submit ideas explicitly required to sound "probably bad" (not just risky), specifically to counter self-censorship; real products, including Stripe Press and support for the Stellar cryptocurrency network, originated from this process, modeled on how Amazon and Google kept launching genuinely new product lines well after their core businesses matured.
