# How to Get Your First Customers | Startup School

> **Key principle:** Talking to customers and doing sales are the same skill, so founders have to run their own early sales rather than outsourcing it. Startups that skip this step and wait for a good product to sell itself almost never take off; founders have to manually recruit their first customers one at a time.

*Gustaf Alströmer (YC group partner, ex-Airbnb) on getting your first customers, Y Combinator Startup School · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hyYCn_kAngI) · 23 min.*

## Key lessons

- The "startup curve" (launch spike, trough of sorrow, wiggle of false hope, promised land of PMF) explains why most companies die in the quiet period right after launch; the founders who keep testing and shipping through that trough instead of quitting are the ones who eventually break through.
- A working outbound sales email should be short (6-8 sentences), written in plain text with no HTML formatting, free of jargon, and end with a single clear call to action; the early Brex beta-recruitment email to fellow YC founders is used as a real example that converted despite running a bit long.
- Sell to your easiest prospects first, not your biggest possible logo: startups make better early customers than large companies because they decide fast and lack the procurement bureaucracy that stalls enterprise deals, and people already in your network are easier to close than cold strangers.
- Skip free trials for B2B products; use a money-back guarantee or a month-to-month opt-out instead, and treat a prospect's refusal to pay anything as a legitimate signal to move on to a better-fit customer rather than evidence that the price needs to come down.
- Track the full sales funnel (prospect list, outreach, demo, pricing, close, onboarding) with real numbers. Founders who send too few outbound messages and get zero customers often wrongly conclude "sales doesn't work" for their product, when the real issue is that they never sent enough volume to reach a statistically meaningful conversion rate.
- Onboarding is the funnel step founders most often forget: without it, new customers under-use the product and quietly churn even after a successful close, since most early products are not self-explanatory.
- Scalable channels like SEO, paid search, and referral programs are almost never how a company's very first customers actually arrive, even when they become the dominant channel years later; early growth comes from manual, one-to-one selling that doesn't scale, exactly as Airbnb's earliest users came from Paul Graham's "do things that don't scale" playbook rather than any of Airbnb's later scalable channels.
