# Suhail Doshi - How to Measure Your Product

> **Key principle:** Growth is just five measurable steps in sequence: people visit, sign up, do something valuable, come back later, and spread the product to others. Those five steps map onto three plain-language questions worth obsessing over: do people understand what you made, is it easy to get started, and are people actually coming back, and answering those three well matters far more than building a sprawling 25-metric dashboard.

*Suhail Doshi (Mixpanel co-founder) on how to measure your product, Y Combinator Startup School · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MABmQhOlmJA) · 59 min.*

## Key lessons

- Companies at every size, even thousand-person ones, tend to over-measure: tracking 25 metrics split across teams produces decision paralysis and endless "well, maybe it was sunny outside" causation arguments, whereas picking three to five north-star numbers that actually guide the team is what Doshi has found works, based on watching thousands of companies (and making the same mistake himself early at Mixpanel).
- The five-step growth formula (visits to signups to valuable action to return visits to sharing) doubles as a diagnostic: a confusing landing page, a too-hard signup flow, or a product people don't value will each show up as a specific drop in a specific stage, which is enough to localize most growth problems without more sophisticated analysis.
- Mixpanel's own first landing page (Doshi's own design, tagline "metrics that'll make you drool") tried to appeal to everyone (startups, big companies, app developers) at once and had real usability problems; years and much iteration later it converged on "actions speak louder than pageviews," a positioning line that lasted roughly five years because it directly answered the single most common question prospects asked ("how is this different from Google Analytics?").
- Simple signals for whether people understand your product before real usage data exists: whether they bother to sign up at all, whether they click past the landing page into any deeper feature, and for B2B specifically, whether they click through to a pricing page, which Doshi calls a reasonably strong signal of real interest even before a purchase happens.
- Getting started has to be radically simple before any other feature matters: a YC company (an early Google Slides-style product) reportedly didn't implement a "save" button for the first year because they knew, from talking to users, nobody had gotten far enough to need it, and Mixpanel itself skipped "forgot password" for about 18 months by ruthlessly prioritizing what customers actually asked for.
- Concrete early-funnel traps to avoid: optimizing for fraud or spam problems a young startup doesn't actually have yet, and requiring email or SMS confirmation steps, which Doshi says routinely drop 60-70% of users who never click through (and risk landing in spam entirely); where possible, let people experience real product value before forcing signup at all, the way Airbnb and Google do.
- The "shark fin" pattern describes viral-but-unretentive growth: a spike of acquisition (often from a viral moment on a platform like Facebook or Instagram) followed by a churn rate that outpaces new signups, crashing the user base back down; reactivation campaigns after this point are described as reliably weak, and LivingSocial is cited as a company that had to pivot its entire business (into Groupon-style deals) after an earlier, viral-but-unretentive Facebook app failed to hold users.
- Track new-user retention (percentage of a signup cohort still active 7 and, more importantly, 30+ days later) and daily active users over monthly active users for products meant to be used often; Doshi calls monthly active users "the new BS metric" (comparing it to the once-popular but meaningless "registered users" figure) and reports Mixpanel found a strong, high-leverage correlation between daily engagement and lower revenue churn.
- Monthly revenue churn compounds brutally and non-intuitively: a seemingly tolerable 7% monthly churn rate actually erases about 58% of a company's revenue base over a year, meaning a company earning $1M has to first replace roughly $580K in lost revenue before any real net growth counts; Doshi describes this exact math as the source of some of his worst early sleepless nights at Mixpanel.
- With fewer than 50 users, skip formal analytics entirely and just talk to people directly; Doshi describes personally chatting with all 10-12 of Mixpanel's earliest customers over instant messenger to make real product decisions (such as choosing between a vertical or horizontal funnel visualization) long before there was enough volume for meaningful quantitative testing.
