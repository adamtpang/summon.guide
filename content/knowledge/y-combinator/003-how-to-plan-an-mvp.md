# Michael Seibel - How to Plan an MVP

> **Key principle:** A minimum viable product is nothing more than the simplest thing a founder can hand to their very first target users to see if it delivers any value at all. It is not supposed to be impressive, and none of the billion-dollar companies used as examples (Airbnb, Twitch, Stripe) started with anything close to their eventual product.

*Michael Seibel on planning and shipping a minimum viable product, Y Combinator Startup School · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1hHMwLxN6EM) · 14 min.*

## Key lessons

- The two most common ways founders sabotage themselves before an MVP even matters: never actually launching anything (a startlingly common way for founder journeys to quietly end), and refusing to seek feedback on the "shitty" early version because they've mentally attached to the "real" product still in their head.
- Seibel's rule for scope discipline: hold the problem you're solving and the customer you're solving it for tightly, but hold the specific solution loosely; if the first attempt fails, fix the solution rather than searching for a different problem the same tool might solve.
- Famous MVPs were rough by design: Airbnb's first version had no in-app payments (hosts and guests exchanged cash in person) and no map view, Twitch launched as Justin.tv with one person's low-resolution personal livestream and zero video games, and early Stripe had no bank deals and its founders personally flew out to integrate the product for each new user.
- "Heavy MVPs" apply in regulated or hard-tech industries (biotech, banking, drones, rockets) where a real product can't be built in weeks; the fallback there is a simple explanatory website that gives people something concrete to react to while the real build is underway.
- Big public launches are largely a myth for early-stage founders: almost nobody remembers the actual day Google, Facebook, or Twitter "launched," so the real early milestone worth chasing is simply getting any paying or using customer, not a splashy press moment.
- Practical build hacks: time-box the spec to what's realistically shippable in the chosen window (e.g., three weeks), write the spec down so scope creep becomes visible and honest, cut unimportant features first and important ones second rather than slip the deadline, and keep moving once anything is live, since delay compounds delay.
- Don't fall in love with the MVP. None of the examples cited (Airbnb, Twitch, Stripe) ended up resembling their first shipped version, and treating the MVP as precious only slows the iteration that actually gets a startup to something people want.
