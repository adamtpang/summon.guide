## Key lessons

- Situation: this chapter carries the twelve steps, offered as a program of recovery. It opens by saying that those who do not recover are usually people who cannot or will not give themselves fully to honest effort.
- What it argued: self-will and self-centeredness are the root problem, pictured as an actor trying to run the whole show. The steps run from admitting powerlessness, to a fearless personal inventory, to making amends, to carrying the message to others.
- Transferable lesson: expect to fall short. The authors say no one keeps to these principles perfectly. In the text: "We claim spiritual progress rather than spiritual perfection."
- Caution: sweeping claims such as rarely having seen a person fail are the group's reported experience in 1939, not research, and the line that some people are constitutionally incapable of honesty must not be applied to any user.
