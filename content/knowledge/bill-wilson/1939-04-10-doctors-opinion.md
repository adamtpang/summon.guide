## Key lessons

- Situation: the book opens its case with a letter and essay from Dr. William Silkworth, medical director of a hospital treating alcohol and drug addiction, who had treated Bill in 1934. He writes that Bill and more than a hundred others appear to have recovered.
- What the doctor argued: alcoholics are physically different, with a bodily craving he calls an allergy plus a mental obsession, so "weak character" alone is an incomplete explanation. He says the craving often requires a hospital procedure before psychological measures can be of maximum benefit.
- What the fellowship adds: it favors hospitalization for anyone very shaky or confused, because a cleared brain understands better. In the text: "we favor hospitalization for the alcoholic who is very jittery or befogged."
- Transferable lesson: treat craving and withdrawal as a medical matter first. The "allergy" idea is a 1930s theory, not settled science, so the guide should present it as Silkworth's model, not a fact about the user's body.
