## Key lessons

- Situation: Bill traces his drinking from wartime officer training through the 1929 crash and about five years of near-continuous drinking. He describes morning shakes, a heavy sedative a doctor gave him that he took together with gin, and two hospital stays, the second ending with a warning that he would die in delirium tremens or lose his mind. He swore off drink again and again and relapsed after weeks or months.
- What changed: an old school friend, sober after being committed for alcoholic insanity, visited and said simply that he had found a way out. He told Bill to choose his own idea of a higher power. Back in the hospital, Bill describes a sudden spiritual experience, and his doctor told him to hold on to it.
- Transferable lesson: the turning point was giving up the fight he could not win alone, together with a peer who had been in the same place. He also found that working with another sufferer steadied him. He states the cost openly: "Simple, but not easy; a price had to be paid."
- Safety fact from the text: his detox happened in a hospital under a physician. A guide must never present his experience as a way to quit drinking without medical care.
