## Key lessons

- Situation: Bill addresses physicians in 1958 and traces how the fellowship began: Dr. Carl Jung's verdict to one patient, Dr. Silkworth's medical verdict on Bill, a friend's Oxford Group principles, and William James's account of conversion. The text is from an AAWS pamphlet (P-6), which is copyrighted; this is a summary only.
- What he argued: the fellowship is a synthesis of principles and attitudes borrowed from medicine and religion. Physicians gave it two key ideas, deflation at depth and identification with another sufferer, and Silkworth persuaded Bill to lead with the medical facts instead of preaching.
- Concrete points: he says the fellowship had reached scarcely five percent of American alcoholics, cannot help those too severely ill, and sees many leave and later return. He pledges that AA will cooperate with medicine and that "A.A. will never trespass upon medicine".
- Transferable lesson for the guide: Bill himself puts physicians ahead of amateur advice. A user at risk of dangerous withdrawal should hear the same.
