## Key lessons

- Situation: in January 1961, months before Jung's death, Bill writes to thank him for what he told a patient decades earlier and to trace how that chain led to the fellowship. The letters were published in the AA Grapevine in January 1963, which holds the copyright; this is a summary only.
- What Bill told Jung: his physician had said he was hopeless, a friend arrived visibly changed, and in despair Bill cried out for help and had an experience he says ended his obsession. From it came a vision of a society of alcoholics, each passing the news to the next.
- What Jung answered: a craving for alcohol may be a low-level form of a spiritual thirst, and he names the remedy "spiritus contra spiritum". It is a private letter, not a clinical claim.
- Transferable lesson: Bill writes that he knew he must find an experience like his friend's, or die. Use with care: it is one man's account of a personal crisis, not proof of a method, and no substitute for medical treatment.
