## Key lessons

- Situation: the chapter describes the great obsession of proving one can drink like other people, and lists the rules the authors tried: beer only, no drinking alone, none in the morning, only at home, switching drinks, taking a trip, swearing off on a solemn oath, and more.
- What it argued: in the group's experience, once control of drinking is lost it does not come back, and each attempt ends worse. A man who stayed dry twenty-five years, then had a drink after retirement, was in a hospital within two months and dead within four years, so a long dry stretch did not restore safe drinking.
- Transferable lesson: an honest, repeated self-test is more useful than argument. The authors say recovery begins when one will "fully concede to our innermost selves that we were alcoholics".
- Caution: these are the authors' convictions from their own group in 1939. The guide should not diagnose anyone or claim that moderation is impossible for every drinker; that is a question for a clinician.
