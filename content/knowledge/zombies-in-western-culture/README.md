## Caveats

- The identity of the book was confirmed from the title page, copyright page, DOI and contents. The printed license is CC BY 4.0, so this text is openly licensed, although the file sits in the private library.
- The file has very long lines (one line holds most of the book), so the Read tool could not page through it. The text was read in full by printing consecutive byte ranges to the terminal. Nothing was copied to disk.
- The text is a PDF extraction: page headers, footnotes and figure captions are run into the prose, and locators in the notes are chapter and section numbers, not page numbers.
- Statistics (Pew, Gallup, General Social Survey, WHO, CDC and others) are reported as the book cites them, dated to roughly 2016 or earlier. They were not independently checked and may be out of date. The book itself notes the GSS social isolation finding was disputed.
- Chapter 5 discusses suicide rates at length. Chapter 4 describes severe harm to the Grassy Narrows First Nation, relying on Brian Walsh's 2006 account.
- Chapter 6 is described by the authors as an introductory overview of the historical genealogy, with the full argument deferred to later work. Its claims about Luther, Ockham and the Black Death are the authors' interpretation, stated briefly and without detailed sourcing.
- The book attributes Shaun of the Dead to Zack Snyder, which appears to be an error in the source. The notes do not repeat that attribution.
- The authors limit their claims to North American and Western culture, with mostly American examples.

No source text is stored here. Every lesson is a paraphrase.
