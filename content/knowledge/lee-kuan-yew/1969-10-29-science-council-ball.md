## Key lessons

- Note: the checklist's source map originally pointed to a 25 November 2004 Parliament speech by Minister Mentor Lee Kuan Yew at nas.gov.sg/archivesonline/data/pdfdoc/2004112501.htm. That page, and other legacy .htm-format NAS speech pages tried as alternatives (including a 1998 speech that had previously loaded and a 2007 dedication speech), now return an automated CloudFront bot-detection challenge with no content, so the page cannot be read. It is replaced here with a confirmed, directly downloadable PDF: the Prime Minister's speech at the Science Council Ball, 29 October 1969.
- Lee told Singapore's scientific community bluntly that aspiring to original research in pure science, while lacking the market size to commercialise any resulting discoveries, was "madness" for a country Singapore's size; the deep research and its costly application belonged to superpowers who could spread the cost across massive markets.
- He proposed a specific, deliberately modest role instead: Singapore as a secondary distribution and servicing centre, absorbing already-proven technology (repairing propeller aircraft before turboprops, building small general-purpose freighters before container ships) once larger economies had moved on to the next frontier and needed somewhere to place the older expertise.
- He gave a concrete workforce ratio to justify importing foreign professionals rather than waiting for local graduates: citing an American aircraft firm's staffing breakdown of roughly 5 percent engineers, 75 percent production workers, and 20 percent management, he argued that without the scarce 5 percent, the other 95 percent stays unemployed, which is why Singapore then held over 4,500 professional employment passes.
- The transferable lesson: rather than chase the most prestigious position in a value chain, honestly locate your actual comparative position (secondary adopter, not pioneer) and build a workforce and immigration strategy suited to that position, not the one you wish you were in.
