## Key lessons

- Welcoming eleven new MPs in 1977, Lee told them directly that the government's real burden was "finding a successor Government worthy of its responsibilities," and urged them to abandon inherited, inadequately adapted Westminster conventions (such as saying "Hear, hear") in favour of a style suited to Singapore. He treated institutional continuity, not any single election, as the standing test of the government's legitimacy.
- He spent a large part of the speech rebutting a specific opposition talking point: that education had suffered from constant ministerial turnover. Rather than dismiss the claim, he answered it with an exact record, naming all four education ministers across 16 years (1959 to 1975) with their tenures in years and months, plus the near-decade of unchanged permanent secretaries beneath them, and called a repeated but unrebutted debating point one that "becomes part of the accepted mythology."
- He also used hard demographic data to show how politics had shifted school enrollment: the Malay-medium stream's share of Primary 1 registrations rose through the Malaysia merger years (1963 to 1965) and then fell by roughly a quarter immediately after separation in 1966, treating the raw percentages as more persuasive than any political narrative about the merger's effects.
- The transferable lesson: when a critical claim about your record keeps recurring in public debate, dismantle it once with a complete, dated, checkable account rather than repeated general denial, because an unrebutted claim, in his words, hardens into accepted myth.
