## Key lessons

- Speaking to students in 1971, Lee explained why six years of planning for a merged Singapore-Malaya (1959 to 1965) had to be discarded overnight after the August 1965 separation, requiring education, security and economic policy to be rebuilt for a Singapore standing alone, with less entrepot trade and more manufacturing for export. The lesson is that a leader has to be willing to abandon a long-invested plan the moment its premise disappears, rather than defend it out of sunk cost.
- He set out an explicit order of priority: political stability first, because without it there is no investment or jobs; economic growth second; and only then a security policy worth defending, since there is nothing to defend without homes built and wealth created. He argued many new nations fail specifically because their first-generation independence leaders never learn administration or economics after winning freedom from colonial rule.
- He made a striking, numeric claim about how thin Singapore's governing capacity actually was: about 300 key people, drawn from different language schools and backgrounds purely on merit, carried the country's political, economic and security planning, and he said plainly that if all 300 died in one plane crash, Singapore would disintegrate.
- The transferable lesson: an honest leader states the real, narrow base an organisation depends on (not a comforting fiction of broad institutional strength), and treats deliberately enlarging that small core as an urgent task rather than an afterthought.
