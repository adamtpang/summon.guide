## Key lessons

- Note: the checklist's source map listed a May Day Rally speech with the year unconfirmed. The National Archives of Singapore record resolves to this specific speech, delivered at the Singapore Conference Hall on 1 May 1983; it is used here in place of the undated entry.
- Lee opened by contrasting the fiery, lorry-and-banner May Day rallies of the 1950s, when he was himself part of an anti-colonial, anti-capitalist labour movement, with the seated, air-conditioned 1983 rally, using his own political evolution to argue that militant unions which fight employers regardless of the wider economy end up wrecking the economy their members depend on.
- He defended a live succession dispute at the National Trades Union Congress head-on: after difficulties with outgoing Secretary-General Lim Chee Onn, some "old guard" unionists concluded that university-educated "technocrats" should stay as backroom advisers rather than lead. Lee rejected this by pointing to his own start in 1952 as a 28-year-old legal adviser to a postal workers' union, arguing that if he and colleagues like Goh Keng Swee had stayed backroom boys, "the history of Singapore would have been different."
- He was explicit that competent succession is never friction-free: he stated plainly that even careful screening of successors produces unexpected problems, and that the incoming leader Ong Teng Cheong shared the same objectives as his predecessor, with the only real difference being style and personality, not direction.
- The transferable lesson: when defending a leadership transition under criticism, separate what changed (the person, the style) from what didn't (the objective), and use your own career as concrete evidence rather than abstract reassurance when arguing that credentialed newcomers deserve real authority, not just advisory roles.
