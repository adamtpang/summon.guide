## Key lessons

- Situation: about two months after Joy's death Lewis told Shelburne, an American friend he never met in person, how he was taking his sorrow.
- What he said: the answer is "in nearly all the possible ways", because grief is a process, not a state, and it changes like a winding road with a new landscape at each bend.
- Two odd discoveries: the moments when you call most desperately to God are the ones when you seem to get no help, and the moments he felt nearest Joy were those when he mourned her least.
- Transferable lesson: do not expect one steady feeling or a schedule. Shifting moods are the normal shape of mourning, and closeness to the dead often arrives when you stop pushing for it.
- Source note: read through a biographer's blog page that quotes the letter, which places it "two months later" than 15 July; the exact day is not shown there.
