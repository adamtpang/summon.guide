## Key lessons

- Situation: after his wife Joy Davidman died on 13 July 1960, Lewis kept four notebooks of raw entries. They were published in 1961 under the pseudonym N.W. Clerk, and she appears only as "H".
- What he argued: he was angry at people who offered religious comfort too fast. He warned that talk about the consolation of religion makes a grieving person suspect the speaker does not understand. The PBS page reproduces this passage.
- What turned: in a later entry, at the very moment he felt he was mourning least, he found he remembered Joy best. The clearest memory came when he stopped straining for it.
- Transferable lesson: grief does not move in a straight line, and vivid memory often returns after numbness rather than through effort. In his words: "I remembered her best."
- Caution: the PBS page gives Joy's death as 14 July; Wikipedia and a Lewis biographer's page give 13 July. This guide uses 13 July.
