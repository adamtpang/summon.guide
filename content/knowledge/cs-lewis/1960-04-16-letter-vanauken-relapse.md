## Key lessons

- Situation: in spring 1960 Joy's cancer had returned after a remission that began in 1957. Lewis wrote to Vanauken, an American friend who had lost his own wife.
- What he did: he asked plainly for prayer and stated the medical facts without dressing them up, saying doctors held out no hope.
- He resisted the idea that the earlier recovery had been pointless. His point was that a miraculous reprieve is not a pardon: "Lazarus was raised from the dead to die again."
- Transferable lesson: name the truth of the diagnosis, ask for company in it, and value the extra years as real gifts even though they were not permanent.
- Source note: the letter is read through a biographer's blog page that quotes Lewis's Collected Letters. I checked the quoted wording on that page, not in the printed edition.
