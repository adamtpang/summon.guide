## Key lessons

- Situation: Lewis describes, decades later, the cancer that killed his mother in 1908 when he was a boy. It ran the usual course: a home operation, an apparent recovery, a return of the disease, rising pain.
- What he noticed: the boys lost her gradually to nurses, delirium and drugs before the death itself, and the house filled with strange smells and night-time comings and goings. He says this was in some ways harder on the children than the death.
- His father, overwhelmed, became unpredictable and unjust, and the two brothers leaned on each other as the only steady thing left. Lesson: loss often arrives with a collapse of the whole household routine, and that deserves naming as part of the grief.
- His own summary of the effect: "With my mother's death all settled happiness, all that was tranquil and reliable, disappeared from my life." He names what was lost (security), which is a first step in mourning well.
