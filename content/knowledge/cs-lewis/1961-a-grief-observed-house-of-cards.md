## Key lessons

- Situation: the page is a commentary on the third of the four sections of A Grief Observed. Lewis, a famous defender of Christian belief, finds his own faith giving way under the loss of Joy.
- What he argued: his collapse shows his faith was weaker than he thought. He asks whether he really cared about the sorrows of the world if his own sorrow overwhelmed him so much. He then wonders whether God had to knock the structure down so that he could see it.
- He also fears that any later "restoration of faith" may be another house of cards, and he would rather admit that than pretend.
- Transferable lesson: when loss shakes what you believed, honesty about the shaking is more useful than defending the old certainty. The line the commentary quotes: "If my house has collapsed at one blow it is because it was a house of cards."
